## 1. AGSR: Aperture-Guided Super-Resolution for Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.25934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25934)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 19, 2026. Accepted at IEEE MMSP 2026, where it won the Best Paper Award.

**What it is:** A method for rendering a trained 3DGS scene at higher resolution than it was trained at. At inference time it shrinks each Gaussian's projected covariance (a smaller "aperture"), which recovers fine detail that standard splatting blurs out. It beats lightweight image-space super-resolution baselines on Mip-NeRF 360.

**What's new:** Most 3DGS super-resolution work either retrains with high-resolution supervision or adds a 2D SR network after rendering. AGSR works directly in the splatting operator and needs no retraining.

**Why it matters to you:** It gives cheap zoom-in or high-DPI rendering for existing captures, which is useful for XR headsets with high pixels-per-degree displays. It also complements anti-aliasing work such as Mip-Splatting.

## 2. LiDAR-Assisted 3DGS for Surface Deterioration Mapping in Built Heritage

**Source:** [Nature portfolio (npj Heritage Science)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-03090-6)
**Date:** Published Nov 18, 2026

**What it is:** A pipeline that combines LiDAR registration with image-based 3DGS to map moisture and stress damage in historic semi-subterranean cellars in Utrecht. It also checks how well damage annotations carry over between the point-cloud and splat representations.

**What's new:** This is an applied validation of 3DGS as a measurement and documentation tool, not just a visualisation tool. Broad moisture patterns carried over between representations reliably, but thin cracks did not.

**Why it matters to you:** It is a concrete data point on where 3DGS geometry is accurate enough and where it fails. Thin, high-frequency structures remain a weak spot, which is relevant to any metric or inspection use of splats.

## 3. Kosmo-GFM — A Production-Ready Geometry Foundation Model

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.16840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16840) · [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/KosmoResearch/Kosmo-GFM) · [Radiance Fields write-up](https://radiancefields.com/papers/kosmo-gfm-a-production-ready-geometry-foundation-model) · [AlphaSignal coverage](https://alphasignal.ai/news/kosmo-gfm-rebuilds-3d-scene-capture-from-sparse-photos-26x-faster)
**Authors:** Sheng Tang et al. (Kosmo Research)
**Date:** arXiv v1 posted Nov 12, 2026. Coverage was still appearing this week.

**What it is:** A system with three parts:
- **GFM:** a 1.66B-parameter feed-forward model that predicts cameras, depth, cross-view correspondences and dynamic 3D points from images or video. It has camera-conditioned, 4D-motion and matching variants.
- **PoseEngine:** combines the GFM's predictions with geometric optimisation to get scene-level poses for downstream 3D and 4D Gaussian Splatting.
- **KosmoWorld:** a data engine that renders extra training supervision from reconstructed real scenes.

**What's new:** It reports the lowest AbsRel depth error and the highest pose AUC@5° on the SpatialBench Sparse and Medium subsets among the methods it compares. Coverage cites a "26× faster" sparse-photo capture pipeline. It is designed for production use rather than as a benchmark-only model.

**Why it matters to you:** It could replace COLMAP as the pose and initialisation front-end for 3DGS and 4DGS, especially with sparse or casual captures. **Caveats:** the public release leaves out the full PoseEngine and splatting pipelines. Original code is Apache-2.0, but VGGT-derived terms also apply. Sources disagree on how much code is actually released.

## 4. SplatAnything — Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-Forward 3DGS

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08264) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08264) · [3D&4D News](https://izutsuya.io/3d4d-news/en/2026/11/14/01.html) · [AI Weekly alert](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/splatanything-releases-inference-code-with-gaussian-spacing-fix) · Code: `zju3dv/SplatAnything`
**Date:** Paper ~Nov 11, 2026. Inference code was reported released shortly after.

**What it is:** A feed-forward 3DGS model meant to work on any kind of scene (object, indoor, outdoor, aerial). Its key component, **GSNorm**, sets the coordinate scale from the scene's *median Gaussian spacing* instead of from a radial-distance statistic.

**What's new:** The authors show that radial-distance normalization leaves up to 4.5× variation in median Gaussian spacing across scene categories. That inconsistency hurts generalisation, and GSNorm removes it while keeping local spacing variation intact.

**Why it matters to you:** The normalisation idea is simple and easy to transfer to any feed-forward splatting or pointmap model you train on mixed datasets. Reported CLI options include `--resolution` (a multiple of 14, default 756×756), `--sky_seg` and `--opacity_thresh` for PLY export. Check them against the official README before use.

## 5. MatrixSplat — Matrix-Parallel, Feature-Scalable 3DGS Rendering on GPUs

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.15153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15153)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 11, 2026. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

**What it is:** A rasterizer that recasts tile-based splatting as matrix operations that run on Tensor Cores. It adds a Gaussian packing strategy and scales well as per-Gaussian feature dimensions grow.

**What's new:** Existing 3DGS rasterizers run on scalar CUDA cores. MatrixSplat moves alpha blending onto matrix hardware, which matters most for high-dimensional features such as semantic, language or neural-texture splats.

**Why it matters to you:** It is directly useful for feature-field splatting (for example, splats carrying CLIP or DINO features) and for real-time XR rendering budgets.

## 6. OQ-Stereo — Single-Shot 3D Capture with Optical Quasicrystal Patterns

**Source:** [EurekAlert press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1151637)
**Team:** Dayong Qiao (Northwestern Polytechnical University), Yijie Shen (NTU Singapore), with collaborators at the University of Stuttgart
**Date:** Nov 12, 2026

**What it is:** A structured-light method that merges 12 stripe orientations into one quasicrystal projection pattern, then decodes depth with an AI model (OQ-Stereo). It reports sub-0.1 mm surface-fitting precision and was demonstrated on dynamic scenes such as a rotating fan.

**What's new:** It gets multi-pattern accuracy from a single frame, which makes high-accuracy capture of moving objects possible.

**Why it matters to you:** It is a possible source of high-precision ground truth or depth priors for dynamic-scene reconstruction and robotics datasets. Only the press release is available so far, with no paper DOI or full benchmarks.

## 2. DPGS-SLAM: Diffusion-Prior, Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM

**Source:** arXiv 2611.12038 (via [awesomepapers.io listing](https://awesomepapers.io/computer-vision/papers/2611.12038))
**Date:** Listed November 2026 (this week)

**What it is:** A monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM system built for dynamic scenes. It uses a diffusion model to fill in the background hidden behind moving objects, and it tracks "provenance", meaning which parts of the map were observed and which were hallucinated by the prior.

**What's new:** Most GS-SLAM systems assume the scene is static and simply mask out dynamic objects, which leaves holes in the map. Using a generative prior to fill those holes, while keeping track of which parts are generated, is a new and principled combination.

**Why it matters to you:** This is directly relevant if you capture splats from casual handheld or head-worn video with people moving through the scene. The provenance idea also helps evaluation, because it separates measured geometry from imagined geometry, which matters for metrology and digital-twin use cases.

## 3. P-MGS: *Perception Over Geometry: Rethinking Gaussian Allocation in Mesh-based Splatting*

**Source:** arXiv [2611.07979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07979) · [Radiance Fields summary](https://radiancefields.com/papers/perception-over-geometry-rethinking-gaussian-allocation-in-mesh-based-splatting)
**Date:** Submitted November 5, 2026

**What it is:** Mesh-bound splatting methods such as GaMeS assign a fixed number of Gaussians to each mesh face. P-MGS instead allocates Gaussians according to perceived appearance complexity, estimated from multi-view observations. When the mesh is edited, it updates Gaussian centers and covariances using local affine deformation transfer.

**What's new:** It decouples the Gaussian budget from mesh tessellation. Detailed textures get more Gaussians and flat regions get fewer, no matter how the mesh is triangulated. The authors report better novel-view quality with fewer Gaussians, and fewer floaters and misalignments after edits. These numbers have not been independently verified.

**Why it matters to you:** It connects 3D modeling (editable meshes, rigging, DCC tools) with photoreal splat rendering, a hybrid that suits XR assets that must be both editable and lightweight. The allocation idea could also carry over to compressing splat assets for streaming.

## 6. Gaussian Splat Tooling Wave (Blender, Unreal/UEFN, Unity, MipMap, PowerPoint)

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/) news roundup: [Unity GS 2.0.0](https://radiancefields.com/unity-gaussian-splatting-2-0-0-loads-ply-and-spz-directly) · [MipMap Desktop 3.3](https://radiancefields.com/mipmap-makes-gaussian-splats-walkable-in-desktop-3.3) · [Splat Slides](https://radiancefields.com/splat-slides-brings-gaussian-splat-scenes-to-powerpoint) · [SculptForge 0.9.0](https://radiancefields.com/sculptforge-trains-gaussian-splats-from-phone-video)
**Date:** November 2–10, 2026

**What it is:** Several mainstream tools added splat support in quick succession:
- **Blender 5.3** imports splats natively from PLY, SPZ, USD and glTF, and renders them in Workbench, EEVEE and Cycles.
- **Epic UEFN 43.00** imports PLY and SPZ splats and renders them on every platform UEFN supports, including mobile.
- **Unity Gaussian Splatting 2.0.0** loads PLY and SPZ directly but removes its in-plugin editing tools, pointing users to SuperSplat instead.
- **MipMap Desktop 3.3** adds a walk mode, scale calibration, measurement and direct upload to SuperSplat.
- **SculptForge 0.9.0** trains splats locally from phone video, using Brush and COLMAP.
- **Splat Slides** is a free PowerPoint add-in for interactive splat scenes.

**What's new:** Splats are becoming a native asset type in major DCC tools and game engines, not just a research format. SPZ and the glTF/USD paths are converging as interchange formats.

**Why it matters to you:** It's now much easier to move research outputs into production and demos. It's worth targeting SPZ and glTF/USD export in your pipelines so your results load directly in Blender, Unreal and Unity.

**Date:** Recent (late October to November 2026; exact date not confirmed)

**What it is:** A single feed-forward model that predicts depth, 3D objects, occupancy and Gaussian splats from multi-camera driving video. It comes with a benchmark of 1,253 clips.

**What's new:** It merges what are normally separate perception stacks (detection, occupancy) and reconstruction (splats) into one 4D foundation model, continuing the feed-forward trend set by VGGT and D4RT.

**Why it matters to you:** It suggests where feed-forward 4D reconstruction is heading: one backbone with many heads. The benchmark could be useful if you evaluate dynamic-scene reconstruction.

## 8. 3D Gaussian Splatting as a Wireless Environment Representation

**Source:** arXiv [2611.01122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01122)
**Date:** Submitted October 31, 2026

**What it is:** A position and methods paper arguing that 3DGS can model radio-frequency propagation environments (channel and coverage modeling), not just visual appearance.

**What's new:** It applies the splatting representation, with its differentiable rendering and explicit primitives, outside optical rendering.

**Why it matters to you:** It's a cross-domain use case for digital twins. One captured splat scene could serve both visual XR and RF or network planning. The paper's reasoning about the representation may also help with multi-sensor splatting (LiDAR, radar, thermal).

## 1. SplatAnything — Gaussian Spacing Normalization
**Source:** [arXiv 2611.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08264) · Nov 5, 2026
Argues that feed-forward 3DGS methods normalize Gaussian attributes with a global radial statistic, which makes spacing between neighbouring Gaussians inconsistent across scene scales. Proposes Gaussian Spacing Normalization (GSNorm).
**Why it matters:** Scale generalization is a key weakness of feed-forward splat predictors; relevant if you run them on mixed indoor/outdoor captures.

## 2. CrossPressor — Scalable Cross-View Fusion
**Source:** [arXiv 2611.06258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06258) · Nov 4, 2026
Targets scaling cross-view fusion in feed-forward 3DGS to long multi-view sequences; reports up to 26.12× faster fusion.
**Why it matters:** Cost of attending across many views is the bottleneck for feed-forward reconstruction from video.

## 3. OctreeSplat — Hierarchical Octree-Guided Feed-Forward 3DGS
**Source:** [arXiv 2611.02658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02658) · Nov 2, 2026
Allocates Gaussian primitives by local scene complexity using an octree; evaluated on Replica, TUM, Tanks and Temples, DTU.
**Why it matters:** Adaptive primitive budgets give more compact scenes, useful for XR memory/bandwidth limits.

## 4. Sony XYN — Cloud Gaussian-Splat Capture for Individuals
**Source:** [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329998/20261103/sony-xyn-brings-real-time-gaussian-splatting-us-virtual-production-stages.htm) · Nov 3, 2026
Sony's cloud spatial capture tool reportedly opens to individual creators and small studios in the US/Canada (~$20/month).
**Why it matters:** Another sign of splatting moving from research into commodity production tooling.

## 5. Blender — Native 3D Gaussian Splat Support

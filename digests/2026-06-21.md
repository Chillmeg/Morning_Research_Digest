## 1. Chaos Vantage 3.3.0 — Gaussian Splat Relighting in Real-Time Ray Tracer

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/chaos-vantage-3.3.0-brings-gaussian-splat-relighting-to-its-real-time-ray-tracer) · [Chaos Docs 3.3.0](https://documentation.chaos.com/space/LAV/908558346)  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** Chaos Vantage 3.3.0 adds Gaussian splat relighting to its real-time GPU ray tracer. Imported 3DGS captures — which previously baked illumination at scan time — now respond dynamically to scene lights placed or adjusted inside Vantage. A single Vantage session can mix both procedurally modelled geometry and scanned Gaussian splat environments, all responding to the same light rig.

**What's new:** Scanned splat environments have always clashed with custom lighting rigs in professional visualisation workflows because lighting was baked in at capture time. Vantage 3.3.0 mirrors what Chaos already delivered in V-Ray 7 Update 3 for batch rendering — completing the pipeline from offline to interactive real-time review with the same relighting capability.

**Why it matters:** For anyone using 3DGS captures in architectural visualisation, VFX, or XR scene composition, this breaks the biggest practical barrier to mixing splat-captured environments with dynamic virtual lights. It opens a direct path from photogrammetric capture to real-time XR preview at a quality level previously requiring a full re-render.

## 2. ComfyUI v0.23.0 — Native GAUSSIAN Node Type + TripoSplat

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/comfyui-adds-native-3d-gaussian-splat-generation-with-triposplat) · [ComfyUI blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/bringing-native-support-for-3d-gaussian) · [Comfy docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/3d/triposplat)  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** ComfyUI v0.23.0 ships a native `GAUSSIAN` data type and a set of first-party manipulation nodes for working with 3D Gaussian splats directly in the graph editor. It also integrates TripoSplat, an open-source image-to-3DGS model from VAST-AI (Tripo), as a day-0 bundled template — a single image produces a renderable Gaussian splat asset from within the workflow graph.

**What's new:** Previously, 3DGS workflows in ComfyUI required custom community extensions. A first-party `GAUSSIAN` type makes splats a composable primitive in the node graph — meaning splats can flow between nodes the same way images and latents do. TripoSplat is a separately notable model: it produces a full 3DGS from a single 2D image, bringing image-to-3D into a generalist diffusion pipeline environment.

**Why it matters:** ComfyUI is one of the most widely used ML workflow tools. Native GAUSSIAN support means the ecosystem of community nodes (ControlNet, IP-Adapter, custom samplers) can now be connected to 3DGS generation and editing tasks, dramatically lowering the barrier to prototyping 3D-aware generative pipelines for XR and scene synthesis.

## 3. Cesium June 2026 Release — Standalone GS / Point Cloud Pipelines

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/) · [Cesium](https://cesium.com/)  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** Cesium ion's June 2026 release removes the previous requirement that reconstruction jobs include mesh generation. Users can now run point cloud or Gaussian splat pipelines without generating a mesh — allowing direct upload and tiling of raw GS captures for streaming as 3D Tiles with hierarchical LOD.

**What's new:** Previously, Cesium ion's reconstruction pipeline always produced a mesh as the primary deliverable, with Gaussian splats as an add-on. Decoupling mesh generation allows GS-only workflows that are faster, cheaper (skip mesh processing), and produce more faithful representations of real-world environments. This builds on Cesium's April 2026 addition of hierarchical LOD for GS in 3D Tiles, CesiumJS, and Cesium for Unreal.

**Why it matters:** For geospatial XR applications — navigation, digital twins, infrastructure inspection — this makes GS the first-class output format rather than a secondary overlay. Combined with the LOD streaming infrastructure, photorealistic scanned environments can now stream from city scale to sub-centimetre detail without a mesh intermediary.

## 4. Niantic SPZ 4 — Next-Generation GS Compression Format

**Source:** [Niantic Spatial blog](https://www.nianticspatial.com/blog/spz4) · [Auganix](https://www.auganix.org/xr-news-niantic-spz-4-gaussian-splats/) · [GitHub (MIT)](https://github.com/nianticlabs/spz)  
**Date:** May 2026

**What it is:** Niantic Spatial released SPZ 4, a major update to its open-source compressed 3DGS file format. SPZ (described as "JPG for Gaussian splats") achieves ~10× compression vs. PLY via fixed-point quantisation; v4 brings 3–5× faster compression, 1.5–2× faster loading, **removes the 10-million-point limit** (now supporting tens of millions of Gaussians), replaces the single GZip stream with six parallel ZSTD streams for multi-core parallelism, and adds a vendor extension system for custom metadata.

**What's new:** The previous 10 M point ceiling was a blocking constraint for large-scale or high-fidelity outdoor captures (typical city-scale scenes run 50–100 M+ Gaussians). Parallel ZSTD decompression makes loading feasible on mobile-class hardware. The Khronos Group has also adopted SPZ as `KHR_gaussian_splatting_compression_spz` — a companion compression extension to the main KHR_gaussian_splatting extension.

**Why it matters:** SPZ 4 is now the de facto open interchange format for GS assets across XR, robotics, and web workflows. If you distribute or stream GS scenes — particularly for XR or autonomous-system map data — upgrading to SPZ 4 removes both the scale ceiling and the compression bottleneck that limited large-scene use cases.

## 5. KHR_gaussian_splatting — glTF Extension Approaching Ratification

**Source:** [Khronos press release](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/gltf-gaussian-splatting-press-release) · [GitHub spec](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_gaussian_splatting/README.md) · [Architosh](https://architosh.com/2026/02/the-khronos-group-announces-gltf-gaussian-splatting-standard/)  
**Date:** RC announced February 3, 2026; ratification targeted Q2 2026 (window closes June 30)

**What it is:** The Khronos Group's KHR_gaussian_splatting extension for glTF 2.0 defines how GS attributes (position, orientation, scale, colour via spherical harmonics, opacity) are stored within glTF mesh primitives. Supported by Google, NVIDIA, Apple, Adobe, Cesium, and Bentley Systems. Companion extension `KHR_gaussian_splatting_compression_spz` handles Niantic SPZ compression.

**Status:** The release candidate has been in community review since February. Ratification is expected before Q2 2026 closes (June 30). The `TODO: Add known implementations before final ratification` note in the spec suggests it is very close but not yet formally finalised.

**Why it matters:** Once ratified, any glTF-capable viewer, game engine, or DCC tool will have a standardised path for loading and rendering GS assets — the same interoperability leap that made glTF the "JPEG of 3D" for traditional geometry. This is the single most important infrastructure event for GS toolchain standardisation in 2026.

## 6. Learning Representations from 3D Gaussian Splats (arXiv 2605.29549)

**Source:** [arXiv 2605.29549](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29549)  
**Authors:** Not specified in available metadata  
**Date:** May 28, 2026

**What it is:** This paper investigates using 3DGS representations — typically treated as a rendering primitive — as input to downstream ML tasks. Rather than generating images from splats for perception, the work proposes learning directly over the Gaussian attribute space (position, covariance, colour, opacity) as the representation for tasks such as scene understanding, retrieval, and editing.

**What's new:** The predominant use of 3DGS has been as a rendering output; this paper repositions the Gaussian attribute space itself as a learnable input domain. This is analogous to early work that used NeRF latents as representations — but the structured, explicit nature of GS attributes (vs. NeRF's implicit MLP weights) makes them more amenable to standard ML architectures.

**Why it matters:** This opens a pathway where 3DGS captures of environments are directly consumed by perception models — relevant for scene search, robotic navigation, and XR environment understanding — without any RGB render step as an intermediary. A key open question for spatial computing pipelines.

## 7. Survey: Surface Reconstruction Based on 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [PMC 12453780](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12453780/)  
**Date:** Recent (2026 PMC publication)

**What it is:** A comprehensive survey categorising 3DGS-based surface reconstruction algorithms into three phases: (1) scene representation (choice of Gaussian primitives and initialisation), (2) Gaussian optimisation (regularisation, densification strategies, depth priors), and (3) surface structure extraction (mesh extraction, SDF inference, alpha compositing to surfaces). The survey benchmarks current methods on standard datasets.

**What's new:** This is the first systematic survey specifically on *surface* (as opposed to novel-view synthesis) quality from 3DGS. It consolidates a rapidly fragmented literature — 2DGS, PGSR, GOF, SuGaR, NeuSG — into a common taxonomy, making it easier to understand trade-offs.

**Why it matters:** If your use case involves geometry-accurate reconstruction (not just photorealistic rendering) — e.g., for measurement, physical simulation, or mesh export — this survey provides the best current map of which method to use and why. Surface quality has historically been 3DGS's weakest point vs. multi-view stereo; this survey documents how much that gap has closed.

## 8. Survey: Human Reconstruction Using 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [PMC 12678354](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12678354/)  
**Date:** Recent (2026 PMC publication)

**What it is:** A focused survey on reconstructing high-fidelity and animatable 3D human avatars from visual data using 3DGS. Covers monocular and multi-view input setups, avatar parameterisation (SMPL-based, free-form deformation), appearance modelling, and downstream tasks including novel-view synthesis of dynamic humans and animation transfer.

**What's new:** Human avatar reconstruction is one of the most active application domains for 3DGS, with dozens of methods (GaussianAvatar, Gaussian Body, HumanNeRF-GS, etc.) published in 2024–2026. This survey is the first to consolidate the avatar-specific literature and provide a structured comparison of approaches.

**Why it matters:** Animatable avatars are a core deliverable for XR telepresence and social VR. This survey provides a direct navigation tool for selecting the right reconstruction pipeline for real-time avatar capture — which is particularly relevant given visionOS 27's new avatar and spatial interaction APIs.

## 9. Upcoming: SIGGRAPH 2026 — Gaussian Point Splatting (425 M Gaussians, Real-Time)

**Source:** [Project page](https://momentsingraphics.de/Siggraph2026.html) · [GitHub](https://github.com/JorisAR/gaussian-point-splatting)  
**Authors:** Joris Rijsdijk, Christoph Peters, Michael Weinmann, Ricardo Marroquim  
**Date:** Presenting July 20, 2026 at SIGGRAPH 2026

**Reminder / preview:** This paper proposes a stochastic rendering approach that eliminates tile-based rasterisation and sorted Gaussian lists, replacing them with 64-bit atomic accumulation of pixel-sized opaque point samples. It renders **425 M Gaussians interactively** on an NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti SUPER — roughly 4–8× the scale of previous real-time approaches.

**Status update:** Code and paper are now available; the formal SIGGRAPH presentation is in 29 days. Worth following author demos and community benchmarks as they appear in the run-up to the conference.

**Why it matters:** City-scale and large outdoor scans regularly exceed 50–100 M Gaussians. This removes the practical ceiling on real-time 3DGS rendering — the last major performance obstacle between GS captures and true large-scale XR deployment.

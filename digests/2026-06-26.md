## 1. SAD-GS: Faster 3D Gaussian Splatting Convergence via Structure-Aware Densification

**Source:** [arXiv:2604.28016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28016) · [Project page](https://vcai.mpi-inf.mpg.de/projects/SAD-GS/)  
**Authors:** Linjie Lyu, Ayush Tewari, Jianchun Chen, Thomas Leimkühler, Christian Theobalt — Max Planck Institute for Informatics, Cambridge University, Saarbrücken Research Center for Visual Computing  
**Date:** arXiv April 2026; accepted SIGGRAPH 2026 (proceedings published July 3, 2026)

**What it is:** SAD-GS analyses multiscale image structure in input training views and uses that signal to guide anisotropic densification during 3DGS optimisation. The core idea is that the decision to subdivide a Gaussian should be driven by an explicit comparison between its projected screen-space extent and the local texture frequency it must represent — not merely by screen-space positional gradients, which conflate geometric misplacement with frequency aliasing.

**What's new:** Standard 3DGS adaptive density control uses gradient magnitude as the sole densification signal, which either over-densifies in low-frequency regions or under-densifies in high-frequency texture areas. SAD-GS replaces this with a structure-aware criterion derived from multiscale image analysis, producing better-placed Gaussians in fewer iterations and with less memory than the vanilla approach.

**Why it matters to you:** SAD-GS directly reduces training time and VRAM usage for any 3DGS-based pipeline — reconstruction, dataset generation, scene capture for XR — without changing the representation or rendering path. Since it targets the densification loop (not architecture-specific tricks), it should compose cleanly with acceleration methods like FastGS and Faster-GS already in the digest.

## 2. FiCA: Feed-Forward Instant Gaussian Codec Avatars from a Single Portrait Image

**Source:** [arXiv listing (cs.GR, Jun 2026)](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.GR/recent) · [ACM SIGGRAPH 2026](https://s2026.siggraph.org/program/technical-papers/)  
**Authors:** Kim Youwang, Zhengyu Yang, Liuhao Ge, Yu Rong, Timur Bagautdinov, Su Zhaoen, Nir Sopher, Jovan Popović, Teng Deng, Tae-Hyun Oh, Chen Cao — and colleagues  
**Date:** SIGGRAPH 2026

**What it is:** FiCA creates a fully animatable Gaussian Codec Avatar from a *single* portrait photo in a feed-forward pass — no test-time optimisation, no video sequence, no multi-view capture rig required. The model predicts a full set of 3D Gaussians parameterised to match the Codec Avatar representation, enabling real-time re-expression and relighting from the reconstructed result.

**What's new:** Previous Gaussian Codec Avatar pipelines (RGCA, RFGCA) required either multi-camera capture in a studio or at least a short monocular video clip for per-identity optimisation. FiCA removes both constraints. The amortised, feed-forward approach generalises across identities seen during training, making in-the-wild avatar creation from a phone selfie a plausible near-term target.

**Why it matters to you:** This is directly on the critical path for scalable XR telepresence — the bottleneck is always avatar capture, and a single-portrait approach removes that bottleneck entirely. The Gaussian Codec Avatar parameterisation also means downstream re-use in any GS-compatible pipeline (rendering, relighting, animation) without format conversion.

## 3. AA-Splat: Anti-Aliased Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv:2603.29394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29394) · [Project page](https://kaist-viclab.github.io/aasplat-site/)  
**Authors:** Taewoo Suh, Sungpyo Kim, Jongmin Park, Munchurl Kim — KAIST (Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology)  
**Date:** arXiv March 31, 2026

**What it is:** AA-Splat is a feed-forward 3DGS (FF-3DGS) model designed for robust rendering at arbitrary sampling rates. Current FF-3DGS methods embed incorrect screen-space dilation filters, causing severe aliasing and ringing artefacts when rendered at resolutions or distances outside the training distribution. AA-Splat introduces an **opacity-balanced band-limiting (OBBL)** design: a 3D band-limiting post-filter that integrates multi-view maximal frequency bounds into the reconstruction pipeline, combined with opacity balancing to integrate all pixel-aligned Gaussians without tile-sorting artefacts.

**What's new:** Feed-forward 3DGS (e.g. pixelSplat, MVSplat) has been fast enough for real-time sparse-view reconstruction but limited to a narrow resolution/distance operating range. AA-Splat's OBBL formulation derives frequency bounds analytically from the multi-view geometry, giving the filter theoretically correct behaviour across the full scale range — not just the training distribution.

**Why it matters to you:** XR applications need to render the same 3DGS scene at vastly different resolutions (full-res passthrough vs. thumbnail-size preview vs. WebXR stream) and from widely varying distances. AA-Splat is the first FF-3DGS approach that handles this cleanly, which is a prerequisite for deploying FF-3DGS in any production XR or web-streaming context.

## 4. Gemma 4 12B — Encoder-Free Multimodal Open Model

**Source:** [Google DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/gemma-4/) · [Google Developers blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemma-4-12b/) · [Model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4)  
**Authors / Org:** Google DeepMind  
**Date:** June 3, 2026 (Gemma 4 12B); the broader Gemma 4 family launched April 2, 2026

**What it is:** Gemma 4 is a family of four open models (E2B, E4B, 26B MoE, 31B Dense; the 12B variant is the latest addition) built from the same research stack as Gemini 3. The headline architectural change: **no multimodal encoders**. Vision and audio inputs flow directly into the LLM backbone via a shared token representation, eliminating the ViT/audio-encoder bottleneck. Released under Apache 2.0; runs on 16 GB RAM for the 12B version.

**What's new:** Encoder-free multimodal architectures (pioneered by Meta's Chameleon and now scaled here) process vision as tokens with the same attention mechanism as text, enabling arbitrary interleaving of modalities without a separate perception stack. Gemma 4 12B is the largest open model to ship this architecture, and the first to include audio natively at this scale.

**Why it matters to you:** Dense visual feature extraction from Gaussians, depth maps, and rendered novel views can now be done without a separate frozen encoder — the LLM itself becomes the perceptual backbone. For tasks like 3D-LLM scene understanding, VQA over reconstructed scenes, or instruction-tuned 3D editing, Gemma 4 12B offers a practical open-weight baseline that runs on a single consumer GPU.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Feed-forward everything** — FiCA (avatars), AA-Splat (novel-view synthesis), and the earlier D4RT/Flex4DHuman confirm the field has standardised on amortised feed-forward inference replacing per-scene optimisation. The remaining unsolved sub-problem is generalising to large outdoor or unbounded scenes.

2. **Anti-aliasing is the next GS quality frontier** — AA-Splat targeting the FF-3DGS aliasing failure mode, combined with the earlier Anti-Aliased 2D Gaussian Splatting (arXiv:2506.11252), signals that the community is now addressing the rendering-quality constraints that blocked real production deployment.

3. **Encoder-free multimodal backbones** — Gemma 4's encoder-free architecture is the second major open model (after Chameleon/Janus) to prove this works at scale. Expect encoder-free vision to become the default backbone for 3D-LLM and scene-understanding systems within the next two model generations.

4. **XR hardware commoditisation** — Google's three-tier rollout with fashion brands (Gentle Monster, Warby Parker, Gucci) alongside tech partners (XREAL, Samsung) indicates the industry has decided the path to mass adoption is form-factor and fashion, not raw spec. Rendering pipelines need to plan for asymmetric, fashion-driven hardware rather than a clean headset/glasses binary.

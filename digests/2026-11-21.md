## 1. Genie 4 Opens to Developers in the Gemini API

**Source:** [TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/google-opens-genie-4-world-model-to-developers-in-gemini-api/) · [Genie 4 model card](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/genie-4) · [DeepMind blog (Oct 27 announcement)](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/) · [ai-tldr summary](https://ai-tldr.dev/releases/google-genie-4-gemini-api/)
**Date:** API access on Nov 19, 2026, one day after the consumer Project Genie launch on Nov 18

**What it is:** Google DeepMind's Genie 4 is an interactive world model. It generates playable, steerable environments from a text prompt, a reference image, or both. It is now available as `models/genie-4` through the Gemini Interactions API and Google AI Studio. Secondary sources report a 720p/24 fps stream with native audio, paid-tier keys, and roughly $0.10/s with a 120 s session cap. These figures haven't been checked against Google's docs.

**What's new:** Genie 3 and earlier Genie 4 access were limited to research previews and trusted testers. This is the first time developers can call the model programmatically and build on it. The model card says it is built on Gemini Omni Flash, with Gemini models handling real-time steering.

**Why it matters to you:** World models are a new competitor to explicit 3D (splats or meshes) for generating interactive XR content. They are also a source of synthetic video for training reconstruction models. Two experiments worth trying: (a) run feed-forward reconstruction (VGGT/D4RT-style) or 3DGS on Genie-generated walkthroughs to test how consistent the geometry is, and (b) compare latency and controllability against an explicit splat scene for XR use.

## 2. SGAR — Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction via Sparse Geometry-Guided Attention

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.26493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26493)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 19, 2026

**What it is:** SGAR is a feed-forward multi-view reconstruction model. It replaces dense all-to-all cross-view attention with sparse attention guided by geometry, so each token attends only to the regions in other views that are geometrically relevant.

**What's new:** VGGT-style models use dense global attention, whose cost grows quadratically with the number of views. That limits them to tens of views. SGAR targets that bottleneck directly, aiming to let feed-forward reconstruction scale to larger image collections without per-scene optimisation.

**Why it matters to you:** Scaling feed-forward reconstruction to hundreds of frames is the missing piece for replacing COLMAP plus optimisation when capturing casual video at room or building scale. SGAR could be a drop-in initialiser for 3DGS on long captures.

## 3. Infer3D and SceneAnything — New Reconstruction Papers (Nov 20 listing)

**Source:** [arXiv cs.CV new listings](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.CV/new) (Infer3D: arXiv 2611.25061; SceneAnything: arXiv 2611.25340)
**Date:** Appeared in the Friday, Nov 20, 2026 cs.CV batch according to search snippets. I couldn't open the listing to confirm.

**What it is:**
- **Infer3D** frames robust 3D reconstruction as *inverse generative modeling*: it uses a generative prior to explain the observations rather than regressing geometry directly.
- **SceneAnything** is an agent system. It coordinates pretrained vision and graphics tools to reconstruct large, mesh-based scenes from a single video. The authors say they *will* release the harness, agent instructions, libraries and scenes.

**What's new:** Both follow a growing trend this autumn (see also GATOR and Unravel, Oct 2026) of treating reconstruction as reasoning or generation rather than pure regression. That trend tends to produce editable, structured outputs such as meshes and programs instead of point clouds.

**Why it matters to you:** For 3D modeling and XR pipelines that need clean, editable meshes rather than splats, agent-driven reconstruction is becoming a practical option. SceneAnything's harness is worth watching for release.

## 4. CryoGaussian — Gaussian Splatting for Heterogeneous Cryo-EM Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26128)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 19, 2026

**What it is:** CryoGaussian applies an explicit Gaussian representation to cryo-electron microscopy. It learns latents per particle image and per Gaussian, so it can model structural variation across particles.

**What's new:** It brings 3DGS's explicit, differentiable primitives to tomographic reconstruction in a non-optical setting, and it handles heterogeneity (deformation and variability) with per-Gaussian latent codes.

**Why it matters to you:** The per-Gaussian latent conditioning is the same pattern used for dynamic and deformable splats (4DGS, avatars). Ideas may carry over in either direction. It's also more evidence that Gaussians are becoming a general inverse-problem primitive, not just a novel-view-synthesis method.

## 5. LightHarness — Evidence-Guided Programmatic Scene Reconstruction with Lighting Reasoning

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.24565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24565)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 18, 2026

**What it is:** A vision-language-model agent that reconstructs a scene from images as *code*, meaning a procedural scene description. It adds explicit lighting reasoning, so the output matches the materials and illumination of the input, not just its geometry. Project and code pages are linked.

**What's new:** Previous image-to-code scene reconstruction mostly ignored lighting, so results looked flat or wrong. Here the agent gathers evidence (shadows, highlights) and reasons about light sources explicitly.

**Why it matters to you:** Relightable, editable scene programs are what XR authoring and digital-twin workflows need, and baked-light splats can't easily provide them. It pairs well with splat-to-mesh or splat-to-asset pipelines.

## 6. Open4DGS — Genlock-Free 4D Gaussian Splatting from Consumer Cameras

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/asu-open-sources-open4dgs-for-genlock-free-4d-gaussian-splatting)
**Authors:** Arvin Tehrani, Rory Mitchell, Ryat Yezbick, Nonny de la Peña (ASU Narrative & Emerging Media)
**Date:** Nov 18, 2026 (open-sourced)

**What it is:** An open-source pipeline that turns footage from unsynchronised consumer multi-camera rigs (GoPros, Sony action cameras, mixed setups) into 4D Gaussian splat sequences. A software sub-frame synchronisation solver replaces hardware genlock. It reportedly uses RoMa v2 for matching across cameras and MEMFOF for matching across time, and gives fast-motion frames extra keyframes and training passes.

**What's new:** Most 4DGS work assumes strictly synchronised studio rigs. Open4DGS removes that hardware requirement in an open, end-to-end pipeline. The details above come from one secondary article, so verify against the repository.

**Why it matters to you:** It makes volumetric (4D) capture for XR affordable. If you've wanted dynamic splat datasets without a capture stage, this is the most practical route released so far.

## 7. Gaussian Splats Arrive in Mainstream 3D and XR Tools

**Source:** [Blender Developers Blog](https://code.blender.org/2026/10/splatting-the-gaussians-into-blender/) · [Phoronix — Blender 5.3 released](https://phoronix.com/news/Blender-5.3-Released) · [Radiance Fields — 3ds Max 2027.3](https://radiancefields.com/3ds-max-2027-3-adds-splat-viewport-display-and-4dgs) · [VP Land](https://vp-land.com/stories/netflix-explores-gaussian-splats-as-a-streaming-format-while-blender-and-unreal-engine-add)
**Date:** Blender 5.3 released Nov 10, 2026. 3ds Max 2027.3 and the ArcGIS XR Viewer beta came out in November 2026.

**What it is:**
- **Blender 5.3** treats splats as a native point-cloud type. It imports PLY, SPZ and glTF (Radiance Fields says USD too), processes them with Geometry Nodes, and renders them in EEVEE and Cycles. Export isn't supported yet, and relighting is rudimentary.
- **3ds Max 2027.3** adds a splat viewport shader, 4DGS playback from numbered sequences, and PLY export.
- **ArcGIS XR Viewer** (Nov 2026 beta) adds initial splat layer support.
- Also noted: Splat Repair 0.1.0 (an open-source splat editor with a repair brush), and reports that Netflix is exploring splats as a streaming format. The Netflix work is an internship project and is exploratory.

**Why it matters to you:** Splats now have first-class support in the main DCC and GIS tools, so research outputs can go straight into artist and XR workflows. Export from Blender is still missing, and that gap is worth tracking.

## Trends to Watch

1. **Reconstruction as reasoning:** Agent and generative-prior methods (SceneAnything, Infer3D, LightHarness) are producing structured, editable 3D output rather than raw geometry.
2. **Feed-forward scaling:** Sparse attention (SGAR) is pushing VGGT-style models past their limit on view count.
3. **Splats as a standard format:** Native support in Blender, 3ds Max and ArcGIS, plus streaming experiments, show splats moving from a research topic to an asset format.
4. **World models compete with explicit 3D:** Genie 4 API access makes generated interactive worlds a practical alternative or complement to reconstructed scenes for XR.

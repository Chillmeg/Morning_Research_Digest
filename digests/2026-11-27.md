## 1. VersionGS: Compact and Motion-Robust Dynamic Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.33374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33374)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 24, 2026

**What it is:** A dynamic-scene 3DGS method that stores per-frame Gaussian states as "versions" in a shared dictionary. Gaussians reuse states across frames instead of carrying a dense deformation field or a separate copy for every timestep.

**What's new:** Most dynamic 3DGS methods (deformation MLPs, 4DGS) lose quality under fast motion, or they blow up storage when they model motion explicitly. Versioning targets both problems: memory stays compact, and the model stays robust to large, fast motion.

**Relevance to you:** This is useful for compact streamable 4D content in XR, such as volumetric video playback on headsets. Compare it with your current dynamic-GS baseline on high-motion sequences.

## 2. Vizrt Viz Engine 5.6.0: Native Gaussian Splat Rendering

**Source:** Search-result coverage of the Vizrt release (one source, no primary press release found)
**Date:** Nov 25, 2026

**What it is:** Vizrt's real-time broadcast graphics engine adds native Gaussian splat rendering. The release covers SOG and PLY import, octree level of detail (LOD), adjustable spherical-harmonics degree, and relighting from scene lights.

**What's new:** Gaussian splats now work as first-class assets in a mainstream broadcast and virtual-production engine, and they can be relit by that engine's lights. Relighting baked-radiance splats is still an open research problem, so watch how Vizrt approximates it.

**Relevance to you:** More evidence that splats are becoming a standard asset format (after Blender 5.3 added initial splat support in October). The SOG + octree LOD pairing also points to the formats that production tools are settling on.

**Source:** Search-result coverage of the 0.3 release (one source)

**What it is:** Version 0.3 open-sources the project's Unreal Engine plugin for 3D and 4D Gaussian splatting under Apache-2.0. It adds UE 5.9 builds, Chaos fracture support and splat shadows.

**What's new:** A permissively licensed, engine-integrated 4D splat renderer that interacts with UE physics (Chaos) and casts shadows. Most earlier UE splat plugins were closed-source or supported static scenes only.

**Relevance to you:** A ready-made path from research output to UE-based XR prototypes, without writing your own renderer. Apache-2.0 also allows you to modify it and ship it.

## 5. UPP-Gauss: Physics-Informed Gaussian Splatting for Ultrasound

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.33920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33920)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 25, 2026

**What it is:** Applies Gaussian splatting to novel-view synthesis of B-mode ultrasound. It models acoustic propagation physics instead of the optical alpha-compositing model used in standard 3DGS.

**What's new:** It swaps the image-formation model inside the splatting framework, keeping the primitives and the differentiable rasterization but replacing the rendering physics.

**Relevance to you:** Even outside medical imaging, it shows how to adapt 3DGS to non-optical sensors such as sonar, radar or depth. It is also an example of physics-informed rendering on top of Gaussian primitives.

**Date:** Submitted Nov 23, 2026. A SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 acceptance was reported but not confirmed.

## 8. GSDet: Gaussian Splatting for Oriented Object Detection

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.30602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30602) · [IJCAI 2025 proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/101) · [Code](https://github.com/wokaikaixinxin/GSDet)
**Date:** arXiv posting Nov 23, 2026. The paper was originally published at IJCAI 2025.

**What it is:** Reframes oriented object detection as splatting. Objects are 3D Gaussians in a feature space built from the image, and projecting them to 2D Gaussians gives oriented boxes. It reports small AP50 gains on DIOR-R, DOTA-v1.0 and DOTA-v1.5.

**What's new:** Not new research, but a newly posted arXiv preprint of last year's paper. Its interest is conceptual: it uses Gaussian splatting as a detection head, not as a scene representation.

**Relevance to you:** Low priority. Worth knowing if you are looking at Gaussian primitives as a shared representation for detection and reconstruction.

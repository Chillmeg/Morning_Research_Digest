## 1. GraphiXS — A Graphical Model for 4D Gaussian Splatting Under Uncertainty

**Source:** [arXiv 2601.19843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19843) · [Project page](https://graphixs.yilmazdoga.net/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/realcrane/Graphical-X-Splatting) · [SIGGRAPH 2026 proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3799902.3811085)
**Authors:** Doğa Yılmaz, Jialin Zhu, Deshan Gong, He Wang
**Date:** Presented at SIGGRAPH 2026 (July 19–23, 2026)

**What it is:** GraphiXS reformulates 4D Gaussian Splatting as a probabilistic graphical model that explicitly represents data uncertainty — missing frames, view sparsity, and camera desynchronization — rather than assuming clean, fully-observed multi-view video. It generalizes across primitive types (Gaussians, Student's-t distributions) to model confidence alongside geometry.

**What's new:** Prior 4D-GS methods silently degrade or hallucinate when input capture is incomplete or asynchronous, a routine real-world condition for multi-camera rigs. GraphiXS is the first framework to make that uncertainty a first-class, learnable part of the splatting pipeline instead of a downstream artifact.

**Why it matters to you:** Real capture rigs (marker-less mocap, multi-phone rigs, drop-frame webcams) rarely deliver perfectly synchronized data. A splatting model that reasons about missing/asynchronous observations natively is directly useful for dynamic scene capture pipelines feeding XR avatars or telepresence.

## 2. XenVision — AI-Driven Musculoskeletal Health Platform

**Source:** [ACM SIGGRAPH Blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/2026/07/xenco-medical-unveils-xenvision-an-ai-driven-musculoskeletal-health-platform-at-siggraph-2026.html/) · [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260720014438/en/Xenco-Medical-Unveils-XenVision-an-AI-Driven-Musculoskeletal-Health-Breakthrough-at-SIGGRAPH-2026-the-Worlds-Premier-Computer-Graphics-Conference) · [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-xenco-medical-is-using-ai-to-see-human-movement-differently)
**Date:** Unveiled July 19, 2026 at SIGGRAPH

**What it is:** XenVision combines computer vision-based pose estimation, biomechanical modeling, and clinical AI to turn ordinary video capture into objective musculoskeletal assessments (posture, joint kinematics, range of motion, gait symmetry) in under two minutes, trained on 700,000+ real-world assessment datasets.

**What's new:** It's a production deployment of markerless, video-only pose/motion capture for clinical-grade biomechanical inference — historically the domain of expensive marker-based mocap labs — validated at scale and recognized with a TIME100 Impact Award.

**Why it matters to you:** A concrete example of computer-vision pose estimation and temporal skeletal tracking moving from research demos into regulated, real-world deployment. The pipeline (video → skeletal representation → clinically interpretable metrics) is a useful reference architecture for any markerless human-motion capture work feeding avatars or biomechanics.

## 3. CASA-SDF — Curriculum-Aware Spatial Adaptation for Neural Implicit Surfaces

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.13492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13492)
**Date:** arXiv, mid-July 2026

**What it is:** CASA-SDF targets a core weakness of MLP-based SDF surface reconstruction in indoor scenes: large texture-less planar regions (walls, floors) need heavy regularization to avoid high-frequency noise, while thin structures (chair legs, railings) need the opposite — sharp, adaptive representational capacity. It unifies both via curriculum-driven supervision and curvature-guided density adaptation.

**What's new:** Most indoor-scene SDF methods pick one regularization regime and accept degraded results on the other geometry class. CASA-SDF's curriculum approach adapts capacity per-region instead of globally.

**Why it matters to you:** Indoor scene reconstruction quality directly gates XR room-scale scanning, furniture-aware AR placement, and robotics mapping — this addresses a failure mode (blurry walls vs. missing thin geometry) that shows up constantly in real-world captures, independent of whether the downstream representation is SDF or Gaussian-based.

## 4. OASIS — Occlusion-Aware Single-Image Hand Avatar Reconstruction via 3DGS

**Source:** Accepted to ACM Multimedia 2026 (via search aggregation)
**Authors:** Zhisheng Han, Shiyao Wu, Jiayan Qiu, Yakun Ju, Lu Liu, Le Zhang, Pengfei Feng, Huiyu Zhou, Zheheng Jiang

**What it is:** OASIS reconstructs an animatable, textured 3D Gaussian hand avatar from a single RGB image, explicitly modeling self-occlusion (fingers occluding fingers/palm) rather than assuming full visibility.

**What's new:** Most hand-avatar 3DGS work assumes multi-view or occlusion-free input; OASIS pushes single-image hand reconstruction toward practical accuracy under realistic occlusion, which is the default case for casual capture.

**Why it matters to you:** Hand tracking and hand avatars are a persistent weak point in consumer XR (controller-free interaction, gesture-based UI). A single-image, occlusion-robust pipeline lowers the capture bar for personalized hand avatars in XR/telepresence apps.

## 5. Niantic Scaniverse 4 & Open-Sourced SPZ Compression Format

**Source:** [Niantic Labs](https://nianticlabs.com/news/scaniverse4) · [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/platforms/scaniverse)
**Date:** Ongoing 2026 rollout

**What it is:** Scaniverse (Niantic Spatial) continues as a free, on-device mobile Gaussian-splat capture app, now also distributing splats via SPZ (Splat Zip) — an MIT-licensed, fixed-point-quantized compression format achieving ~90% size reduction over raw PLY splats — alongside new paid Plus/Pro tiers for higher-fidelity capture.

**What's new:** SPZ being open-sourced under MIT and adopted natively by Scaniverse pushes toward a de facto interchange standard for distributing splats at mobile-friendly file sizes, complementing the Khronos glTF `KHR_gaussian_splatting` extension that standardized the scene-graph side in 2025.

**Why it matters to you:** Standardized, heavily compressed splat formats are the missing piece for shipping Gaussian-splat content at consumer scale (web, mobile AR, app stores with size limits). Worth tracking SPZ + glTF adoption if you're building any splat-delivery pipeline.

## 7. NVIDIA Cosmos 3 — Open Omnimodel for Physical AI & World Simulation

**Date:** Launched May 31, 2026 at GTC Taipei (still propagating through the robotics/synthetic-data ecosystem)

**What it is:** Cosmos 3 is an open-weight mixture-of-transformers foundation model pairing a reasoning transformer with an expert generation transformer, natively handling text, image, video, ambient sound, and action trajectories for physical-world simulation and robot policy training.

**Why it matters to you:** World models capable of generating physically-consistent video/action trajectories are a natural upstream source of synthetic training data and simulation environments for 3D reconstruction and robotics-adjacent XR — an open, well-adopted base model here shapes what synthetic-data tooling looks like for the next year.

## 10. VidSplat — Video-Diffusion-Guided Gaussian Splatting for Sparse Views

**Source:** [arXiv 2605.11424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11424) · [SIGGRAPH 2026 proceedings](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3799902.3811138)
**Date:** SIGGRAPH 2026

**What it is:** A training-free generative reconstruction framework that uses video diffusion priors to synthesize novel views compensating for missing input coverage, recovering complete 3D Gaussian scenes from sparse captures.

**What's new:** Rather than training a bespoke sparse-view 3DGS model, VidSplat plugs an off-the-shelf video diffusion prior directly into the reconstruction loop at inference time — no additional training required to handle undersampled viewpoints.

**Why it matters to you:** Sparse-view capture is the realistic default for casual/consumer splat capture (a handful of phone photos, not a structured rig). A training-free approach that leverages existing video-diffusion models is easy to adopt without retraining a scene-specific reconstruction network.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Uncertainty-aware and diffusion-assisted splatting are converging on the same problem** — GraphiXS (explicit uncertainty modeling) and VidSplat (diffusion-prior view completion) both attack incomplete/imperfect real-world capture from different angles. Expect these to merge into single pipelines that are robust to missing frames *and* missing viewpoints.

2. **Splat delivery standardization is maturing** — SPZ's MIT open-sourcing plus glTF's `KHR_gaussian_splatting` extension are closing the "how do we ship a splat to a phone" gap that has held back consumer splat apps; watch for broader tooling/engine adoption over the next few months.

3. **Vision-based motion capture is exiting the lab** — XenVision's clinical deployment and OASIS's occlusion-robust single-image hand avatars both point to markerless pose/avatar pipelines becoming production-ready rather than research-only, which lowers the barrier for personalized XR avatars.

4. **Open physical-AI world models as a shared substrate** — NVIDIA's Cosmos Coalition mirrors the "shared foundation model" pattern now appearing in physical-AI/robotics; if adopted broadly, Cosmos 3 becomes a common synthetic-data source for training 3D reconstruction and XR perception models, similar to how DINOv3 became a shared vision backbone.

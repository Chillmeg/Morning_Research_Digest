## 1. Meta Muse Spark 1.1 — 1M-Token Agentic Multimodal Model

**Source:** Meta AI Blog · reported via ThursdAI AI-releases roundup
**Date:** July 13, 2026

**What it is:** Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 is a 1-million-token-context agentic model that Meta claims rivals GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 on agentic evaluations, topping MCP Atlas, JobBench, Humanity's Last Exam, and Finance Agent V2. It ships alongside Meta's first-ever paid developer API in public preview and adds computer-use control across desktop, browser, and mobile plus parallel subagent delegation.

**What's new:** This is Meta's first commercial developer API and its first model positioned explicitly as an agent/computer-use system rather than a chat assistant, putting Meta into direct competition with the agentic tooling from OpenAI and Anthropic.

**Why it matters to you:** Computer-use and long-context agentic models are increasingly used to drive multi-step 3D/CV pipelines (asset triage, dataset curation, automated capture QA). A capable, cheap agentic backbone from a second major lab lowers the cost of building orchestration layers around vision and reconstruction tools.

## 2. V-Ray 7.4 for Maya Reaches Gaussian Splatting Feature Parity

**Source:** Radiance Fields
**Date:** July 12, 2026

**What it is:** Chaos's V-Ray 7.4 for Maya update brings Gaussian splatting to feature parity with its other DCC integrations, adding in-render relighting controls for splats and the ability to clip unwanted regions when rendering on GPU.

**What's new:** Splats can now be relit and selectively cropped directly inside a production renderer rather than requiring a pre-bake or external tool pass — closing a long-standing gap between splats and traditional CG assets in VFX pipelines.

**Why it matters to you:** In-renderer relighting and region clipping remove two of the most common blockers to mixing captured Gaussian splats with synthetic CG elements in the same shot, which is directly relevant to any hybrid capture + CG 3D content pipeline.

## 3. RhinoGaussianSplatting — Free Rhino 8 Plugin

**Source:** Radiance Fields
**Date:** July 12, 2026

**What it is:** A free, MIT-licensed plugin that imports, edits, meshes, and bakes 3D Gaussian splatting datasets as native, editable objects inside Rhino 8.

**What's new:** Most CAD/modeling tools still treat splats as an inert point cloud or require a separate viewer; this plugin makes splats first-class, editable Rhino geometry, including a path to converted meshes.

**Why it matters to you:** Rhino is widely used in industrial design, architecture, and engineering — native splat editing there extends Gaussian splatting's reach from pure visualization into CAD-adjacent modeling workflows, a use case relevant to scan-to-CAD 3D reconstruction pipelines.

## 4. PlayCanvas splat-transform v3.0.0 — Chunked Streaming for Out-of-Core Splats

**Source:** Radiance Fields
**Date:** July 10, 2026

**What it is:** A rewrite of PlayCanvas's open-source 3DGS command-line tool that streams splats in chunks, converting and building LODs for scenes too large to fit in memory.

**What's new:** Previous versions of splat-transform required loading an entire scene into memory; the chunked architecture removes that ceiling, enabling conversion and LOD generation for city-scale or otherwise oversized splat datasets on commodity hardware.

**Why it matters to you:** Combined with efforts like Gaussian Point Splatting (425M-Gaussian real-time rendering, covered in the June 12 digest), this closes the tooling gap on the ingestion/preprocessing side — large-scale splat capture is now practical end-to-end, not just at render time.

## 5. aiMotive Brings Relightable Gaussian Splatting to aiSim 6

**Source:** Radiance Fields
**Date:** July 8, 2026

**What it is:** aiMotive's autonomous-driving simulator aiSim 6 now uses PBR (physically-based rendering) splatting to dynamically relight 3D Gaussian splatting digital twins of real road scenes, without retraining the splat for each lighting condition.

**What's new:** Standard 3DGS bakes lighting into each Gaussian's appearance, making relighting expensive or impossible without recapture. PBR-splatting decomposes appearance into relightable material properties, letting one captured scene be evaluated under arbitrary lighting/weather.

**Why it matters to you:** Relightable splatting is a prerequisite for using captured (not synthetic) scenes in simulation-heavy domains like autonomous driving, robotics, and digital twins — directly relevant if your reconstruction work needs to support downstream simulation rather than just static viewing.

## 8. ReCal3R — Reliability-Calibrated Learning Rates for Streaming 3D Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.05356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05356)
**Authors:** Xinze Li et al. — Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University, Jilin University, Nanyang Technological University
**Date:** July 6, 2026

**What it is:** A method for recurrent/streaming 3D reconstruction models that estimates the reliability of each token in a maintained scene state and uses it to calibrate the learning rate applied on each streaming update — preventing reliable historical scene information from being overwritten by noisy new observations.

**What's new:** Prior streaming reconstruction methods (Point3R, STream3R, Anchor3R) apply a fixed or heuristic update rate per step; ReCal3R derives a per-token calibrated rate from state reliability, token alignment, reconstruction residual, and recent update pressure jointly.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're building live/incremental 3D capture (SLAM-adjacent, streaming multi-view capture for XR), state corruption from noisy updates is a real failure mode — this is a directly applicable fix for online reconstruction pipelines rather than offline batch methods.

## 9. Restore3D — Shape and Texture Restoration of Broken 3D Objects

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.00522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00522)
**Date:** July 1, 2026

**What it is:** A framework that jointly restores both the shape and texture of damaged/incomplete 3D objects from multi-view images, using an automated data-generation pipeline that synthesizes paired incomplete/complete samples from large-scale 3D datasets to train the restoration model.

**What's new:** Most prior restoration work handles shape completion and texture inpainting as separate stages; Restore3D unifies them and, notably, builds its own supervised training data automatically rather than relying on scarce real damaged-object scans.

**Why it matters to you:** Directly applicable to heritage/archival digitization, damaged real-world scan cleanup, and any capture pipeline where partial occlusion or physical damage leaves gaps that need plausible geometry and texture filled in — a common practical problem in real (non-lab) 3D capture.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Splatting tooling is consolidating around production DCC/CAD parity, not new core algorithms.** This week's items (V-Ray relighting, RhinoGaussianSplatting, PlayCanvas chunked streaming) are all about making splats behave like first-class assets inside existing pipelines — the frontier has shifted from "can we render/train splats" to "can artists and engineers use them like any other 3D asset."

2. **Relightable, physically-based splatting is becoming a real requirement.** aiMotive's PBR-splatting for simulation echoes a broader push (also visible in NVIDIA's earlier vkSplatting multi-light/ray-traced-shadow work) toward splats that support arbitrary lighting rather than baked appearance — necessary for simulation, digital twins, and any reuse of captured scenes outside their original lighting conditions.

3. **Streaming/online 3D reconstruction is an active sub-field of its own.** ReCal3R joins a fast-growing cluster of 2026 papers (Point3R, STream3R, Anchor3R, LASER) tackling the specific failure modes of incremental, memory-based reconstruction — relevant to any live capture or SLAM-adjacent XR application.

4. **Geo-anchored splats + streamed map tiles is the emerging XR mapping architecture.** Arrival.Space's splat/3D-tiles compositing is a smaller-scale preview of the same pattern Apple is building into Maps Flyover — expect more platforms to adopt "splat overlay on a streamed photorealistic basemap" as the default architecture for location-based XR.

## 1. PlayCanvas Opens SuperSplat's Public REST Publishing API

**Source:** [PlayCanvas Forum announcement](https://forum.playcanvas.com/t/supersplat-publishing-api-is-live-with-three-partner-integrations/42526) · [PlayCanvas Blog](https://blog.playcanvas.com/)  
**Date:** August 18, 2026

**What it is:** PlayCanvas has shipped a public REST API for its SuperSplat hosting/viewer platform, letting third-party scanning apps and Gaussian splat trainers upload splats directly to `superspl.at` hosting instead of exporting a multi-gigabyte PLY and re-uploading it by hand. Authentication uses a PlayCanvas access token as a bearer token, and uploads are resumable and multipart. Three partner integrations launched alongside the API.

**What's new:** This is one of the first standardized, programmatic publishing pipelines for Gaussian splats — previously every capture tool (scanning apps, trainers, cameras) had its own bespoke export/hosting story. A shared REST contract means capture and viewing can now be decoupled cleanly.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're building or evaluating a 3DGS capture-to-viewer pipeline, this removes a real integration tax — you can push splats straight from a training job or mobile capture app to a hosted, shareable viewer without hand-rolled infrastructure. It's also a signal that splat hosting is consolidating around a few standard formats/APIs, worth tracking if you're deciding what to build vs. buy.

## 2. GaussianSplatting.jl v2.0.0 — Multi-GPU, MCMC Densification, Sky Dome

**Source:** [Anton Smirnov's blog](https://pxl-th.github.io/blog/better-gs-julia/) · [Radiance Fields coverage](https://radiancefields.com/gaussian-splatting-in-julia) · [GitHub](https://github.com/JuliaNeuralGraphics/GaussianSplatting.jl) · [Hacker News discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49232039)  
**Date:** August 13, 2026

**What it is:** A major update to the pure-Julia 3DGS implementation, adding cross-vendor GPU backend support (AMD, NVIDIA, Apple Metal) via KernelAbstractions.jl, an MCMC-based densification strategy (treating Gaussian counts as a Markov Chain Monte Carlo process for more precise control and less initialization sensitivity), depth/normal-based geometry supervision, a Sky Dome pass to disentangle backgrounds from foreground geometry, and a responsive multithreaded UI.

**What's new:** Unlike the CUDA-only reference implementation, this is a genuinely vendor-agnostic 3DGS trainer/viewer — a rarity in a field still dominated by NVIDIA-locked CUDA kernels. The MCMC densification approach also reduces one of the most finicky parts of standard 3DGS training (manual densification/pruning schedules).

**Why it matters to you:** If your hardware isn't NVIDIA (Apple Silicon, AMD), this is one of the few production-quality paths to train and view Gaussian splats without CUDA. The MCMC densification and geometry supervision are also useful reference techniques to borrow even if you stay in the PyTorch/CUDA ecosystem.

## 3. Insta360 X6 "Spatial Capture" — 3D Gaussian Splatting Built Into a Consumer 360 Camera

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/insta360-brings-gaussian-splatting-to-the-x6-with-spatial-capture) · [Spatial Insiders](https://spatialinsiders.com/stories/insta360-x6-august-12-launch) · [Insta360 × Splatica partnership](https://www.insta360.com/blog/enterprise/insta360-splatting-gaussian-splatting-partnership.html)  
**Date:** Full reveal August 12, 2026; on sale now ($699.99)

**What it is:** The Insta360 X6 ships with a "Spatial Capture" mode that lets users record a space in 360° and turn it into a navigable 3D Gaussian splat scene directly in the Insta360 mobile app — no external SfM/COLMAP pipeline or desktop trainer required. Scenes can then be revisited on Quest 3, Apple Vision Pro, and other XR headsets. Insta360 is also partnering with Splatica for native 360-to-3DGS conversion support.

**What's new:** This is one of the first mass-market consumer cameras with an end-to-end capture-to-splat pipeline baked into the stock app, rather than requiring users to shoot footage and run it through a separate desktop trainer (Postshot, LichtFeld Studio, etc.).

**Why it matters to you:** It's a strong signal of where the capture side of the 3DGS pipeline is heading — on-device/on-app reconstruction for casual users. For research and product purposes, it lowers the bar for acquiring real-world 3DGS training/eval data at scale, and it's a template worth studying if you're designing consumer-facing capture UX for XR.

## 5. World Labs' Marble — Spatial Intelligence in the Spotlight at Ai4 2026

**Source:** [World Labs](https://www.worldlabs.ai/) · [World Labs Research Blog](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog) · [Technology Magazine coverage](https://technologymagazine.com/news/marble-worlds-how-world-labs-advances-spatial-ai)  
**Date:** Fei-Fei Li keynoted alongside Geoffrey Hinton and Andrew Ng at Ai4 2026, Las Vegas, August 4, 2026

**What it is:** Marble, World Labs' generative spatial-intelligence product, creates spatially cohesive, persistent 3D worlds from text, images, video, or rough 3D layouts, with AI-native editing to expand/combine environments. The company (founded by Fei-Fei Li) has raised $1.23B total, with strategic backers including NVIDIA, AMD, and Autodesk, and Marble scenes are usable in Vision Pro and Quest 3.

**What's new:** Marble continues to push "world models" from single-shot generation toward persistent, editable 3D environments — a step beyond one-off novel-view-synthesis outputs, aiming at reusable spatial assets for games, VFX, and VR.

**Why it matters to you:** Marble is a useful benchmark for what generative 3D scene creation looks like when optimized for downstream editability (vs. pure visual fidelity). If you're evaluating text/image-to-3D tools for XR content pipelines, its persistence and multi-headset export support are the differentiators to watch relative to competitors like Odyssey's Starchild-1 or Luma's Ray series.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Capture is moving on-device.** Insta360's Spatial Capture and NavVis's native splat mode both point toward 3DGS reconstruction happening at the point of capture (phone/camera app or scanning device) rather than as a separate offline desktop step.

2. **Vendor lock-in to CUDA is eroding.** GaussianSplatting.jl's multi-GPU backend and LichtFeld Studio's Vulkan migration are independent signals that the open-source 3DGS tooling ecosystem is actively de-risking itself from NVIDIA-only pipelines.

3. **Splat publishing/hosting is standardizing.** PlayCanvas's REST API, OTOY's SPZ v4 support, and NavVis's native viewer integration together suggest the industry is converging on a small set of interchange formats and hosting patterns rather than one-off exports.

4. **Generative "world models" are the next 3D battleground.** World Labs (Marble), Odyssey (Starchild-1), Google DeepMind (Genie 3), and Luma are all racing on persistent, editable, explorable 3D/4D scene generation — worth tracking as a category distinct from single-scene 3DGS reconstruction.

5. **ECCV 2026 paper season has begun.** With papers going public ahead of the September 8–12 Malmö conference, expect a wave of new Gaussian splatting, 4D reconstruction, and vision-foundation-model preprints on arXiv over the next few weeks — a good time to set up a recurring scan for standout results.

## 1. CADSplat — Sparse-View 3D Gaussian Splatting Aided by CAD Models for Robust, Photorealistic Digital-Twin Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.18473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18473) (Overdulve, Jorissen, Michiels)
**Date:** Submitted mid-September 2026

**What it is:** CADSplat reconstructs photorealistic, geometrically accurate digital twins of physical objects from very sparse (fewer than 15), wide-baseline posed images by regularizing 3DGS with an explicit CAD shape prior. It retrieves a CAD model whose shape resembles the captured object, registers cameras to that model by matching segmented object silhouettes against silhouettes rendered from a CAD library, anchors Gaussians to the retrieved mesh, and jointly optimizes 3DGS parameters, camera-to-object registration, and a non-rigid deformation field to close the gap between the CAD reference and the real object.

**What's new:** Most sparse-view 3DGS work adds generic priors (depth, diffusion, multi-view consistency); CADSplat instead injects object-specific shape knowledge from an existing CAD library exactly where views are scarcest and self-occlusion is worst. Registering every camera into the object's own canonical frame is also a byproduct that other pipelines have to solve separately.

**Why it's useful to you:** Beyond novel-view synthesis, the CAD-anchored registration directly enables markerless AR registration, per-image object pose estimation, physics-ready simulation assets, and transfer of part labels from the design file to the reconstruction — a genuinely multi-purpose output for anyone reconstructing manufactured objects (industrial digital twins, product scanning) rather than general scenes.

## 2. Blender 5.3 Alpha Adds Native 3D Gaussian Splat Import and Rendering

**Source:** [Radiance Fields — Blender 5.3 coverage](https://radiancefields.com/blender-5.3-will-bring-native-3d-gaussian-splat-import-and-rendering); tracked via PR#163102
**Date:** Landed in Blender 5.3 Alpha, September 17, 2026 (alpha through Sep 30, final release targeted Nov 10, 2026)

**What it is:** Blender is getting first-class Gaussian Splat support: native import/rendering of PLY and SPZ splat files, with support across Workbench, EEVEE, and Cycles, plus geometry-nodes integration. This follows a design task opened by the Blender Foundation in June 2026.

**What's new:** Splats have until now lived almost entirely in bespoke viewers and engine plugins (Unreal, Unity, custom web viewers); native support in a mainstream open-source DCC tool means splats become a first-class asset type alongside meshes and point clouds, editable with the same node/material/animation systems artists already use. Known rough edges in the alpha: performance isn't yet production-ready, the import pipeline assumes sRGB color space, and low-opacity/high-radiance regions can render incorrectly since Cycles/EEVEE operate in linear space.

**Why it's useful to you:** If your workflow involves any hand-finishing, compositing, or hybrid mesh+splat work, this collapses a format-conversion step that previously required a dedicated splat tool. Worth tracking through the alpha since the linear/sRGB handling issue will likely affect anything you bring in before it's fixed.

## 3. Crate 2.0.2 — Gaussian Splat Previews Land in a Nuke Asset Browser

**Source:** [GitHub — CrateTools/Crate](https://github.com/CrateTools/Crate)
**Date:** Released September 18, 2026

**What it is:** Crate is an Apache-2.0 asset-management and batch-processing toolkit/browser for Nuke. Version 2.0.2 adds in-browser 3D previews of Gaussian splats and bundles PlayCanvas's `splat-transform` CLI for format conversion, alongside its existing thumbnail/preview generation for geo, image, video, and sequence assets (via F3D, OpenImageIO, FFmpeg, ImageMagick, MediaInfo). Windows-only for now.

**What's new:** It's a concrete instance of the same trend as the Blender item above — splats moving from standalone viewers into the asset-management layer of existing production pipelines (compositing/VFX, in this case), with automatic format conversion built into the browsing step rather than a separate manual pass.

**Why it's useful to you:** If splats are entering a Nuke-based pipeline (compositing captured scenes, digital-twin plates, etc.), this removes a manual conversion/preview step; the bundled `splat-transform` also means one less external dependency to manage per artist workstation.

## 4. Arcturus Vision Ships a $149 Steam Frame Passthrough Camera and Plans a Free "Gaussian XR" Viewer

**Source:** [Radiance Fields — Arcturus Vision coverage](https://radiancefields.com/arcturus-vision-opens-orders-for-a-149-steam-frame-passthrough-camera)
**Date:** Orders opened mid-September 2026 (ahead of Valve's Steam Frame launch, covered in yesterday's digest)

**What it is:** A $149 5K HDR passthrough camera accessory for Valve's newly launched Steam Frame headset, paired with an official script that turns Steam Frame camera recordings into a 3DGS scene (`.sog`) via COLMAP and LichtFeld Studio on a Linux workstation. Arcturus also has "Gaussian XR," a free splat viewer, planned for release before the end of September 2026.

**What's new:** This is one of the first concrete third-party capture-to-splat pipelines built specifically around a just-shipped consumer VR headset's camera hardware, rather than a phone or dedicated photogrammetry rig — turning a mainstream headset into a casual Gaussian-splat capture device.

**Why it's useful to you:** If you're evaluating consumer-grade capture-to-splat workflows for XR content creation, this is a concrete, shipping (not conceptual) hardware+software pipeline to test — directly relevant given Steam Frame's launch volume covered in the prior digest.

## 5. CorbeauSplat v2.0.0-beta.4 Fixes a Real 4DGS/COLMAP Correctness Bug

**Source:** [GitHub — freddewitt/CorbeauSplat releases](https://github.com/freddewitt/CorbeauSplat/releases/tag/v2.0.0-beta.4)
**Date:** Released September 19, 2026

**What it is:** A pre-release fix for a substantive bug in CorbeauSplat's 4D Gaussian Splatting pipeline: `FourDGSEngine.run_colmap()` was passing every extracted frame from every camera into COLMAP's feature extraction/mapping stage, instead of one synchronized snapshot per camera. Since COLMAP's structure-from-motion assumes a static scene, matching frames captured at different instants of a moving scene produces invalid correspondences and corrupts the reconstruction. The fix adds a `_build_static_reference_set()` step that stages exactly one reference frame per camera before running COLMAP — matching the approach used by the reference `hustvl/4DGaussians` pipeline.

**What's new:** Not a new capability, but a worth-noting correctness fix that illustrates a subtle, easy-to-miss failure mode in dynamic/4DGS pipelines: naively pointing COLMAP at a full multi-camera, multi-timestep frame dump silently violates its static-scene assumption rather than erroring out.

**Why it's useful to you:** If you maintain or build on any dynamic-scene Gaussian splatting pipeline that calls COLMAP for camera calibration, this is a good sanity check to run against your own frame-staging logic — the failure mode (corrupted reconstruction, not a crash) is exactly the kind of bug that can go unnoticed until output quality is inexplicably poor.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Gaussian splatting is being absorbed into mainstream production tooling, not just research code.** Blender 5.3 (native DCC support) and Crate 2.0.2 (Nuke asset browser) both moved splats from bespoke viewers into general-purpose pipelines within the same week — a sign the format is stabilizing enough for tool vendors to commit engineering effort to first-class support.

2. **Consumer XR hardware is becoming a capture device, not just a display device.** Arcturus Vision's Steam Frame passthrough camera + capture-to-splat script turns a mainstream VR headset into a casual 3DGS capture rig, extending the "phone as a splat scanner" pattern to headset cameras.

3. **Shape priors are being used to rescue sparse-view reconstruction quality.** CADSplat's CAD-library approach is part of a broader pattern (also seen in recent semantic- and material-informed digital-twin work) of injecting object-specific structural knowledge to compensate for too few input views — particularly relevant for industrial/manufactured-object use cases where a CAD reference often already exists.

4. **As splatting pipelines multiply, so do subtle correctness bugs.** CorbeauSplat's COLMAP static-scene violation is a useful reminder that dynamic/4D splatting pipelines have sharp edges around classical SfM assumptions that don't announce themselves as errors — worth auditing your own frame-staging code if you run a similar pipeline.

## 2. Kaininja — Extending Native 3D Generators to the Part Level

**Source:** [Hugging Face Papers (trending)](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15659) · arXiv:2609.15659
**Date:** Submitted September 14, 2026; trending on Hugging Face Papers September 15

**What it is:** Kaininja extends TRELLIS.2's native 3D generation pipeline to produce part-decomposed 3D assets instead of single fused meshes, using a dual-volume form of TRELLIS.2's O-Voxel representation. Because a single O-Voxel volume can only store one surface sheet per voxel, it can't represent part-contact interfaces — packing geometry into two coordinated volumes solves this without needing a mask or segmenter anywhere in the pipeline.

**What's new:** It keeps TRELLIS.2's generation speed and quality while adding part-level structure "for free." Training data mixes CAD models with assets authored by an LLM-driven agent — reportedly the first 3D generative model trained on agent-authored part data. It cuts whole-object Chamfer distance by 40% and raises strict part F-score by 16% versus segmentation-based part-generation baselines.

**Why it matters to your research:** Part-aware generation is exactly what's missing from most "single blob" text/image-to-3D pipelines for game-asset, CAD, and XR-scene authoring workflows — editable, animatable, physically-separable parts (doors, limbs, mechanical components) rather than one static mesh. Worth evaluating as a drop-in replacement wherever you currently generate then manually re-segment 3D assets.

## 3. Gaussian-Splatting Ptychography

**Source:** arXiv:2609.14891 (submitted September 14, 2026)

**What it is:** Applies the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation to ptychographic imaging (a coherent diffractive scientific-imaging technique), representing the object and probe as explicit, interpretable Gaussian primitives instead of the dense pixel grids conventional ptychography solvers use.

**What's new:** Primitives relocate to concentrate density where scene structure actually is, and overlapping primitives couple neighboring pixels — which the authors report unexpectedly restores low-frequency phase information that conventional pixel-grid solvers lose. It's a sign that the explicit-primitive/differentiable-rendering toolkit built for novel-view synthesis is starting to generalize to inverse-imaging problems well outside computer vision.

**Why it matters to your research:** A useful data point on how far the Gaussian-splatting representation transfers beyond RGB scene capture — the same "sparse, overlapping, differentiable primitives" trick that makes 3DGS efficient for radiance fields may be reusable in other inverse-rendering or sensor-reconstruction contexts you work with (e.g., depth/LiDAR fusion, sensor calibration).

## 4. Deformable 2D Gaussian Splatting for Efficient 4K Video Compression

**Source:** arXiv:2609.14129 (submitted September 12, 2026)

**What it is:** A video codec that represents a Group of Pictures with a coarse-to-fine, multi-scale 2D Gaussian Splatting structure plus a lightweight deformation network, instead of traditional block-based motion compensation.

**What's new:** Applies the 3DGS toolchain (differentiable rasterization of primitives, deformation fields) to standard 4K video compression rather than 3D scene capture — another instance of Gaussian primitives displacing a classical signal-processing pipeline.

**Why it matters to your research:** If you're storing/streaming large volumes of captured video for downstream 3D reconstruction or XR content pipelines, Gaussian-primitive video codecs could eventually offer a shared representation between "compressed video" and "reconstructable scene," collapsing a step in the capture→reconstruction pipeline.

## 5. Vidu S2 — Real-Time Interactive, Editable, and Spatial Video Generation

**Source:** arXiv:2609.11638 · [GitHub](https://github.com/shengshu-ai/Vidu-S)
**Authors:** Jintao Zhang et al. (Shengshu AI)

**What it is:** Vidu S2 pairs a real-time interactive digital-character model (S2-Avatar, 720p, dynamic-reference updates) with a real-time video-editing model (S2-Editing: style transfer, clothing/character/background replacement) that runs on a live video stream.

**What's new:** S2-Avatar's output stream can be split into synchronized left/right-eye views within the generation pipeline itself, producing real-time spatial (stereoscopic) video of a generated character — i.e., XR-ready avatar video generated live rather than rendered offline and converted to stereo afterward.

**Why it matters to your research:** Real-time stereo-native generative video is a natural complement to Gaussian-splat scene capture for XR: splats handle real captured geometry, while this class of model could supply live, editable synthetic characters/content inside the same spatial-video pipeline.

## 6. Omdia: XR Headwear Shipments Fall 39% as Glasses Gain Share

**Source:** [Omdia](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/pr/2026/sep/global-xr-headwear-shipments-fall-39percent-in-1h26-as-xr-glasses-growth-fails-to-offset-headset-decline)
**Date:** September 2026

**What it is:** Global XR headwear shipments fell 38.7% year-on-year to 1.9M units in 1H26, but XR glasses (lighter, tethered/display-only devices) grew their share of total shipments from 10.2% (1H25) to 25.8% (1H26), even as they failed to fully offset the standalone-headset decline.

**Why it matters to your research:** Confirms the market is bifurcating toward lightweight glasses tethered to phones/compute pucks (see Steam Frame's wireless-PC-streaming design and last cycle's XREAL × Google Project Aura) rather than fully standalone headsets — a useful signal for prioritizing streamed/cloud rendering architectures over pure on-device XR rendering when targeting where hardware volume is actually heading.

## 7. ABot-Recon — Long-Horizon Streaming 3D Reconstruction

**Source:** arXiv:2608.27529 · [GitHub](https://github.com/amap-cvlab/ABot-Recon)
**Date:** Submitted August 27, 2026; still trending in 3D-reconstruction leaderboards this week

**What it is:** Turns a single continuous video into a globally consistent 3D reconstruction online, using only a fixed local context (the preceding 11 frames' cached KV features) with no persistent long-range learned state. A lightweight temporal refiner and a composition-aware pose loss curb drift over very long sequences.

**What's new:** Cuts absolute trajectory error by ~40% relative to prior best results on the Oxford Spires benchmark, while keeping memory bounded regardless of video length — directly addressing the drift/memory blowup that has limited streaming (as opposed to batch/offline) 3D reconstruction from casual video.

**Why it matters to your research:** A bounded-memory streaming reconstruction front end like this is a strong candidate as the online pose/geometry estimator feeding a live Gaussian-splatting or SLAM pipeline, particularly for long-duration XR capture sessions where offline batch reconstruction isn't an option.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Gaussian primitives are generalizing beyond scene capture.** This cycle alone saw 3DGS-style explicit primitives applied to scientific ptychographic imaging and 4K video compression — evidence the representation is becoming a general differentiable-rendering toolkit, not just a radiance-field technique.
2. **3D generation is moving from "one mesh" to "parts."** Kaininja's part-level extension of TRELLIS.2 continues a push (following PartGen and others) toward generative 3D assets that are directly usable in production pipelines without manual re-segmentation.
3. **XR hardware is bifurcating toward tethered/streamed glasses.** Steam Frame's dedicated wireless-streaming radio and Omdia's shipment data both point toward compute-light glasses paired with strong wireless links, rather than fully standalone on-device rendering, as the near-term consumer XR form factor.
4. **Streaming 3D reconstruction is closing the gap with offline methods.** ABot-Recon's bounded-memory, drift-corrected approach suggests online/live reconstruction is becoming viable for long XR capture sessions, not just short clips.

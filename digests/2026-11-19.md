## 1. AMD Completes World Labs Acquisition

**Source:** [AMD IR press release](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1306/amd-completes-acquisition-of-world-labs) · [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/amd-finalizes-82-billion-world-labs-acquisition-adds-godmother-of-ai-as-evp-and-chief-scientist-171451986.html) · [Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/amd-completes-acquisition-of-ai-research-firm-world-labs/) · [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/amd-closes-world-labs-acquisition-and-names-fei-fei-li-chief-scientist/)
**Date:** Close announced Nov 16, 2026 (deal first announced Sep 28), with wide coverage through Nov 18

**What it is:** AMD closed its all-stock acquisition of World Labs, valued at about $8.2B. World Labs makes generative 3D world models: its *Atlas* model and tools output walkable 3D Gaussian-splat scenes or meshes. Fei-Fei Li joins AMD as EVP and Chief Scientist, reporting to Lisa Su. The research team stays intact and will work with AMD's AI-infrastructure engineers.

**What's new:** It is the largest acquisition so far of a company built around spatial intelligence and 3D world generation. It comes days after Atlas's tools and APIs (Viewfinder, Chisel and a developer API that exports SPZ splats) opened to the public on Nov 12 ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/world-labs-opens-atlas-world-model-to-public-with-viewfinder-chisel-and), [World Labs blog](https://worldlabs.ai/blog)).

**Why it matters to you:** A large GPU vendor now owns a leading splat-native world model. Expect ROCm-optimised splat rendering and training stacks, and stronger industry backing for SPZ as an interchange format. If you use the Atlas API for synthetic scene generation or XR content, watch for pricing and licensing changes after the acquisition.

## 2. Blender 5.3 — Native Gaussian Splat Support

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/blender-5-3-ships-with-native-gaussian-splat-support) · [Linux Today](https://www.linuxtoday.com/blog/blender-5-3-released-with-gaussian-splatting-vulkan-by-default/) · [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/blender-5-3-finally-gets-game-changing-native-gaussian-splat-support) · [Digital Production (preview)](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/09/22/blender-5-3-brings-gaussian-splats-home/)
**Date:** Stable release around Nov 10–18; the Linux Today write-up is dated Nov 18, 2026

**What it is:** Blender 5.3 imports PLY and SPZ splats as a new native point-cloud type and renders them in Workbench, EEVEE and Cycles without an add-on. Geometry Nodes gets a *Set Point Cloud Type* node and an *Import SPZ* node, and *Import PLY* now outputs a point cloud when it detects splat data. Vulkan is now the default backend on Linux.

**What's new:** Until now, splats in Blender needed third-party add-ons such as KIRI's. Blender 5.3 cannot export splats yet; the release is import and render only.

**Why it matters to you:** You can composite splat captures with meshes, lights and path-traced renders in one free DCC tool, and use Geometry Nodes to procedurally edit or instance splat data. That makes Blender useful for building datasets, for evaluating splat relighting against Cycles ground truth, and for laying out XR scenes. Because export is missing, you still need SuperSplat or similar tools to write splats back out.

## 3. Accelerating 3DGS Training through Selective Freezing and Computation Caching

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.22059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22059)
**Date:** Submitted ~Nov 17, 2026 (cs.AR, Hardware Architecture)

**What it is:** The paper proposes *progressive Gaussian freezing*: Gaussians that have converged are frozen, and their cumulative contribution to the image is cached so later iterations skip recomputing them. The authors pair this with a dedicated accelerator design called the **ElsaGS core**.

**What's new:** Earlier work such as GSta already froze converged Gaussians in software. This paper also caches what frozen Gaussians contribute to rendering and co-designs hardware around that, which treats 3DGS training as a workload worth dedicated silicon.

**Why it matters to you:** The algorithmic part (freezing plus caching) could slot into existing fast-3DGS trainers such as FastGS and Faster-GS. The hardware part suggests where on-device splat capture for XR headsets and phones could go. Search engines did not index the full text, so check the paper's speedup numbers before citing them.

## 4. Topo-4DGS & TopoEgo — Topology-Aware 4D Gaussian Splatting for Egocentric Video

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.16012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16012)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 11, 2026

**What it is:** The paper releases **TopoEgo**, which it describes as the first benchmark for topological changes in egocentric video: fission, fusion, opening and closing (for example, tearing paper or opening a drawer). It also proposes **Topo-4DGS**, a 4D Gaussian splatting method designed to handle those changes.

**What's new:** Most dynamic 3DGS methods assume each primitive deforms continuously over time, so they fail when objects split, merge or open. This work targets that failure mode and provides a benchmark for it.

**Why it matters to you:** Head-worn video of hands manipulating objects is a central XR and embodied-AI use case. TopoEgo gives you an evaluation set for that setting, and the topology handling is relevant to any 4D capture pipeline for AR replays or training robots on manipulation.

## 5. Stage-wise Lineage-aware Dropout for Sparse-View 3DGS

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.17752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17752)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 13, 2026

**What it is:** This regulariser targets overfitting in sparse-view 3DGS. Gaussians are grouped into "lineage families" by the founder Gaussian they came from through cloning or splitting, and dropout is applied per family and per training stage, not per individual Gaussian.

**What's new:** Earlier dropout methods for sparse-view 3DGS (DropGaussian, DropoutGS) drop Gaussians independently. Using the densification family tree is a new structural prior that takes into account how Gaussians that come from the same parent tend to adapt together to the training views.

**Why it matters to you:** The change is cheap and easy to add to the training loop, and it helps in few-view capture settings such as quick phone scans, XR room capture and robot views. It may also combine with feed-forward initialisation.

## 6. SplatAnything — Gaussian Spacing Normalization (GSNorm) for Feed-forward 3DGS

**Source:** [Radiance Fields paper page](https://radiancefields.com/papers/splatanything-gaussian-spacing-normalization-for-feed-forward-3dgs-across-arbitrary-scenes) · [AI Weekly: code release](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/splatanything-releases-inference-code-with-gaussian-spacing-fix) · [HF Daily Papers, Nov 2026](https://huggingface.co/papers/month/2026-11)
**Date:** Among the most-upvoted Hugging Face papers for the week ending Nov 15; inference code released this week

**What it is:** Feed-forward 3DGS models usually predict Gaussian attributes in a space normalised by a global radial statistic. That does not control how far apart neighbouring Gaussians sit, so the training targets are ambiguous. GSNorm normalises by a robust scene-level statistic of Gaussian spacing instead. The authors report state-of-the-art results, with the largest gains when scene scale and viewpoint vary widely, from tabletop objects to aerial views.

**What's new:** It identifies the choice of normalisation space as a hidden cause of poor scale generalisation in feed-forward splatting and fixes it with a simple change.

**Why it matters to you:** If you train or fine-tune feed-forward reconstructors (pixelSplat, MVSplat, AnySplat or VGGT-based heads), this is an easy change to test, and the released code makes it quick. Don't confuse it with the NeurIPS 2026 paper *"Geometry Is All You Predict"*, which has a similar name ([NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/166115)).

## 7. Feed-forward Gaussian Splatting Beyond RGB: ThermalSplat & UltraVoxelGS

**Source:** [ThermalSplat, arXiv 2611.15435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15435) · [UltraVoxelGS, arXiv 2611.11951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11951) · [OctreeSplat, arXiv 2611.02658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02658)
**Date:** November 2026 (UltraVoxelGS Nov 9; ThermalSplat mid-Nov)

**What it is:** **ThermalSplat** reconstructs scenes from a few thermal images with a generalisable feed-forward splatting model that needs no camera poses. **UltraVoxelGS** is a voxel-first feed-forward GS model for 3D ultrasound reconstruction. **OctreeSplat** allocates feed-forward Gaussians through an octree, adapting density to local scene complexity.

**What's new:** Feed-forward GS is spreading from RGB benchmarks to other sensing modalities, and toward compact, adaptive primitive budgets.

**Why it matters to you:** The papers show that pose-free feed-forward GS backbones carry over to thermal and medical imaging. That is useful if you work with multi-sensor rigs such as the RGB-thermal cameras on robots or drones, and for compact representations that XR devices can stream.

## Trend Watch

1. **The splat ecosystem is converging on SPZ.** Blender 5.3, UEFN 43.00 and World Labs' API all read or write SPZ, which makes it the practical interchange format.
2. **Hardware and infrastructure players are moving into 3D world models.** AMD's purchase of World Labs and the ElsaGS accelerator paper suggest splat training and rendering are becoming first-class compute workloads.
3. **Feed-forward GS is maturing.** Recent work focuses on normalisation (GSNorm), adaptive budgets (OctreeSplat) and new modalities (thermal, ultrasound), not only higher benchmark quality.
4. **Egocentric and 4D research is lining up with the glasses market.** As Apple's and Google's glasses get closer to shipping, benchmarks for egocentric 4D reconstruction and topology changes become more relevant to products.

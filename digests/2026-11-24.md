## 1. WildAnySplat — Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting in the Wild

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.31023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31023)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 23, 2026. Listed as accepted to SIGGRAPH Asia 2026.

**What it is:** A feed-forward network that predicts 3D Gaussians from unconstrained, in-the-wild photo collections. It is built to handle transient occluders (people, cars) and appearance changes (lighting, weather) while keeping single-pass inference speed.

**What's new:** Earlier "in-the-wild" 3DGS methods (WildGaussians, GS-W) needed per-scene optimization. Earlier feed-forward models (AnySplat, WildSplat, WildSplatter) either assumed clean captures or handled appearance and transients only partly. According to its abstract, this paper targets both problems in one feed-forward model.

**Relevance to you:** This is the most practical step toward splatting internet or crowd-sourced imagery without hours of per-scene fitting. It also fits casual XR capture, where people walk through the frame. *Caveat:* a second search couldn't confirm the venue, so check the SIGGRAPH Asia list.

## 2. SpectralGS — Spectral Gaussian Splatting for Unsupervised Spectral Super-Resolution

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.29388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29388)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 22, 2026

**What it is:** An unsupervised method that recovers hyperspectral images from low-spectral-resolution input (e.g. RGB or multispectral). It models the spectral dimension with Gaussian primitives, a "splat along wavelength" formulation.

**What's new:** It applies the Gaussian splatting representation to a non-spatial axis: a continuous spectrum instead of 3D space. It also needs no paired hyperspectral training data.

**Relevance to you:** Mainly a signal that the 3DGS representation generalises as a continuous-function primitive. It could matter if you work on material or BRDF capture or on multispectral scene reconstruction.

## 3. VG3R — Video Generation Model for Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.28520](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.28520)
**Date:** November 2026

**What it is:** VG3R repurposes a pretrained video *generation* model as the backbone for feed-forward geometry prediction. It reports **14–33% lower video-depth error than VGGT**. Camera-pose estimation still lags dedicated geometry foundation models.

**What's new:** Video diffusion priors are being used as geometry learners, not just as data generators. This follows the broader trend of treating generative world models as 3D foundation models.

**Relevance to you:** If you use VGGT or similar backbones for depth or initialisation, VG3R is a candidate replacement for depth-heavy tasks. The weaker pose estimates mean you may still want a VGGT-style head or SfM for cameras.

## 4. SGAR & EcoVGGT — Scaling Feed-Forward Reconstruction to Long Sequences

**Sources:** [SGAR — arXiv 2611.26493](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26493) · [EcoVGGT — arXiv 2611.18099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18099)
**Date:** November 2026

**What they are:**
- **SGAR** replaces dense global attention with *sparse, geometry-guided attention* and fine-tunes from VGGT-Ω. The authors report a **7.5× speed-up over VGGT-Ω at 1,000 views**.
- **EcoVGGT** is a *training-free* "pinned-key" inference scheme for streaming long videos. On 500 frames it raises VGGT's success rate from **70% to 82%** while running **8.5× faster**.

**What's new:** Together they target the main practical limit of VGGT-family models, which is quadratic cost and failure on long captures. EcoVGGT can be applied to existing checkpoints with no retraining.

**Relevance to you:** These are directly usable for room- and building-scale captures and long XR walkthroughs. EcoVGGT is the low-effort option to try first.

## 5. GC-GS — Gradient Conflict as a Geometry Signal

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.28111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28111)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 18, 2026

**What it is:** The authors observe that gradients from different reconstruction losses *conflict* throughout 3DGS training, most severely in Gaussian **position** parameters. They use that conflict as a signal to guide geometry optimisation.

**What's new:** It is a diagnostic idea in the spirit of Faster-GS. It turns a training pathology into a cue for geometry, which could plug into densification and pruning or into surface-regularisation schedules.

**Relevance to you:** It is cheap to try if you are fighting floaters or poor surface quality in 3DGS-to-mesh pipelines.

## 6. AGSR and the Selective-Freezing Accelerator — 3DGS Quality and Speed

**Sources:** [AGSR — arXiv 2611.25934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25934) · [Selective Freezing & Caching — arXiv 2611.22059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22059)
**Dates:** Nov 19 and Nov 17, 2026

- **AGSR** (accepted to MMSP 2026) is aperture-guided super-resolution for Gaussian splatting. It renders above training resolution and beats lightweight image-space SR baselines on Mip-NeRF 360.
- **Selective Freezing** progressively freezes converged Gaussians and proposes a hardware accelerator that caches their contributions. It is a hardware-and-algorithm co-design for faster training.

**Relevance to you:** AGSR is relevant for high-PPD XR displays, where captured resolution falls short of display resolution. The freezing idea applies to software training loops too, not only to custom hardware.

## 7. PhyVGGT & IGRecon4D — Feed-Forward 4D: Articulation and Driving Scenes

**Sources:** [PhyVGGT — arXiv 2611.22244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22244) · [IGRecon4D — arXiv 2611.21126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21126)
**Date:** November 2026

- **PhyVGGT** reconstructs *articulated objects* (doors, drawers, joints) from monocular video in a single pass on top of a frozen 3D foundation model.
- **IGRecon4D** reconstructs dynamic driving scenes with pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians per frame and explicit awareness of dynamic objects.

**Relevance to you:** Feed-forward models are moving from static scenes to articulated and dynamic ones, continuing the D4RT and 4D-VGGT line. PhyVGGT is especially relevant for interactive XR digital twins, where objects need to move.

## 1. Alibaba Launches Qwen3.8-Max — 2.4T-Parameter Multimodal Flagship

**Source:** [TNGlobal](https://technode.global/2026/08/04/chinas-alibaba-launches-qwen3-8-max-ai-model-with-2-4t-parameters-1m-token-context-window/) · [Alibaba Group announcement](https://www.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-2021044032125272064) · [Quartz](https://qz.com/alibaba-qwen38-max-ai-model-launch-080326)
**Date:** August 3, 2026

**What it is:** Qwen3.8-Max is Alibaba's largest and most capable model to date — a 2.4T-parameter sparse Mixture-of-Experts model with 95B active parameters at inference, a 1M-token context window, and native multimodal support for text, images, and video. It's available now via API ($2/$6 per million input/output tokens), with open weights due on Hugging Face the week of August 10.

**What's new:** Despite its enormous total parameter count, the sparse-MoE routing keeps inference cost comparable to much smaller dense models. Alibaba reports the model ranks 5th on Text Arena and 2nd on Vision Arena — a strong showing for a model shipping open weights, which is unusual at this scale.

**Why it matters:** A near-frontier open-weight vision-language model at this scale (once weights land next week) would be immediately useful as a captioning/semantic backbone for 3D asset pipelines, scene understanding in XR passthrough, and as a judge/annotator for large-scale 3D reconstruction dataset curation.

## 3. NVIDIA Déjà View (DVLT) — Looped Transformers for Multi-View 3D Reconstruction

**Source:** [Project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dvl/projects/dvlt/) · [arXiv 2605.30215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215) · [GitHub — nv-tlabs/dvlt](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/dvlt)
**Authors:** NVIDIA, University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, University of Toronto, ETH Zürich
**Date:** Paper May 2026; code and checkpoints publicly live now

**What it is:** A 117M-parameter looped transformer that reconstructs camera poses and dense geometry (rays, depth, confidence) from an unordered set of images by repeatedly applying the same shared attention block. A single checkpoint exposes inference compute as a slider — more loop iterations trade latency for accuracy.

**What's new:** At 8–10× fewer parameters and 1.9–2.3× less compute than prior feed-forward baselines (e.g., VGGT-class models), Déjà View matches or beats systems over 1B parameters across five indoor/outdoor reconstruction benchmarks. The "compute as a slider" design is a genuinely new interface for feed-forward 3D reconstruction.

**Why it matters:** This is directly relevant if you run 3D reconstruction on constrained hardware (on-device XR, edge capture rigs) — the parameter/compute efficiency means near-SOTA geometry estimation without a datacenter GPU, and the adjustable-compute knob is useful for real-time vs. offline quality tradeoffs in the same deployed model.

## 4. NVIDIA ArtiFixer — Repairing Under-Observed Regions in Gaussian Splat Scenes

**Source:** [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/nvidia-s-artifixer-beats-prior-3d-scene-reconstruction-by-3-db) · [DigitalToday](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/74408/nvidia-unveils-artifixer-ai-model-for-3d-scene-reconstruction)
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** ArtiFixer targets a well-known 3DGS failure mode: regions that are poorly observed in the source captures (occluded corners, sparse-view gaps) render as blurry artifacts. NVIDIA released it in three variants — an autoregressive base model that hallucinates plausible novel viewpoints, ArtiFixer3D which distills that output into a 3D representation, and ArtiFixer3D+ which applies the autoregressive step again as post-processing.

**What's new:** NVIDIA reports a 3 dB improvement over prior 3D scene reconstruction quality metrics, particularly in complex indoor scenes with many occluding objects — a scenario where standard 3DGS pipelines degrade sharply.

**Why it matters:** If you capture real-world scenes for splatting (interiors, product scans, XR environments), sparse/occluded coverage is the most common practical failure mode. A drop-in repair model for this specific problem is directly usable in production capture pipelines.

## 5. Foundry Nuke 17.1 Open Beta — Animated, Relit Gaussian Splats

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/nuke-17.1-open-beta-adds-dynamic-gaussian-splats-and-basic-relighting) · [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/07/foundry-releases-nuke-17-1-in-open-beta/)
**Date:** July 2026 (open beta, publicly available)

**What it is:** Building on Nuke 17.0's native Gaussian splat import/export (Feb 2026), the 17.1 open beta adds animated splats via GeoSequencer, SPLAT file export, basic relighting (Direct/Point/Spot lights composited onto splat scenes via SplatRender), and a Hydra 2.0-backed 3D viewport.

**What's new:** Relighting is the significant addition — previously, splats were essentially "baked" lighting-wise, which limited their use for VFX compositing where shots need to match a scene's lighting. This is one of the first mainstream compositing tools to offer even basic relighting for splats.

**Why it matters:** For anyone integrating splat captures into film/VFX or mixed-media pipelines, this closes a major workflow gap — splats can now be dropped into a comp and adjusted to match scene lighting rather than treated as a fixed background plate.

## 6. Khronos KHR_gaussian_splatting — glTF Extension Nearing Ratification

**Source:** [Khronos press release](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/gltf-gaussian-splatting-press-release) · [GitHub spec](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/tree/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_gaussian_splatting) · [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/khronos-moves-to-integrate-gaussian-splatting-into-gltf-3d-format/)
**Date:** Announced Feb 2026; release-candidate review ongoing, ratification targeted Q2–Q3 2026

**What it is:** A cross-platform glTF 2.0 extension defining how Gaussian splat attributes (position, orientation, scale, spherical-harmonic color, opacity) are stored inside standard glTF mesh primitives — making splats a first-class, interchangeable asset type rather than a proprietary format per tool.

**What's new:** The 3D Formats Working Group is in the final feedback-collection phase before formal ratification. This is the standardization step the ecosystem has been waiting for — once ratified, splats become as portable as any other glTF asset across engines, viewers, and DCC tools (Nuke, Unreal, Unity, Blender, web viewers).

**Why it matters:** If you're building tooling around splats, this is the format contract to target now — building against the release candidate positions you ahead of the ratification, and avoids the fragmentation of proprietary .splat/.ply variants.

## 7. World Labs Marble × NVIDIA Isaac Sim — Generative Splats for Robotics Simulation

**Source:** [NVIDIA Technical Blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/simulate-robotic-environments-faster-with-nvidia-isaac-sim-and-world-labs-marble/) · [World Labs blog](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/marble-world-model)
**Date:** Ongoing rollout through 2026

**What it is:** Marble generates fully explorable, Gaussian-splat-represented 3D environments from a single image or text prompt, imagining plausible geometry outside the frame. NVIDIA's integration with Isaac Sim lets these generated worlds be dropped directly into robotics simulation, cutting environment-authoring time from weeks to hours. World Labs also reports Sony Pictures using Marble for virtual production at roughly 40× the speed of legacy VP pipelines.

**What's new:** The combination of generative world models with physics-capable simulation platforms is a meaningfully new capability — previously, simulation-ready environments required manual scene construction or photogrammetry capture; now a single image can seed a full explorable, physically-groundable scene.

**Why it matters:** For sim-to-real robotics work or rapid XR environment prototyping, this collapses a traditionally weeks-long asset pipeline into a same-day iteration loop — worth evaluating if scene-authoring time is currently a bottleneck in your work.

## 9. SpatialBench — Cross-Paradigm Benchmark for Spatial Foundation Models

**Source:** [arXiv 2605.27367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27367) · [Hugging Face paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.27367) · [GitHub](https://github.com/Ropedia/SpatialBench)
**Date:** May 26, 2026

**What it is:** A benchmark spanning 19 datasets and 546 scenes across 5 spatial domains, evaluating 41 models across 6 different modeling paradigms (feed-forward reconstruction, SLAM-style streaming, NeRF/3DGS optimization, etc.) on 5 task suites under 4 input-density settings. The authors also release DA-Next-5M, a large-scale dataset, and DA-Next, a strong baseline model.

**What's new:** Prior benchmarks tended to evaluate within a single paradigm (e.g., only feed-forward reconstruction models against each other). SpatialBench is the first to put fundamentally different architectural approaches on the same footing, and finds that full-context attention maximizes accuracy while bounded-memory approaches are what actually unlock long-sequence scalability — and that domain alignment/data quality matters more than raw dataset scale.

**Why it matters:** If you're choosing an architecture family for a new 3D reconstruction or spatial-understanding project, this is a genuinely useful reference for which paradigm actually fits your input density and sequence-length regime, rather than defaulting to whatever's currently trendy.

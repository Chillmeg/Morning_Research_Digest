## 1. Tactile Sensing & Representation Learning Survey

**Source:** arXiv (via web search) — authors Chandrakant Bothe and Parag Chourasia
**Date:** August 24, 2026

**What it is:** A comprehensive survey arguing that vision-only perception is insufficient for robust dexterous manipulation in "Physical AI," and that explicit contact/tactile information needs to be integrated into representation-learning pipelines alongside visual features.

**What's new:** It's a fresh (yesterday, relative to this digest) synthesis of tactile representation-learning methods specifically framed against the current wave of vision-centric foundation models — pushing back on the assumption that scaled vision encoders alone are enough for embodied tasks.

**Why it matters to you:** If your 3D reconstruction or XR work touches embodied/robotic capture (e.g., scanning objects for manipulation, digital-twin pipelines that need contact-consistent geometry), this is a useful map of where tactile-conditioned representations could plug into vision pipelines you already run.

## 3. NVIDIA vkSplatting 2026.1 — Instanced, Indexed Gaussian Splats

**Source:** NVIDIA Developer blog / splatlabs.ai coverage (via web search)
**Date:** 2026 (current release line)

**What it is:** The latest release of NVIDIA's open-source (Apache 2.0) Vulkan Gaussian-splatting sample, `vk_gaussian_splatting`, adds the ability to instance, index, and reuse splats across a scene via global index tables and unified sorting — mirroring how game engines handle mesh instancing.

**What's new:** Prior real-time 3DGS renderers generally treated every splat as scene-unique data. Instancing support means repeated objects (trees, furniture, props) in a splatted scene no longer need to be stored and sorted redundantly, which is a meaningful step toward game-engine-grade splat scenes rather than one-off captures.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're building interactive 3DGS scenes with repeated assets (e.g., procedurally populated environments, XR scene composition), this removes a real memory/perf bottleneck and is a signal that splat tooling is converging with traditional real-time rendering pipelines.

## 4. Khronos KHR_gaussian_splatting glTF Extension — Ratification Watch

**Source:** Khronos Group press materials, CG Channel, UploadVR (via web search)
**Date:** Release candidate announced Feb 3, 2026; ratification expected Q2 2026

**What it is:** A standardized way to store 3D Gaussian splats inside glTF 2.0, extending the mesh primitive to carry per-Gaussian position, orientation, scale, color, and opacity. Backed by NVIDIA, Google, Adobe, and Cesium, with early adopters including CesiumJS, Esri ArcGIS, Niantic Spatial's Scaniverse, and XGrids Lixel Cybercolor.

**What's new:** This is the interoperability layer the Gaussian splatting ecosystem has been missing — right now every tool (Polycam, Luma, Postshot, Nerfstudio) effectively has its own splat format. A ratified glTF extension means splats become a portable, engine-agnostic asset type for the first time.

**Why it matters to you:** Worth tracking for pipeline decisions — once ratified, exporting/importing splats between capture tools, DCC software, and XR runtimes (visionOS, Android XR) should become far less bespoke than today's per-tool converters.

**Why it matters to you:** Directly relevant if your capture pipelines ever operate outside controlled studio conditions (outdoor, low-light, hazy environments) — the winning methods are a good starting point for robustifying a 3DGS/NeRF pipeline against non-ideal input.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Gaussian splatting is standardizing.** Between Khronos's glTF extension nearing ratification and NVIDIA adding game-engine-style instancing to vk_gaussian_splatting, the ecosystem is visibly moving from "every tool has its own splat format" toward portable, interoperable splat assets.

2. **Robustness benchmarks are catching up to production reality.** NTIRE's RealX3D challenge (adverse-condition 3D reconstruction) and the growing SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 slate on sparse/uncalibrated/refractive splatting both point to the field moving past clean-lab benchmarks toward real-world failure modes.

3. **The literature volume is now unmanageable by hand.** At ~4 new radiance-field papers a day, the practical skill is triaging via conference highlight lists, award papers, and curated digests rather than tracking arXiv directly — which also means digests like this one should stay skeptical of any single day's arXiv listing looking "quiet."

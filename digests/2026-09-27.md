## 3. Towards Practical Compression of 3D Gaussian Splatting (COSA-GS)

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.30245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30245)
**Authors:** Pengpeng Yu et al.
**Date:** September 24, 2026

**What it is:** COSA-GS tackles the large storage footprint of trained 3DGS scenes by building compression context without spatial aggregation, using anchor-wise causal factorization to encode Gaussian attributes more compactly.

**What's new:** Most prior 3DGS compression methods rely on spatial neighborhood aggregation (e.g., octrees, hash grids) to build entropy-coding context, which adds overhead and can blur local detail. Anchor-wise causal factorization is a structurally different approach to context modeling for compression.

**Why it matters to my research areas:** Storage and bandwidth are the practical bottleneck for shipping 3DGS scenes to consumer devices (web, mobile, XR headsets) — better compression directly affects how large/detailed a splat scene can be streamed or shipped in an app bundle.

## 4. GaussPDE — Graph-Based PDE-Driven Rendering for 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.27264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27264)
**Authors:** Haoyuan Yue, Fengyuan Ye, Ziyin Li
**Date:** September 23, 2026

**What it is:** GaussPDE injects physically structured partial differential equation (PDE) dynamics directly into a pretrained 3D Gaussian scene, without requiring mesh extraction, voxelization, or model retraining.

**What's new:** Simulating physics (fluid, deformation, heat diffusion, etc.) on top of a Gaussian splat scene has typically required converting to an intermediate representation (mesh/voxel grid) first. Applying PDE dynamics directly on the Gaussian primitives keeps the pipeline end-to-end and avoids reconstruction artifacts from format conversion.

**Why it matters to my research areas:** Physically-plausible dynamics layered onto captured splat scenes is directly relevant to interactive XR content and simulation-in-the-loop applications where a captured real-world scene needs to respond physically rather than remain static.

## 5. SplatLabel — Pseudo-Labelling Through 4D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.29836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29836)
**Authors:** Nitya Nanvani, Andras Palffy, Holger Caesar
**Date:** September 24, 2026

**What it is:** An automated pipeline that uses a 4D Gaussian representation to generate LiDAR semantic segmentation labels with predictive confidence, plus semantic occupancy grids at arbitrary voxel resolutions.

**What's new:** Rather than using 4D Gaussian Splatting purely for view synthesis, this repurposes the reconstructed dynamic-scene representation as a labelling engine — turning a reconstruction technique into a data-generation/annotation tool for downstream perception models.

**Why it matters to my research areas:** This is a good example of Gaussian splatting being used as infrastructure (auto-labelling, dataset generation) rather than only as a rendering target — relevant for anyone building perception training pipelines on top of reconstructed scenes, including autonomous driving and robotics contexts.

## 6. OceanXL — Large-Scale Underwater 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.29985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29985)
**Date:** September 23, 2026

**What it is:** A fast, scalable 3DGS framework for large-scale underwater scene reconstruction. It uses a divide-and-conquer block-partitioning strategy to optimize spatially coherent chunks of a scene while preserving global geometric consistency, plus adaptive pruning tuned to underwater conditions (light attenuation, scattering) to keep the representation compact.

**What's new:** Underwater 3DGS work to date has mostly targeted small scenes with dedicated appearance/restoration modules; OceanXL is explicitly designed for large-scale coverage (five large scenes tested) while remaining competitive on small-scale benchmarks with a much smaller model footprint than underwater-specific baselines.

**Why it matters to my research areas:** Extends 3DGS's demonstrated large-scale scalability (already proven for outdoor/urban scenes) into a genuinely different, harder domain — useful signal for how the block-partitioning + pruning recipe generalizes to degraded-imaging conditions more broadly (fog, low light, aerial haze).

## 7. GAPS — Generative Active Pseudo-View Selection for Sparse-View 3DGS

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.23436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23436)
**Date:** Submitted Sep 20, 2026; revised Sep 24, 2026

**What it is:** An alternating optimization framework that uses a pretrained image diffusion model to generate geometrically consistent "pseudo-views," providing extra supervision for 3D Gaussian Splatting when only a sparse set of real input views is available.

**What's new:** Sparse-view 3DGS quality typically degrades sharply as view count drops; GAPS actively selects *which* pseudo-views to generate (rather than randomly sampling novel viewpoints) to maximize their usefulness as training supervision, closing the loop between generation and reconstruction.

**Why it matters to my research areas:** Sparse-view capture is the realistic capture condition for most consumer and field-capture use cases (a handful of phone photos, not a full multi-camera rig) — active pseudo-view generation is a promising direction for making high-quality splats practical outside lab conditions.

## 8. YOLO26 — Ultralytics' Real-Time Vision Model with Built-In Depth Estimation

**Source:** [Ultralytics announcement](https://www.ultralytics.com/news/ultralytics-redefines-state-of-the-art-vision-ai-with-yolo26)
**Date:** September 2026

**What it is:** The latest YOLO release, engineered for lightweight, fast deployment in real-world real-time settings, and notably adding a native depth-estimation task that predicts per-pixel, metric-scale depth from a single RGB image.

**What's new:** Bundling metric-scale monocular depth estimation directly into the YOLO family (rather than requiring a separate specialized depth model) is new — it puts a fast, deployable depth signal in the hands of anyone already running YOLO for detection/segmentation.

**Why it matters to my research areas:** A lightweight, real-time, metric-depth-capable detector is directly useful as a fast geometry prior for 3D reconstruction and Gaussian splatting initialization on edge/mobile devices, where running a large depth foundation model (e.g., DINOv3-scale) isn't feasible.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Gaussian Splatting compression and efficiency keep compounding.** COSA-GS (compression), GAPS (sparse-view supervision), and OceanXL (large-scale scalability under adverse conditions) all target the same underlying goal from different angles: making 3DGS practical to capture, store, and ship outside idealized lab conditions.

2. **3DGS is becoming infrastructure, not just a rendering target.** SplatLabel's use of 4D Gaussian Splatting as an auto-labelling engine for LiDAR segmentation, and GaussPDE's use of Gaussians as a physics simulation substrate, both show the representation being repurposed for tasks well beyond novel-view synthesis.

3. **XR hardware is bifurcating toward tethered, ultralight form factors.** Meta's Project Phoenix headset (tethered puck, ~100g) alongside the broader Android XR ecosystem's glasses push suggests the next wave of consumer XR hardware will trade on-device compute for weight — with direct implications for how splat/mesh scenes need to be streamed rather than rendered locally.

4. **NeurIPS 2026 notification wave incoming.** With notifications sent September 24, expect a surge of newly-public 3D vision, generative 3D, and world-model papers on arXiv over the next 1–2 weeks as authors post camera-ready and pre-print versions — worth a dedicated look in early October.

## 2. FR3D — Future Dynamic 3D Reconstruction with Disentangled Ego-Motion

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.18250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18250) · [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18250)  
**Authors:** Nils Morbitzer, Jonathan Evers, Artem Savkin, Thomas Stauner, Nassir Navab, Federico Tombari, Stefano Gasperini — TU Munich / Google  
**Date:** June 16, 2026 — Accepted at ICML 2026

**What it is:** FR3D is a world model that predicts a persistent 3D latent representation for *future* dynamic scene states, not just the current observed frame. The key contribution is explicitly decoupling the 3D evolution of the scene from the agent's own motion (ego-motion), which is treated as a latent proxy for action. This disentanglement resolves the ambiguity between self-motion and world-motion that causes prior generative video world models to produce geometrically inconsistent outputs (objects morphing, vanishing) over long time horizons.

**What's new:** Existing 2D generative world models (e.g., UniSim, GAIA-2) achieve high photorealism in video synthesis but mix ego-motion and environmental dynamics in image space, breaking geometric consistency over time. FR3D lifts prediction into 3D latent space and separates these two sources of motion, maintaining physical consistency well beyond what 2D models can achieve.

**Why it matters to you:** FR3D represents a direct advance toward autonomous systems that can anticipate how a 3D scene will look from a future vantage point — critical for XR navigation (pre-loading scene geometry ahead of the user's path), dynamic object avoidance in spatial computing, and generating temporally consistent multi-view training data for downstream 3DGS and NeRF models.

## 3. Leveraging NeRF-Rendered Images for 3D Gaussian Splatting (Street Scenes)

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.09034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09034) · [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09034v1)  
**Authors:** (from arXiv 2606.09034)  
**Date:** June 2026 (arXiv preprint)

**What it is:** This paper proposes a hybrid pipeline that uses a pre-trained street-specific NeRF to generate auxiliary training images for a 3DGS model, specifically targeting street-level driving scenes. NeRF-rendered images serve two purposes: (1) they are used to inpaint/remove transient objects (pedestrians, vehicles) from real input views, and (2) they generate bird's-eye-view frames that augment the sparse angular coverage of dashcam imagery. A diffusion-based image enhancement step further sharpens the synthetic views before they enter 3DGS training.

**What's new:** Prior work treats NeRF and 3DGS as competing approaches. This paper exploits their complementary strengths — NeRF's higher geometric fidelity and view-synthesis quality in sparse regions versus 3DGS's real-time rendering speed — within a single unified training pipeline. The bird's-eye-view augmentation from NeRF is a novel strategy for addressing the severe angular bias in driving datasets.

**Why it matters to you:** Street-scene 3D reconstruction is a core bottleneck for XR navigation and autonomous-driving simulation. The approach of using NeRF as a "data generator" to bootstrap 3DGS quality is a broadly transferable pattern — applicable to any domain where input views are sparse or angularly biased (aerial photography, indoor scanning with limited viewpoints).

## 4. Beyond Spherical Harmonics — Rethinking Appearance Models for Radiance Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.10478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10478)  
**Authors:** Ewa Miazga et al.  
**Date:** June 8, 2026 (arXiv preprint)

**What it is:** Spherical Harmonics (SH) have been the default appearance model for view-dependent color in both NeRF and 3DGS since their inception, but SH basis functions are frequency-limited and cannot represent high-frequency specular highlights accurately without increasing basis order (and thus memory). This paper performs a systematic analysis of alternative appearance models for radiance field reconstruction and proposes a more expressive, compact replacement that captures fine specular detail without the memory overhead of high-order SH.

**What's new:** Most 3DGS improvements focus on Gaussian geometry (densification, pruning, deformation) while leaving the SH appearance model unchanged. This paper directly attacks the appearance bottleneck — the component responsible for specular reflections, metallic surfaces, and angular colour variation — which has been an underexplored frontier since the original 3DGS paper.

**Why it matters to you:** Reflective and specular surfaces are among the most visually prominent artefacts in current 3DGS reconstructions (cf. Ref-DGS below). A drop-in SH replacement that improves appearance fidelity would benefit virtually every 3DGS-based pipeline. This is particularly relevant for XR product visualization, where accurate material appearance is non-negotiable.

## 5. 3D-CoS — VLM Code Synthesis as a New 3D Reconstruction Paradigm

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.11314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11314)  
**Date:** June 9, 2026 (arXiv preprint)

**What it is:** 3D-CoS (3D via Code Synthesis) proposes using a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to write and execute reconstruction code conditioned on an input image or scene description, rather than having a neural network directly regress 3D geometry. The VLM reasons about the scene structure in language space and emits procedural code that constructs a geometric representation, which is then refined by gradient-based optimisation.

**What's new:** All prior learning-based 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS, single-view reconstructors) map inputs directly to neural or explicit geometry. 3D-CoS inserts a code-generation layer between perception and geometry — leveraging the semantic and compositional reasoning capabilities of VLMs that are difficult to distill into a direct regression model. It is one of the first papers to treat 3D reconstruction as a code-generation task.

**Why it matters to you:** This approach has implications for compositional and editable 3D reconstruction — if the VLM generates modular, parameterized code, individual scene elements can be identified and modified post-reconstruction in a way that NeRF/3DGS black-box approaches do not support. Relevant to XR scene authoring workflows where semantic editability is required.

## 6. Ref-DGS — Reflective Dual Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2603.07664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07664) · [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07664v3)  
**Authors:** (from arXiv 2603.07664)  
**Date:** March 2026 — Accepted at SIGGRAPH Conference Papers 2026 (July 19–23, 2026)

**What it is:** Ref-DGS addresses one of the most persistent failure modes of 3DGS: scenes containing reflective materials (glass, metal, polished surfaces). These violate the multi-view appearance consistency assumption that 3DGS relies on, because the same surface point appears drastically different from different view angles due to specular and indirect reflection. Ref-DGS introduces a dual Gaussian representation — one set models geometry and diffuse colour, the other models view-dependent reflective effects — and trains both with physically-motivated reflection priors.

**What's new:** Prior GS-based methods either ignore reflections entirely (producing blurry or incorrect appearance on shiny objects) or apply post-process correction. Ref-DGS is the first 3DGS method to model indirect and specular reflections as first-class scene properties within the Gaussian primitive framework, with results presented at SIGGRAPH — the premier venue for rendering.

**Why it matters to you:** Reflective surfaces are ubiquitous in real-world scenes (windows, displays, furniture, vehicles). Any XR application scanning and rendering real indoor or outdoor spaces will encounter this problem. Ref-DGS's SIGGRAPH acceptance signals it is production-quality and likely to be integrated into downstream pipelines in the near term.

## 8. Recent Advances and Trends in Learning-based 3D Representations — Survey

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04871) · [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04871)  
**Date:** June 2026 (arXiv preprint, ~2 weeks ago)

**What it is:** A comprehensive survey covering the current landscape of learning-based 3D representations — from classical discrete explicit formats (voxel grids, point clouds, meshes) through continuous implicit fields (NeRF variants, occupancy networks, SDFs) to the latest neural rendering and primitive splatting approaches (3DGS, 2DGS, 4D extensions). The survey maps the trade-offs for 3D reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, shape and motion analysis, recognition, and generation tasks.

**What's new:** With 3DGS having emerged only in 2023, comprehensive surveys of the representation landscape that include splatting methods as first-class citizens alongside NeRF are still relatively rare. This paper provides a unified taxonomy that frames the "representation choice" as a fundamental design decision affecting efficiency, quality, and downstream task capability.

**Why it matters to you:** A useful reference for framing research contributions against the state of the field, identifying gaps in current methods, and justifying representation choices in new work. Particularly valuable when writing papers or proposals that need to contextualise a novel 3D representation approach against the full prior art landscape.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **NeRF and 3DGS are converging, not competing** — Papers like Leveraging NeRF for 3DGS (2606.09034) and prior hybrid methods suggest the field is moving toward pipelines that use NeRF's high-quality geometry as initialization and quality oracle for 3DGS's real-time rendering. Expect this hybridization to become standard practice in production pipelines within 6–12 months.

2. **Appearance modeling is the next Gaussian frontier** — With geometry (density, deformation, scale) now well-optimised, the remaining quality gap in 3DGS is appearance: specular reflections, subsurface scattering, and high-frequency colour variation. The arXiv 2606.10478 (Beyond Spherical Harmonics) and SIGGRAPH-accepted Ref-DGS are the first two shots in what will be a rich research thread.

3. **World models entering 3D** — FR3D (ICML 2026) joining D4RT (CVPR 2026) signals that the world-model paradigm — previously dominated by 2D video (GAIA, UniSim) — is migrating to native 3D latent representations. The combination of geometric consistency and temporal prediction is the precursor to real-time predictive rendering for XR.

4. **Video generation as a 3D data source** — As Grok Imagine Video 1.5 and its competitors commoditise photorealistic video generation at low cost, using generated video as synthetic training data for 3D reconstruction (multi-view synthesis, dynamic scene augmentation) becomes economically viable. Expect dedicated "video → 3D" dataset generation pipelines to emerge in the second half of 2026.

5. **Android XR creates a new deployment target** — The arrival of 5+ Android XR devices in 2026 gives the Android developer ecosystem a unified XR platform for the first time. Research pipelines that can run on-device Gaussian Splatting or lightweight neural rendering on Android XR hardware will have immediate practical relevance.

## 1. MPEG Opens Call for Content for Dynamic Gaussian Splat Coding

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/mpeg-calls-for-dynamic-gaussian-splat-test-material-ahead-of-a-coding-call-for-proposals) · [Ofinno standards readout](https://ofinno.com/standards-readout/gaussian-splat-coding-exploration-advances-as-next-gen-video-coding-timeline-takes-shape/)
**Date:** ~August 4, 2026

**What it is:** MPEG's working groups have formally opened a Call for Content, requesting dynamic I-3DGS (per-frame, INRIA-format 3D Gaussian Splatting) test material ahead of a full Call for Proposals for a Gaussian splat coding standard. Submissions need COLMAP-format intrinsic/extrinsic calibration, prioritize 6DoF dynamic object- and scene-centric captures (people, non-rigid objects, sports/stage performances) at 25–30 fps, and require sequences of at least 5–10 seconds with no scene cuts. Deadline for submission is October 15, 2026.

**What's new:** This is the clearest sign yet that Gaussian Splatting is moving from a research/vendor-specific format toward a standardized, interoperable video codec — the same trajectory MPEG followed for H.264/HEVC and point-cloud compression (V-PCC/G-PCC).

**Why it matters to you:** A standardized dynamic 3DGS codec would directly enable interoperable volumetric video pipelines (capture → compress → stream → render) across vendors — critical for XR telepresence, volumetric sports/entertainment, and any pipeline currently locked into proprietary splat formats.

## 2. MiniMax H3 — Open-Weight Omni-Modal Generation Model

**Source:** [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/01/minimax-releases-minimax-h3-an-omni-modal-video-model-that-generates-15-second-2k-clips-with-native-stereo-audio/) · [MiniMax announcement](https://www.minimax.io/news/minimax-h3-open-source) · [RunPod technical writeup](https://www.runpod.io/blog/minimax-h3-the-open-weight-omni-modal-video-model-and-what-it-takes-to-run-it)
**Date:** Announced July 31, 2026 (API); weights published to Hugging Face August 3, 2026

**What it is:** H3 is MiniMax's omni-modal model that reads text, image, video, and audio as a single unified context and generates video (up to 2K, 15 seconds) with native stereo audio. Independent Artificial Analysis evaluation ranks it first globally in video editing, second in text-to-video, third in image-to-video — positioned against ByteDance's Seedance 2.5 at a fraction of the cost.

**What's new:** Unlike most generative video models that bolt audio on afterward, H3 treats all modalities as one context from the start, and — unusually for a frontier-tier omni-modal model — its weights are openly released under a community license.

**Why it matters to you:** An open-weight, unified multimodal backbone is a candidate building block for synthetic training data (video + audio pairs for scene understanding), and its cross-modal context handling is architecturally relevant to any pipeline fusing video with other sensor streams — including 4D/dynamic scene capture for XR.

## 3. World Labs Acquires SceniX

**Source:** [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/21/world-labs-acquires-scenix-to-bridge-generative-world-models-and-physical-robotics-for-embodied-ai/) · [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/world-labs-acquires-scenix-to-bring-generative-ai-into-physical-robotics-and-embodied-intelligence) · [a16z interview, late Jul 2026](https://a16z.com) (Fei-Fei Li & Yunzhu Li on simulation for robotics)
**Date:** July 21, 2026

**What it is:** Fei-Fei Li's spatial-intelligence company World Labs (creator of the "Marble" text/image-to-3D-world generator, $1.23B raised) acquired robotics-simulation startup SceniX to bridge its generative world models with physical robot training — sidestepping the cost of real-world robot data collection by generating simulated training grounds instead.

**What's new:** This is World Labs' first acquisition and a concrete signal that generative 3D world models (Marble-style scene generation) are being aimed squarely at embodied AI / robotics training, not just content creation or XR.

**Why it matters to you:** If you work on 3D scene generation or reconstruction, this validates a growing use case beyond visualization: generated/reconstructed 3D worlds as simulation environments for training embodied agents — an application area that will likely pull more research investment into geometrically- and physically-consistent scene generation.

## 4. SketchUp Labs Ships Native Gaussian Splatting Extension

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/sketchup-adds-native-gaussian-splatting-support) · [SketchUp Help — Gaussian Splats](https://help.sketchup.com/en/gaussian-splats-extension)
**Date:** July 2026

**What it is:** SketchUp released a Gaussian Splats extension (via Extension Warehouse / SketchUp Labs, Windows-only at launch, requires SketchUp 2024+ Pro tier) that lets users import, view, and directly manipulate splats inside SketchUp. It ships three display modes (Rendering, Modeling — which disables blending to expose individual splats for snapping, and Point Cloud for fast navigation) plus dedicated Move/Rotate/Crop/Scale tools built for splat geometry rather than meshes.

**What's new:** This follows Blender's own design task (opened June 2, 2026) for native splat IO/rendering — mainstream DCC tools are now racing to treat Gaussian splats as a first-class geometry type rather than a plugin curiosity.

**Why it matters to you:** Native splat support in mainstream modeling tools removes a major workflow friction point (round-tripping splats through custom viewers/converters) for anyone integrating captured 3D scenes into design, AEC, or content pipelines.

## 5. Sparse-View Surface Reconstruction via Gaussian Splatting with Normal Priors

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.03765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03765)
**Date:** July 4, 2026

**What it is:** A method for high-fidelity surface reconstruction from sparse-view inputs using 3D Gaussian Splatting, combining high-confidence depth propagation with normal priors to constrain geometry in under-observed regions — a persistent weak point for 3DGS-based surface extraction compared to mesh/SDF methods.

**What's new:** Sparse-view reconstruction remains one of the harder open problems for 3DGS (dense multi-view capture is easy to overfit; sparse capture is not). Normal-guided depth propagation is a relatively lightweight addition that directly targets the geometric consistency gap without a full architecture change.

**Why it matters to you:** Most real-world capture (phone scans, drive-by/fly-by footage) is sparse-view by necessity. Techniques that improve surface fidelity under sparse-view conditions are directly applicable to consumer-scale 3D capture workflows (cf. Scaniverse, SketchUp splats) rather than lab-controlled dense-capture rigs.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Gaussian Splatting is standardizing.** MPEG's Call for Content, plus native support landing in Blender and SketchUp within weeks of each other, signals 3DGS moving from research artifact to an interoperable, tool-agnostic content format — the same maturation path meshes and point clouds went through years ago.

2. **Generated/reconstructed 3D worlds as robot training grounds.** World Labs × SceniX is part of a broader pattern (also visible in NVIDIA's Cosmos world models) of treating 3D scene generation as embodied-AI infrastructure, not just a visualization or XR content pipeline — worth watching if your reconstruction work could double as simulation environments.

3. **Sparse-view remains the frontier for practical capture.** Both the normal-priors GS paper and the consumer capture tools (Scaniverse, SketchUp) point to the same bottleneck: most real-world 3D capture is sparse and casual, and techniques that close the quality gap between sparse and dense capture will have outsized practical impact.

4. **Open-weight multimodal models are catching up to closed frontier models.** MiniMax H3's open release, ranking competitively against ByteDance's closed Seedance 2.5, suggests unified text/image/video/audio backbones may become as commoditized as text-only LLMs — worth tracking as a potential foundation layer for 4D/dynamic-scene pipelines.

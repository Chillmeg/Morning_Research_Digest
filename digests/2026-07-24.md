## 2. "Robust Planar Maps for 3D Vectorization" — SIGGRAPH 2026 Best Paper

**Source:** [ACM SIGGRAPH Blog — Awards](https://blog.siggraph.org/2026/05/siggraph-2026-technical-papers-awards-best-papers-honorable-mentions-and-test-of-time.html/) · [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/siggraph-2026-technical-papers-showcase-the-research-making-visual-computing-faster-more-reliable-and-accessible-302813822.html)  
**Authors:** Robert Fuchs, Keenan Crane — Carnegie Mellon University / Roblox  
**Date:** Award announced May 2026; presented at SIGGRAPH Jul 2026

**What it is:** A geometry-agnostic method for converting arbitrary 3D scenes into clean, scalable 2D vector images (SVG-style) with correct occlusion handling, replacing brittle curve–curve intersection math with more tractable curve–line intersections.

**What's new:** Prior 3D-vectorization tools broke down on the messy, disconnected curve descriptions common in real production files. This method runs orders of magnitude faster and is robust enough to bring automatic 3D vectorization into production pipelines for the first time — Crane notes teams were "still often having to trace out 3D renderings by hand."

**Why it matters to you:** Useful wherever a Gaussian-splat or mesh reconstruction needs to be turned into clean, editable vector line art — technical illustration, CAD-style output from captured scenes, or stylized NPR rendering pipelines downstream of 3D reconstruction.

## 3. NVIDIA DLSS 5 — Neural Rendering Detailed at SIGGRAPH

**Source:** [Guru3D](https://www.guru3d.com/story/nvidia-refines-dlss-5-neural-rendering-with-greater-developer-control-at-siggraph-2026/) · [TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/350916/nvidia-shows-dlss-5-progress-and-technical-details-at-siggraph-2026)  
**Date:** SIGGRAPH 2026 (week of Jul 19)

**What it is:** NVIDIA detailed DLSS 5's modular neural-rendering architecture: three separate models (A/B/C) with different tradeoffs between structural fidelity, global illumination, and texture detail, targeting 4K/60fps with sub-16ms latency on a single GPU. Full release is slated for fall 2026.

**What's new:** Earlier DLSS generations were criticized for overriding artistic intent; DLSS 5 exposes per-object artist controls and separates identity/animation/lighting preservation from the generative enhancement pass, giving developers explicit levers over what the neural renderer is allowed to change.

**Why it matters to you:** The identity-preserving/generative-split design is a pattern directly transferable to neural rendering of Gaussian splats and NeRFs — separating "what must stay consistent" from "what can be hallucinated for detail" is exactly the tension in splat super-resolution and view-extrapolation work.

## 6. VRSplat — Fast and Robust Gaussian Splatting for Virtual Reality

**Source:** [ACM TOG (full text)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3728311) · [arXiv 2505.10144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10144)  
**Date:** Published/presented at SIGGRAPH 2026

**What it is:** VRSplat combines and extends recent 3DGS advances specifically to solve VR's three hard problems: temporal popping during head movement, projection-based floater artifacts, and framerate collapse at high Gaussian counts. It adds an efficient foveated rasterizer handling focus and peripheral regions in a single GPU launch, sustaining 72+ fps.

**What's new:** It's the first systematically evaluated 3DGS method validated for VR specifically via a controlled 25-participant user study, rather than relying on desktop-rendering benchmarks that don't capture stereo/head-motion artifacts.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're deploying splats in a headset (rather than a flat viewer), VRSplat's foveated rasterizer and anti-popping fixes are a direct, benchmarked recipe for hitting VR framerate/comfort requirements that generic 3DGS renderers don't guarantee.

## 9. ATSplat — Compact Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting via Adaptive Token Expansion

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.20417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20417)  
**Authors:** Cho In, Jeonghwan Cho, Mijin Yoo, Gim Hee Lee, Seon Joo Kim  
**Date:** Posted late July 2026

**What it is:** A newly-posted feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting method that uses an adaptive token-expansion strategy to keep the token/Gaussian budget compact while preserving reconstruction quality, joining a fast-growing family of token-based feed-forward splatting approaches (TokenSplat, GlobalSplat, F⁴Splat) that emerged around CVPR 2026.

**What's new:** Where earlier feed-forward splatting work allocates a fixed token budget per view or per pixel, adaptive expansion suggests the model learns to spend Gaussian/token budget where scene complexity actually demands it — the same "spend compute where it matters" idea now standard in efficient attention is being applied to splat allocation.

**Why it matters to you:** Feed-forward (single-pass, no per-scene optimization) splatting is the fastest-moving sub-area in 3DGS right now; if you need near-instant scene reconstruction (AR live capture, robotics), this line of work is the one to track for pretrained, generalizable splat predictors.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Gaussian splatting is now a first-class DCC citizen, not a plugin.** Houdini 22, ComfyUI's native nodes, and (from the prior digest) Nuke/Isaac Sim all shipped native splat support within weeks of each other — the tooling gap between splats and traditional meshes/point clouds has essentially closed in 2026.

2. **MCP is becoming the standard interface for agents inside 3D tools.** NVIDIA's coordinated rollout across Adobe, Blender, SideFX, and Epic suggests agent-driven 3D asset pipelines will increasingly be built against a common protocol rather than per-vendor scripting APIs.

3. **VR-specific splatting has matured past "does it run" to "is it comfortable."** VRSplat's user-study-validated foveated rendering signals the field is now optimizing for perceptual/comfort metrics (popping, floaters) rather than raw fps alone — the right benchmark to hold new VR splatting work to going forward.

4. **Feed-forward splatting is consolidating around token-based scene representations.** ATSplat, TokenSplat, GlobalSplat, and F⁴Splat all converge on tokenized/adaptive-budget Gaussian allocation as the mechanism for fast, generalizable, per-scene-optimization-free reconstruction.

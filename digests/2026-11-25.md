## 1. WildAnySplat: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting in the Wild

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.31023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31023) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31023)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 23, 2026. Authors report acceptance to SIGGRAPH Asia 2026.

**What it is:** A feed-forward (no per-scene optimization) 3DGS model that predicts splats directly from unconstrained, in-the-wild photo collections. It is built to handle transient occluders such as people and cars, and varying appearance such as lighting and exposure, while keeping fast inference.

**What's new:** Earlier feed-forward splatters (pixelSplat/MVSplat/AnySplat lineage, and SplatAnything from Nov 5) assume clean, consistent captures. The "in-the-wild" setting has mostly needed per-scene optimization (e.g. WildGaussians, GS-W). WildAnySplat brings robustness to occlusion and appearance changes into the feed-forward regime.

**Relevance:** This is a direct candidate for instant previews from casual or crowd-sourced captures, and for XR scene capture where the environment can't be controlled. Worth benchmarking against your current pipeline on any tourist-photo or phone-capture data.

## 2. Complementary Geometry in Gaussian Splatting (REVEAL)

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.29236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29236)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 22, 2026

**What it is:** REVEAL improves depth and geometry accuracy at novel viewpoints *without modifying the trained, frozen Gaussian representation*. It works as a complementary geometry module on top of existing splats.

**What's new:** Most geometry-aware GS work (2DGS, PGSR, GOF) changes the primitive or the training loss. REVEAL is post-hoc, so it can be bolted onto splats you have already trained.

**Relevance:** It could be useful for occlusion, physics and collision in XR, or for mesh extraction from existing 3DGS assets, with no retraining. Check whether it needs the original training images.

## 3. Kosmo-GFM: A Production-Ready Geometry Foundation Model

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.16840](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16840) · [Hugging Face (KosmoResearch)](https://huggingface.co/KosmoResearch/Kosmo-GFM) · [AlphaSignal coverage](https://alphasignal.ai/news/kosmo-gfm-rebuilds-3d-scene-capture-from-sparse-photos-26x-faster) · [Radiance Fields entry](https://radiancefields.com/papers/kosmo-gfm-a-production-ready-geometry-foundation-model)
**Date:** November 2026 arXiv. The exact day is unconfirmed, and press coverage circulated this week.

**What it is:** A 1.66B-parameter shared multi-view transformer (GFM) that predicts camera parameters, depth, cross-view correspondences and dynamic 3D points from images or video. It comes with a pose-optimization engine (PoseEngine) and a data engine (KosmoWorld), and it ships variants for camera-conditioned input and 4D. Camera-aware attention supports pinhole, fisheye and equirectangular inputs in one backbone.

**What's new / claims (self-reported):**
- Best AbsRel depth and pose AUC@5° on the SpatialBench Sparse/Medium subsets (SpatialBench is a NeurIPS 2026 benchmark).
- "PoseEngine Flash" runs in 2.62 h vs 69.67 h for the GLUEMAP baseline, which is the ~26× speedup in the headlines.
- Weights are on Hugging Face. The full PoseEngine and GS pipelines are reportedly **not** released, and only standalone inference is public.

**Relevance:** This is a VGGT/D4RT-class backbone with native fisheye and 360° support, which matters for headset and robot cameras. It could replace COLMAP for initializing 3DGS on large or sparse captures. Its 4D variant overlaps with D4RT (CVPR '26 Best Paper).

## 5. Gaussian splats land in production DCC and GIS tools

**Source:** [Radiance Fields: 3ds Max 2027.3](https://radiancefields.com/3ds-max-2027-3-adds-splat-viewport-display-and-4dgs) · [Radiance Fields: DBS_SplatTrain for Nuke](https://radiancefields.com/dbs-splattrain-turns-nuke-camera-tracks-into-gaussian-splats) · [Radiance Fields: ArcGIS Reality Server GA](https://radiancefields.com/esri-makes-arcgis-reality-server-generally-available)
**Date:** Nov 17–24, 2026. Individual article dates were not verifiable.

- **Autodesk 3ds Max 2027.3** shows Gaussian splats in the viewport and plays **4DGS sequences on the timeline**.
- **DBS_SplatTrain 0.1.0** (MIT) is a Nuke 17 node that trains a splat from a tracked shot using Brush and returns it aligned to the track camera.
- **Esri ArcGIS Reality Server** is now GA in ArcGIS Enterprise 12.2, with self-hosted, distributed processing of drone imagery into splats.

**Why it matters:** 3DGS and 4DGS are becoming first-class assets in mainstream modeling, VFX and geospatial software, not just research viewers. Interop formats and compression now affect whether research outputs get adopted. Splat-to-DCC compatibility is worth considering for any tool you release.

## 6. AGSR: Aperture-Guided Super-Resolution for Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.25934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25934)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 19, 2026. Accepted at MMSP 2026 with a Best Paper Award.

**What it is / what's new:** A render-time super-resolution method that contracts projected 2D covariances, treating them like an aperture, to render splats at higher resolution than they were trained at. It needs no retraining.

**Relevance:** It is a cheap way to upsample existing captures for high-PPD XR displays. It is a little outside the 24-hour window, but it is new this week.

## Takeaways for this week

1. **Feed-forward 3DGS is maturing toward real-world capture.** WildAnySplat handles robustness and Kosmo-GFM brings camera-model generality. Optimization-free capture for XR is getting close.
2. **Post-hoc upgrades to frozen splats** are a trend worth tracking: REVEAL adds geometry and AGSR adds resolution, without retraining.
3. **4DGS is entering production tools** (3ds Max timeline playback, UE plugins). Dynamic-scene research now has a deployment path.
4. **Glasses-class XR hits mass market Dec 10.** Compact, streamable representations matter more than ever.

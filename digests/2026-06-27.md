## 1. SparseStreet — Sparse Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Street Scene Simulation

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.03909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03909) · Presented at ICMR '26 (Amsterdam, Jun 16–19, 2026)  
**Authors:** ZijunLi et al. — Peking University  
**Date:** arXiv June 2, 2026

**What it is:** SparseStreet is a plug-and-play compression framework that reduces redundancy in existing street-scene Gaussian Splatting methods. It exploits a key asymmetry: dynamic objects (vehicles, pedestrians) require high-fidelity Gaussian representations for temporal consistency, while static background regions contain substantial structural redundancy. The system is evaluated on Waymo and nuScenes benchmarks and achieves up to **80% reduction in Gaussian primitive count** with a **2× FPS improvement**, while maintaining visual fidelity of dynamic objects.

**What's new:** Prior street-GS methods (DrivingGaussian, StreetSurfGS, EVolSplat4D) focused on reconstruction quality and left storage and render-speed as secondary concerns. SparseStreet directly targets practical deployment constraints — a street scene reconstruction that cannot run in real time on target hardware has no use in simulation pipelines. The framework is architecture-agnostic, designed to drop into any existing street-GS system.

**Why it matters:** For autonomous driving simulation and XR map applications, render speed and memory footprint on vehicle-class hardware are hard deployment constraints. SparseStreet's plug-in compression approach is immediately applicable to any 3DGS-based driving simulation pipeline and could close the gap between research demos and in-car deployment.

## 2. Support-Aware Appearance Distillation for Consistent 3D Gaussian Street Scenes

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.26007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26007)  
**Authors:** Long Cao, Zhongquan Wang, Jie Li, Yuhan Chen, Kefei Qian, Xiangfei Huang, Guofa Li — College of Mechanical and Vehicle Engineering, Chongqing University  
**Date:** arXiv ~June 26, 2026 (very recent)

**What it is:** This paper tackles a core unsolved problem in editable 3D Gaussian street scenes: given sparse or imperfect 2D image edits (e.g. lighting changes, weather synthesis, or object re-colouring applied only to a subset of views), how do you lift those edits into a 3D-consistent Gaussian representation? The proposed method introduces *teacher-relative appearance residual distillation* — a structured distillation pipeline that decomposes appearance residuals between a teacher anchor and the original render into frequency components, estimates per-primitive confidence, and lifts the edits to full 3D consistency.

**What's new:** Existing GS editing methods either require per-view supervision that is expensive to produce or ignore view-consistency entirely. By treating appearance editing as a distillation problem — using the 2D edits as a "teacher" to guide the 3D Gaussians — the method tolerates sparse and imperfect input supervision while producing coherent novel-view outputs.

**Why it matters:** Appearance editing of captured street scenes (for simulation augmentation, training data synthesis, or digital twin customization) is a critical capability for autonomous driving and XR city-scale applications. Lifting 2D artistic edits to 3D-consistent Gaussian scenes without full per-view re-editing is a practical workflow requirement this paper begins to address.

## 3. MILo — Mesh-In-the-Loop Gaussian Splatting for Detailed Surface Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2506.24096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.24096) · [Project Page](https://anttwo.github.io/milo/) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2506.24096v2)  
**Authors:** Antoine Guédon et al.  
**Date:** arXiv 2506.24096; accepted SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 — Journal Track (ACM TOG)

**What it is:** MILo bridges the gap between volumetric and surface representations by making mesh extraction fully differentiable and running it at *every training iteration*. The framework constructs the mesh — including vertex locations and connectivity — directly from the parameters of the 3D Gaussians without any post-processing. By coupling the mesh to the Gaussian optimisation loop, the final reconstruction is both high-fidelity for rendering and geometrically clean as a mesh.

**What's new:** Prior mesh extraction from 3D Gaussians (Gaussian Opacity Fields, RaDe-GS, 2DGS) used expensive post-processing steps (Marching Cubes, depth-fusion) that discarded fine geometric detail or produced extremely dense meshes with millions of redundant vertices. MILo eliminates the post-processing entirely, achieving better quality scores while producing **an order of magnitude fewer mesh vertices** than competing methods.

**Why it matters:** Getting a clean, lightweight mesh out of a Gaussian Splatting reconstruction is essential for downstream tasks: physics simulation, rigging, real-time mobile rendering, and integration with traditional 3D DCC tools (Maya, Blender, USD pipelines). MILo's loop-integrated approach is a practical advance for any pipeline that needs both photorealistic novel-view synthesis and usable geometry simultaneously.

## 4. AA-2DGS — Anti-Aliased 2D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2506.11252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11252) · [HAL](https://hal.science/hal-05477758v1)  
**Authors:** Mae Younes, Adnane Boukhayma  
**Date:** arXiv June 2026; submitted to NeurIPS 2026

**What it is:** 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) offers better geometric consistency than volumetric 3DGS but suffers from severe aliasing artifacts when rendered at scales different from training (e.g., camera zoom, varying focal lengths). AA-2DGS introduces two complementary filters: a *world-space flat smoothing kernel* that limits frequency content of Gaussian primitives based on training-view sampling rate, and an *object-space Mip filter* that performs prefiltering in the local coordinate frame of each splat using the ray-splat intersection mapping.

**What's new:** Prior anti-aliasing work on 3DGS (Mip-Splatting) approximated filtering in 3D but did not generalize cleanly to the surface-oriented 2DGS representation. AA-2DGS derives principled filters specific to the 2DGS geometry, significantly improving rendering quality across multi-scale evaluation benchmarks without degrading geometric accuracy.

**Why it matters:** Multi-scale consistency is a practical requirement for any deployment scenario where camera zoom, level-of-detail switching, or varying capture setups are involved — including XR passthrough, mapping, and scene capture from drone imagery at multiple altitudes. AA-2DGS fills an important gap for production 2DGS deployments.

## 5. TuringViT — Xpeng's Efficient Large Vision Transformer

**Source:** arXiv (June 2026) · Xpeng Foundation Model Team  
**Authors:** Foundation Model Team, Xpeng Inc.  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** TuringViT is a large-scale vision transformer optimised for efficiency and high-resolution inputs. It uses a *linear-attention-dominant architecture* that reduces the quadratic cost of standard self-attention, a sophisticated data curation pipeline that achieves competitive performance with substantially less pre-training data, and native dynamic-resolution training that avoids the usual resolution-resizing penalties. It achieves competitive performance on vision and multimodal benchmarks with improved inference efficiency at high resolutions.

**What's new:** Most recent large ViTs (DINOv3, SigLIP-2, EVA-CLIP) trade off computational cost for scale. TuringViT targets the efficiency frontier — making a competitive large vision backbone practical for deployment on hardware with constrained compute, such as automotive SoCs or lightweight XR devices. The Xpeng origin is notable: this is the vision backbone underlying their VLA 2.0 full self-driving system.

**Why it matters:** Efficient large vision backbones are a bottleneck for deploying state-of-the-art perception in embedded or edge XR scenarios. TuringViT's linear-attention approach and dynamic-resolution handling are directly applicable to real-time passthrough scene understanding and to serving as a perceptual prior in 3D reconstruction pipelines where high-resolution feature extraction is required.

## 6. SketchUp Adds Native Gaussian Splatting Support

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/sketchup-adds-native-gaussian-splatting-support) · [SketchUp Help](https://help.sketchup.com/en/gaussian-splats-extension) · [SketchUp Community](https://forums.sketchup.com/t/gaussian-splats-have-arrived-in-sketchup-labs/347799)  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** Trimble has shipped a native Gaussian Splats extension for SketchUp (available in SketchUp Labs, the experimental features channel). It supports import and interactive viewing of `.ply`, `.splat`, `.spz`, `.sklat`, and `.lcc` files directly inside SketchUp models. The extension introduces `.sklat` (SketchUp Splat), a proprietary edit-aware format that preserves user modifications. A five-step configuration wizard handles crop, orientation alignment, axis snapping, real-world scaling, and scene positioning.

**What's new:** Until now, Gaussian Splats in AEC/design tools were third-party plugins built on browser-based or external renderers, not native first-party integrations. SketchUp is one of the most widely deployed DCC tools in architecture, construction, and engineering; first-party splat support normalizes Gaussian Splatting as a standard CAD workflow input rather than a research curiosity.

**Why it matters:** SketchUp native support signals that Gaussian Splatting has crossed the AEC mainstream adoption threshold. For XR construction site documentation, as-built model capture, and spatial computing applications in built environments, this lowers the barrier for practitioners to integrate splat-based captures into their existing workflows.

## 7. Cesium Ion — Mesh-Free Gaussian Splatting Pipeline

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/) (June 2026 update)  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** Cesium Ion's June 2026 release removes the mandatory mesh generation step from its 3D reconstruction pipelines. Users can now run point cloud-only or pure Gaussian Splatting pipelines, streaming them directly through Cesium's tile-streaming infrastructure without needing an intermediate polygon mesh.

**What's new:** Previously, Cesium's pipeline required mesh reconstruction as an intermediate step, adding processing time and introducing the artifacts typical of photogrammetry-to-mesh conversion. The new pipeline allows raw splats to be streamed natively through CesiumJS and Cesium for Omniverse, aligning with how Khronos Group is standardising Gaussian splat streaming in glTF/3D Tiles.

**Why it matters:** Cesium is infrastructure for geospatial 3D applications — its data pipeline is used for city-scale digital twins, geographic data visualization, and simulation. A mesh-free GS pipeline through Cesium means large-scale outdoor splat reconstructions can be streamed to XR and browser clients using existing 3D Tiles infrastructure, without the photogrammetry mesh quality ceiling.

## 8. LichtFeld Studio v0.5.3 — Vulkan Renderer and 8K Gaussian Training

**Source:** [GitHub — MrNeRF/LichtFeld-Studio](https://github.com/MrNeRF/LichtFeld-Studio) · [lichtfeld.io](https://lichtfeld.io/)  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** LichtFeld Studio v0.5.3 is a significant release of the all-in-one desktop application for 3DGS workflows (training, inspection, editing, export). The update retires the CUDA-based renderer entirely, replacing it with a native **Vulkan viewer and rasterizer** for cross-platform GPU portability. New features include an Asset Manager for scene organisation, RAD format export, and support for **8K resolution Gaussian splatting training**.

**What's new:** CUDA-only rendering locked LichtFeld to NVIDIA GPUs. The Vulkan backend enables the same renderer to run on AMD, Intel Arc, and Apple Silicon (via MoltenVK), substantially broadening the hardware footprint. The 8K training capability is directly relevant to high-fidelity heritage scanning, medical imaging, and detailed product visualization workflows.

**Why it matters:** A cross-platform Vulkan renderer for 3DGS training and inference is an enabling step for deploying Gaussian Splatting pipelines on a wider range of hardware — including the Apple Silicon devices common in creative and XR development workflows. 8K GS training is relevant for any application requiring sub-millimeter detail in large capture areas.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **GS compression becoming a first-class concern** — SparseStreet and MEGS² (Memory-Efficient GS via Spherical Gaussians) signal that the community is shifting attention from reconstruction quality to deployment practicality: storage budget, render throughput, and hardware portability. Expect more compression-focused papers at ECCV and NeurIPS 2026.

2. **3D Gaussian street scenes as simulation infrastructure** — Three recent papers (SparseStreet, Support-Aware Appearance Distillation, EVolSplat4D) target autonomous driving simulation specifically. 3DGS is becoming the representation of choice for synthetic data generation in AV pipelines, which will drive demand for editing, lighting, and weather synthesis capabilities.

3. **Platform tooling catches up to research** — SketchUp Labs, Cesium Ion, LichtFeld Studio, and Apple Maps all shipped Gaussian Splatting features this month. The tooling gap between research papers and practitioner workflows is closing faster than in any prior representation shift (NeRF → adoption was ~3 years; 3DGS → adoption is under 2 years).

4. **Physical AI data collection as an XR-adjacent category** — Xvisio's SeerFusion One exemplifies a new hardware category: ego-centric capture devices for embodied AI training data. This is orthogonal to XR displays but relies on the same spatial sensing stack (VSLAM, 6DOF tracking, wide-FOV optics), and represents a new funding/market rationale for continued XR sensor hardware development.

5. **Anti-aliasing and rendering quality as maturation signals** — Papers like AA-2DGS and AAA-Gaussians address rendering artifacts that only matter when a technology is being deployed seriously. The community's focus on aliasing correction, LOD, and multi-scale rendering suggests 3DGS is entering a quality-polish phase similar to the maturation of real-time rasterization in the early 2010s.

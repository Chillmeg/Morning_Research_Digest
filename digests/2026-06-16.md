## 4. Apple Velox — Learning Representations of 4D Geometry and Appearance

**Source:** [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/velox) · [arXiv 2605.04527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04527) · [Project Page](https://apple.github.io/ml-velox/) · [Apple at CVPR 2026](https://machinelearning.apple.com/updates/apple-at-cvpr-2026)  
**Authors:** Anagh Malik, Dorian Chan, Xiaoming Zhao, David B. Lindell, Oncel Tuzel, Jen-Hao Rick Chang — Apple  
**Date:** arXiv May 2026; presented at CVPR 2026, June 6, 2026

**What it is:** Velox is a framework for learning compact latent representations of 4D (3D + time) objects that are simultaneously descriptive (faithfully capturing geometry and appearance), compressive (suitable for downstream efficiency), and accessible (requiring minimal input). An encoder compresses spatiotemporal color point clouds into *dynamic shape tokens*, supervised by two complementary decoders: a **4D surface decoder** modeling time-varying surface distributions, and a **Gaussian decoder** mapping tokens to 3D Gaussians to capture appearance. Evaluated on video-to-4D generation, 3D tracking, and cloth simulation via image-to-4D generation.

**What's new:** Velox is notable for treating 4D scene understanding as a *representation learning problem* — encoding dynamic object geometry and appearance into a single compact token space that can be decoded into Gaussian splats on demand. This is distinct from per-scene optimization (NeRF, 3DGS) or feed-forward reconstruction (D4RT) — it learns a latent space of 4D objects that can be sampled, interpolated, and composed. The Gaussian decoder is a bridge between the compact token representation and the differentiable rendering pipeline.

**Why it matters to you:** Velox directly addresses the gap between efficient 4D representation and real-time rendering: by encoding dynamics into compact tokens that decode to Gaussians, it offers a path toward streaming 4D content (dynamic avatars, animated objects) in XR contexts where both bandwidth and compute are constrained. The connection to cloth simulation suggests applications in real-time avatar physics and garment rendering.

## 5. Survey: "Recent Advances and Trends in Learning-based 3D Representations"

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04871) · [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04871)  
**Date:** June 2026 (arXiv preprint)

**What it is:** A comprehensive survey of the full landscape of 3D representation methods, organized from discrete explicit formats (meshes, point clouds, voxel grids) through continuous implicit fields (NeRF, signed distance functions) to primitive splatting methods (3DGS and variants). For each family the paper presents the general formulation, key variants, known limitations, major applications (games, AR/VR, autonomous driving, robot navigation, medical imaging), and open research challenges. Includes coverage of both task-specific and general-purpose 3D representations.

**What's new:** This is among the first surveys to treat 3DGS as a mature peer of NeRF rather than an emerging challenger — the field has moved fast enough that splatting methods now warrant their own survey sections alongside implicit neural representations. The timing (mid-2026) captures the post-CVPR landscape including FastGS, D4RT, and the broader ecosystem of acceleration and generalisation work.

**Why it matters to you:** A well-structured field survey is directly useful for (1) situating new work within the broader representation landscape, (2) identifying underexplored gaps (e.g., representations that are both compact and editable), and (3) building related-work sections for new papers. Given that this covers the June 2026 state of the art, it's the most current reference document for the entire space.

## 7. Emerging Trend: Smart Glasses Split into Three Market Tiers

**Source:** [Next Reality](https://virtual.reality.news/news/awe-2026-smart-glasses-split-into-three-tiers-as-privacy-concerns-grow/) · [AWE 2026 exhibitors](https://www.awexr.com/usa-2026) · [Tom's Guide AWE preview](https://www.tomsguide.com/computing/vr-ar/im-going-to-awe-2026-and-heres-3-things-im-hoping-to-see-from-snap-specs-to-a-new-snapdragon-chip)  
**Date:** Analysis from AWE 2026 (June 15–18)

**What it is:** The smart glasses market visible at AWE 2026 has fragmented into three distinct hardware/price tiers that reflect different design philosophies and rendering capability assumptions: **(1) Display-first AR glasses** (Snap Specs, XREAL Aura) at $449–$2,500, targeting immersive full-AR with spatial anchoring; **(2) AI-assisted lifestyle glasses** (l'Atitude 52°N Berlin at $449, Meta Ray-Bans) with cameras, translation, and offline AI but no display; **(3) High-performance enterprise/military XR** (Microsoft HoloLens successors, Anduril HMD) with high-fidelity passthrough and specialized sensing. Privacy concerns are also increasingly shaping industrial design — camera placement, indicator LEDs, and consent flows are now major product differentiators.

**What's new:** The trifurcation is new relative to 2024–2025, when the market was dominated by a single "smart glasses" category. The emergence of a distinct mid-tier AI-only segment (strong AI, no display) confirms that the display remains the hardest hardware problem in wearables. Privacy regulation pressure (EU AI Act, US state laws) is forcing explicit design choices about camera visibility.

**Why it matters to you:** Each tier has different requirements for onboard 3D scene understanding: display-based AR (Snap Specs) needs real-time spatial anchoring and rendering; AI lifestyle glasses need passive scene classification without display overhead; enterprise XR needs high-fidelity depth sensing and reconstruction. Understanding which tier a deployment target belongs to is essential for sizing the 3D reconstruction pipeline — particularly whether Gaussian splatting needs to run on-device or can be offloaded to cloud.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **AWE as the deployment inflection point** — The density of consumer AR hardware announcements at AWE 2026 (Snap Specs, Project Aura, Luckey HMD) suggests fall 2026 is the moment when multiple consumer AR headsets simultaneously hit the market. This compresses the timeline for getting research-grade 3D reconstruction onto shipping hardware.

2. **Standalone spatial mapping becomes the key differentiator** — Both Snap (via Illumix) and Apple (via Maps/Gaussian splatting) are investing heavily in persistent, environment-scale spatial maps. The core technical problem is identical to 3DGS-based scene reconstruction: capture, compress, anchor, and stream 3D environment representations to lightweight devices.

3. **4D token representations bridging capture and rendering** — Apple Velox and D4RT (covered June 12) both point toward a paradigm where video → compact 4D tokens → on-demand geometry/appearance is the canonical pipeline, replacing per-scene optimization. Expect this framing to dominate 2026 H2 papers.

4. **Survey moment for 3D representations** — The arXiv 2606.04871 survey signals that the community now considers 3DGS a mature technique, not a nascent one. This typically precedes a period of consolidation and specialisation (task-specific variants, compression, editing) rather than further architectural exploration.

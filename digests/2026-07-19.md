## 2. "Robust Planar Maps for 3D Vectorization" — SIGGRAPH 2026 Best Paper

**Source:** [ACM SIGGRAPH Blog — 2026 Technical Papers Awards](https://blog.siggraph.org/2026/05/siggraph-2026-technical-papers-awards-best-papers-honorable-mentions-and-test-of-time.html/)
**Authors:** Robert Fuchs, Keenan Crane — Carnegie Mellon University
**Date:** Awarded May 2026; presented at SIGGRAPH this week

**What it is:** A geometry-agnostic method for converting 3D scenes into clean, scalable 2D vector images with correct occlusion handling. It replaces numerically brittle curve-curve intersection tests with more tractable curve-line intersections, letting it handle the messy, disconnected curve data common in real-world files.

**What's new:** 3D-to-vector conversion has long been unreliable at production scale because curve-curve intersection math breaks down on imperfect input. This method runs orders of magnitude faster than prior techniques while being robust to the kind of degenerate geometry that ships in real assets.

**Why it matters to you:** Anyone converting captured or generated 3D content (including Gaussian splat reconstructions) into clean vector line-art or technical illustrations now has a production-viable path — useful for CAD-from-scan pipelines, NPR rendering, and asset documentation.

## 3. Gaussian Point Splatting — Now in ACM Transactions on Graphics

**Source:** [Project page](https://momentsingraphics.de/Siggraph2026.html) · [ACM ToG](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3811272) · [GitHub](https://jorisar.nl/gaussian_point_splatting/)
**Authors:** Joris Rijsdijk, Christoph Peters, Michael Weinmann, Ricardo Marroquim
**Date:** Presented at SIGGRAPH, July 20, 2026

**What it is:** A stochastic Gaussian-splat renderer that samples pixel-sized opaque points from each Gaussian and splats them via 64-bit atomics with stochastic transparency — eliminating both sorting and tile-based rasterization. It renders 425M Gaussians interactively on a single RTX 4070 Ti SUPER.

**What's new:** Every prior real-time 3DGS renderer needed sorted, tile-based rasterization, which bottlenecks on memory and creates GPU load imbalance. This paper formalizes how to distribute per-Gaussian point counts so the stochastic render stays faithful to the original splat — removing the sorting bottleneck entirely. It's now a peer-reviewed ACM ToG publication, not just a preprint.

**Why it matters to you:** This lifts the practical ceiling on interactive splat scale for city-scale captures, large outdoor scenes, and dense indoor scans — directly relevant to any XR pipeline (mapping, telepresence) that currently hits a Gaussian-count wall.

## 4. WildSplat — Feedforward 3DGS from Unposed In-the-Wild Photos

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.05347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05347)
**Date:** July 2026

**What it is:** The first feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that supports appearance-conditioned novel-view synthesis directly from unposed, in-the-wild image collections — no camera calibration or per-scene optimization required.

**What's new:** Most feedforward 3DGS methods still assume clean, posed, single-illumination capture. WildSplat targets the messier reality of internet photo collections (varying lighting, transient occluders, unknown poses), extending feedforward splatting from curated datasets toward casual, crowd-sourced capture.

**Why it matters to you:** If your reconstruction pipeline needs to work on real user-submitted photos rather than controlled capture rigs, this closes a meaningful gap between lab benchmarks and deployable "photos-to-3D" products.

## 5. A Wave of 3DGS Compression and Efficiency Papers

**Source:** [SpeedyGS — arXiv 2607.12656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12656) · [AsySplat — arXiv 2607.10995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10995) · [GPU texture-coded SH compression — arXiv 2607.14513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14513) · [Provable Pruning via Coresets — arXiv 2607.02721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02721)
**Date:** July 2026 (arXiv)

**What it is:** Four independent July papers attacking 3DGS storage and compute cost from different angles: SpeedyGS separates structural formation from statistical coding for content-aware compression; AsySplat targets asymmetric compute allocation for long-sequence (video) scene modeling; the GPU texture-coding paper repurposes GPU-native texture compression formats to shrink spherical-harmonic color coefficients; and the coreset paper gives pruning a provable-guarantee foundation instead of heuristic thresholds.

**What's new:** Compression and pruning work is maturing from ad hoc heuristics toward principled, GPU-native, and provably-bounded methods — a sign the field is optimizing for deployment constraints (storage, streaming bandwidth, mobile decode) rather than just training speed.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're shipping splats to constrained targets (mobile XR, web viewers, wireless streaming), these are the papers to watch for drop-in decoders — the GPU texture-coding approach in particular could plug directly into existing GPU compressed-texture pipelines with minimal engineering cost.

1. **SIGGRAPH week as the annual forcing function.** A large share of this week's 3DGS/XR news is timed to the conference — watch for the volume of vendor tool releases (Houdini, D5 Render, XGRIDS, etc.) and paper presentations to keep accelerating through July 23.

4. **XR hardware market is bifurcating.** Android XR's open, glasses-first, multi-vendor push is gaining real sales traction while Apple's premium standalone Vision Pro strategy continues to lose share — a structural signal for where to prioritize rendering/streaming engineering effort.

5. **"World model" is converging on 3D simulation.** ByteDance joining World Labs, Google, and others in treating persistent, simulate-able 3D generation as the core of "world modeling" suggests this will be one of the best-funded research directions in generative 3D over the coming year.

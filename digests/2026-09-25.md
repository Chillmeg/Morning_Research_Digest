## 1. Ego-Exo4D Human Meshes Dataset (Ego-Exo4D-HM)

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.30187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30187) · [Project page](https://abhiram824.github.io/egoexo4d_human_meshes/)
**Date:** September 24, 2026

**What it is:** A large-scale dataset of dense 4D human motion reconstructions built on top of Ego-Exo4D's synchronized egocentric + multi-view exocentric video captures. The authors adapt state-of-the-art 3D human pose/mesh reconstruction methods to exploit Ego-Exo4D's multi-camera synchronization and release the resulting SMPL-H motion sequences, since the original dataset only ships sparse 3D pose annotations.

**What's new:** Ego-Exo4D is one of the largest ego+exo video corpora available for skill learning and embodied AI, but it lacked dense human-body ground truth. Ego-Exo4D-HM fills that gap at scale, turning it into a usable resource for dense 4D human motion research.

**Why it matters:** Directly useful for training/evaluating avatar capture, motion-transfer, and human-centric 4D reconstruction models — relevant to XR telepresence and any pipeline that needs ego-exo-consistent body geometry (e.g., initializing Gaussian avatar rigs or animating XR avatars from first-person video).

## 2. COSA-GS — Towards Practical Compression of 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.30245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30245) · [GitHub](https://github.com/pengpeng-yu/COSA-GS)
**Authors:** Pengpeng Yu et al.
**Date:** September 24, 2026

**What it is:** A 3DGS compression method that builds attribute context without spatial aggregation over the irregular Gaussian point cloud, instead using anchor-wise causal factorization: a compact learnable anchor latent fused with geometry context derived purely from each anchor's coordinates. The context model is a simple stack of linear layers + activations, trained with rate-distortion optimization, adaptive pruning, and quantization-aware integer inference.

**What's new:** Prior context-modeling compressors for 3DGS rely on spatial aggregation over irregular 3D structures, which is slow to train/decode and prone to floating-point inconsistencies across hardware (causing entropy-decode failures). COSA-GS gets state-of-the-art compression while guaranteeing bit-exact, cross-platform decode consistency — a real deployment blocker that prior SOTA methods largely ignored.

**Why it matters:** Bit-exact cross-platform decoding is exactly what's needed to ship compressed splats to heterogeneous XR/mobile hardware (a real constraint for streaming splats to headsets or phones, as opposed to research benchmarks run on a single GPU).

## 3. GaussPDE — Graph-Based PDE-Driven Rendering for 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.27264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27264)
**Date:** September 23, 2026

**What it is:** A framework for injecting physically structured PDE dynamics (e.g., diffusion-like evolution) directly onto a pretrained, static 3DGS scene — with no mesh extraction, voxelization, or retraining. It builds an "active Gaussian graph" using covariance-aware distances plus opacity/appearance/boundary-aware conductance, then evolves the scene via a mass-weighted graph Laplacian PDE directly over the Gaussian primitives, with camera-aware regularization during reconstruction to suppress floaters that would otherwise destabilize the graph topology.

**What's new:** Most attempts to simulate physics on 3DGS scenes go through an intermediate mesh or grid. GaussPDE runs the PDE evolution natively on the Gaussians, producing stable, spatially coherent dynamic visualizations with less cross-boundary leakage than baselines.

**Why it matters:** Enables physically plausible dynamic effects (fluid-like diffusion, deformation) directly on captured static 3DGS scenes — useful for adding interactivity/simulation to real-world scans in XR without a costly mesh-extraction step.

## 5. Meta Connect 2026 — Hologram Ships (Codec Avatars → Product)

**Source:** [Meta blog: Everything We Announced at Connect 2026](https://www.meta.com/blog/meta-connect-2026-everything-we-announced/) · [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/meta-hologram-realistic-avatars-meta-vr-glasses-connect-2026/) · [Mixed News on Gaussian-splat Codec Avatars](https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-codec-avatars-gaussian-splatting/)
**Date:** Announced Sep 17–23, 2026; rolling out now

**What it is:** Meta's "Hologram" feature — a photorealistic, real-time digital representation of a person generated from a single phone self-capture — is moving from research demo to shipping product, beginning in Early Access on WhatsApp for Meta Ray-Ban Display users, with full support planned for Meta VR Glasses next spring. Callers on flat-screen devices (WhatsApp, Zoom) will also be able to see the hologram, with spatial audio for viewers in VR.

**What's new:** This is the productization of Meta's Codec Avatars research line, which recently moved from neural volumetric rendering to Gaussian-splat-based relightable avatars (Relightable Gaussian Codec Avatars) for more realistic, efficient real-time relighting of fine details like hair and pores. Going from a lab capture rig to a single phone scan is the key practical leap.

**Why it matters:** A concrete, large-scale consumer deployment of Gaussian-splatting-based avatar rendering — validates single-shot/few-shot avatar capture as production-ready and sets a reference bar for real-time telepresence quality and latency in consumer XR.

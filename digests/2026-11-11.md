## 1. Blender 5.3 — Native Gaussian Splatting Goes Mainstream

**Source:** [Blender 5.3 release page](https://www.blender.org/releases/5-3/) · [Release notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.3/) · [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Blender-5.3-Released) · [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/11/11/blender-5-3-splat-happens-and-everything-else-gets-better/)
**Date:** Released November 10, 2026

**What it is:** Blender 5.3 is the first stable Blender release that imports and renders 3D Gaussian splats natively. It reads PLY, SPZ and glTF splat files, renders them in Workbench, EEVEE and Cycles, and works with Geometry Nodes. The release also adds a DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction denoiser and makes Vulkan the default backend on Windows and Linux.

**What's new:** Until now, splats in Blender needed third-party add-ons such as the KIRI Engine add-on. The Sept 16 merge has now shipped in a stable release, but with limits. Splats come in as point clouds, not meshes. Coverage says export is not supported yet. The developers also say splat rendering performance is still being improved.

**Why it matters to you:** Blender is now a free, standard tool for inspecting 3DGS outputs. You can composite them with mesh assets and edit them procedurally with Geometry Nodes. That makes it useful for checking reconstructions, laying out mixed mesh-and-splat scenes for XR, and making figures for papers. Without export, though, Blender can't be the last step of your pipeline yet.

**Date:** Nov 9–10, 2026

**What it is:** Ember is the smallest and cheapest model in Meta's proprietary Muse family. It's a reasoning model with image input and tool calling, and secondary sources list a 1M-token context and about $0.10 / $0.50 per million input/output tokens. Meta positions it as the model that carries out agent sub-tasks, while Muse Wonder does the planning.

**Why it matters to you:** It's a cheap option for high-volume vision-language jobs like dataset captioning, filtering captures, or tagging assets in 3D pipelines. Since it's closed, you can't fine-tune it or use it as a backbone.

## 4. SplatAnything — Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-Forward 3DGS

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08264) (from the search index, not verified directly) · [AI Weekly: code release](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/splatanything-releases-inference-code-with-gaussian-spacing-fix) · [Radiance Fields paper page](https://radiancefields.com/papers/splatanything-gaussian-spacing-normalization-for-feed-forward-3dgs-across-arbitrary-scenes)
**Authors:** Hao Yu, Tengfei Wang et al.
**Date:** ~Nov 5, 2026; inference code on GitHub and weights on Hugging Face shortly after

**What it is:** Feed-forward 3DGS models such as AnySplat, pixelSplat and MVSplat predict Gaussian parameters in a space normalized by one global radius-style statistic. That works poorly when scene scale or camera layout changes. GSNorm instead normalizes by a robust statistic of the spacing between neighboring Gaussians. This gives the network a steadier reference, and the authors report state-of-the-art results, with the biggest gains when scene scale or viewpoint shifts.

**What's new:** It's a simple change to the normalization rather than a new architecture. It goes after a known reason feed-forward splatting fails on scenes unlike its training data, such as moving from indoor to outdoor or from object scale to room scale.

**Why it matters to you:** If you use or train feed-forward reconstructors for casual captures, GSNorm could be a cheap way to improve robustness to scale. It also fits instant-preview XR capture well. Careful: a separate NeurIPS 2026 paper, "Geometry Is All You Predict," also uses the name SplatAnything. Check the author list before you cite.

## 5. Chaos Corona 16 — Splats Receive Scene Lighting

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/corona-16-lets-gaussian-splats-receive-scene-lighting) · [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=179990)
**Date:** Nov 4, 2026

**What it is:** Corona 16 for 3ds Max and Cinema 4D lets imported Gaussian splats be lit by the rest of the scene. Before this, their baked-in capture lighting was fixed. It also adds clipping masks for splats, SPZ support, and rendering of the new native 3ds Max splats.

**What's new:** Corona 14 added splat import and Corona 15 let splats take part in global illumination. Version 16 completes that progression with actual relighting. V-Ray, Vantage and SideFX Labs have also shipped splat-relighting tools recently, so commercial renderers are moving toward relightable splats.

**Why it matters to you:** Relighting is the main thing stopping captured splats from being used directly in production and AR compositing. The heuristics commercial tools use set a practical baseline for research on relightable and inverse-rendered 3DGS. They're worth comparing against academic delighting methods.

## 7. SpAItial Echo-3 — iPhone Scans into Metric-Scale Splat Worlds (context)

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/spaitial-echo-3-turns-iphone-scans-into-3d-worlds) · [RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/spaitial-echo-3-iphone-3d-world-capture)
**Date:** Oct 27, 2026

**What it is:** Echo-3 takes a few minutes of iPhone walk-around capture and builds a metric-scale Gaussian-splat scene, using a generative prior to fill surfaces the scan missed. It handles multi-floor spaces and exports SPZ, SOG, meshes and point clouds. It's available through a subscription app and an enterprise API.

**What's new:** It combines a generative world model with metric-accurate capture, rather than either producing purely generated worlds or limiting output to what was observed. SpAItial hasn't published benchmarks.

**Why it matters to you:** It's a strong commercial reference point for completing sparse captures with metric accuracy, which is useful for digital twins and room-scale XR content. Without published metrics, treat the quality claims as unproven until you test it.

## Trend Watch

1. **Gaussian splats are now a standard 3D format.** Blender, Corona, 3ds Max, Rhino (GSRhino beta) and, soon, QGIS all handle them natively. Relighting and export are the remaining gaps, and both are good research targets.
2. **Feed-forward reconstruction is shifting from accuracy to robustness.** Work like SplatAnything targets failures across scales and setups rather than benchmark PSNR.
3. **XR glasses ship before XR displays.** The first Android XR products to reach consumers are audio- and AI-first glasses, and display glasses follow.

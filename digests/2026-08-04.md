## 1. InfiniSplat — Implicit Gaussian Decoding for Large-Baseline Monocular View Synthesis

**Source:** [arXiv 2608.02437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02437) · [Project page](https://zju3dv.github.io/InfiniSplat/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/zju3dv/InfiniSplat)
**Authors:** Jiawei Wang et al. — Zhejiang University 3D Vision Group (ZJU3DV)
**Date:** Submitted August 3, 2026; accepted to SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 (Journal Track)

**What it is:** InfiniSplat is a feed-forward, single-image 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that predicts a full renderable 3D scene from one photo, without multi-view capture or per-scene optimization. Instead of anchoring Gaussians to a fixed pixel grid, it places 2D "supports" according to depth-induced local surface structure, then uses a query-conditioned implicit decoder to predict Gaussian attributes at those supports.

**What's new:** Prior single-image 3DGS methods are pixel-aligned — Gaussians spawn from image-grid locations and are only weakly coupled to the actual scene surface, so they fall apart under large viewpoint changes. InfiniSplat's surface-aligned representation instead tracks the underlying geometry, reporting +1.919 PSNR and +0.048 SSIM over the prior state of the art (SHARP) on large-baseline novel-view synthesis.

**Why it matters to you:** This directly targets the "one photo in, walk-around 3D scene out" problem that limits casual capture pipelines for XR and 3D content creation. A surface-aligned representation is also a more natural bridge to downstream mesh extraction or physics-based editing than pixel-aligned splats.

## 2. WorldExam — Benchmarking World Models from Apparent Appearance to Inherent Reactivity

**Source:** [arXiv 2608.02603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02603) · [Project page](https://worldexam.github.io/)
**Authors:** Yuxue Yang, Shuyao Shang, Jiahe Wang, Zitong Zhou, Liang Tan, Junhan Zeng, Ruizhi Li, Junyan Li, Yu Liu, Xiao Yang, Yong Li, Jun Zhu, Hongsheng Li, Tieniu Tan, Lue Fan, Zhaoxiang Zhang
**Date:** Submitted August 3, 2026

**What it is:** A hierarchical diagnostic benchmark for controllable video-generation "world models," spanning four levels — Visual Quality, Control Adherence, Spatial Consistency, and World Reactivity — across 1,474 cases and eight tasks, covering camera-, action-, and language-driven model paradigms.

**What's new:** Existing world-model benchmarks mostly check whether a requested action visibly happened; WorldExam is the first to systematically probe whether a model can infer *unstated* consequences from scene state (e.g., objects reacting to forces not explicitly described) — a much stronger test of implicit 3D/physical understanding.

**Why it matters to you:** As world models (Genie 3, Marble, and similar) increasingly overlap with 3D scene generation and XR content pipelines, "Spatial Consistency" and "World Reactivity" are exactly the properties you'd want to validate before trusting one of these models as a scene-generation backend.

## 3. GraphiXS — A Graphical Model for 4D Gaussian Splatting Under Uncertainty

**Source:** [GitHub](https://github.com/realcrane/Graphical-X-Splatting) · SIGGRAPH 2026 Technical Papers
**Authors:** Doğa Yılmaz, Jialin Zhu, Deshan Gong, He Wang
**Date:** Presented at SIGGRAPH 2026 (July 19–23, 2026)

**What it is:** GraphiXS is a probabilistic framework that explicitly models data uncertainty in 4D Gaussian Splatting, handling missing frames and sparse spatial/temporal data through a graphical-model formulation rather than treating gaps as clean interpolation targets.

**What's new:** Most 4D GS pipelines assume reasonably complete multi-view video; GraphiXS instead treats sparsity and missing observations as first-class uncertainty to be modeled, which is closer to what real-world capture rigs actually deliver.

**Why it matters to you:** Real capture setups (few cameras, dropped frames, occlusion) rarely match the dense-multiview assumptions baked into most dynamic-scene splatting papers — this is directly applicable if you're reconstructing dynamic scenes from imperfect real-world rigs rather than curated benchmark datasets.

## 4. Structure-Aware Densification for Faster 3DGS Convergence

**Source:** SIGGRAPH 2026 Technical Papers program
**Authors:** Linjie Lyu, Ayush Tewari, et al.
**Date:** Presented at SIGGRAPH 2026 (July 19–23, 2026)

**What it is:** A densification strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting training that uses scene-structure cues to decide where and how to split/clone Gaussians, aiming for faster convergence than heuristic gradient-based densification.

**What's new:** Adds to the same optimization-efficiency thread as FastGS and Faster-GS (covered in our June 12 digest), reinforcing that training-time efficiency is now a major, actively contested research axis for 3DGS rather than a solved problem.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're already evaluating FastGS/Faster-GS for faster iteration, this is another data point worth comparing — structure-aware densification could compose with or outperform gradient-magnitude-based approaches depending on your scene types.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Surface-aligned representations are replacing pixel-aligned ones** in feed-forward 3DGS (InfiniSplat), following the same pattern that pushed 2D Gaussian Splatting to replace ellipsoids for surface reconstruction — expect this to keep spreading to other feed-forward splatting variants.
2. **Uncertainty-aware modeling for dynamic/4D capture** (GraphiXS) is moving 4D Gaussian Splatting away from "assume clean dense multi-view video" toward robustness for real, imperfect capture rigs.
3. **World-model evaluation is catching up to world-model capability.** As Genie 3, Marble, and similar systems mature, benchmarks like WorldExam signal the field is starting to demand rigorous spatial/physical consistency checks rather than just visual quality.
4. **3DGS training efficiency remains a crowded, active subfield** — structure-aware densification is at least the third distinct efficiency approach (after FastGS and Faster-GS) to appear in under two months.

## 1. Topo-4DGS & TopoEgo — Topology-Aware 4D Gaussian Splatting for Egocentric Video

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.16012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16012) · **Date:** submitted Nov 11, 2026

**What it is:** This paper tackles 4D Gaussian splatting from monocular egocentric (head-worn) video in scenes whose *topology* changes, for example when something is cut, torn, or opened. It introduces **TopoEgo**, described as the first benchmark for topological changes in egocentric video, and **Topo-4DGS**, a reconstruction method built to handle them.

**What's new:** Most dynamic-GS methods deform a fixed set of Gaussians, so they break when objects split or open. This work makes topology change an explicit target and gives it its own benchmark.

**Why it matters:** Egocentric capture is the native input for XR glasses. Reconstructing hand–object manipulation (cooking, assembly) is a core problem for AR task guidance and for digital twins captured on-device. TopoEgo also gives you a new evaluation set for dynamic-GS work.

## 2. MatrixSplat — Matrix-Parallel, Feature-Scalable 3DGS Rendering on GPUs

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.15153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15153) · **Date:** submitted Nov 11, 2026 · listed as accepted to **NeurIPS 2026**

**What it is:** This is a GPU rendering framework that recasts 3DGS feature accumulation as matrix operations so they run on **Tensor Cores** instead of the usual per-pixel alpha-blending loops.

**What's new:** Standard rasterizers slow down sharply as the per-Gaussian feature dimension grows (semantic or language features, high-dimensional embeddings). MatrixSplat targets that "feature-scalable" case directly.

**Why it matters:** Feature-field splatting (semantic, CLIP/DINO-distilled, or language-embedded GS) is becoming standard for scene understanding in XR. Faster high-dimensional rendering makes these models practical for interactive or on-headset use. The method could also drop into existing pipelines as a renderer-level speedup.

## 3. DepthGS & SplatAnything — Surface Quality and Feed-Forward Generalization

**Sources:** [DepthGS (2611.13628)](https://awesomepapers.io/computer-vision/papers/2611.13628) · [SplatAnything (2611.08264)](https://awesomepapers.io/machine-learning/papers/hf2611.08264) · **Date:** Nov 2026 (exact days unconfirmed)

**What they are:**
- **DepthGS** uses depth guidance and adaptive regularization to get high-quality *surfaces* (not just good-looking renders) out of Gaussian splatting.
- **SplatAnything** proposes **Gaussian Spacing Normalization**, which lets feed-forward 3DGS models handle arbitrary scene scales, from objects to rooms to outdoor scenes, without per-dataset tuning.

**What's new / why it matters:** These papers attack two long-standing weak points of 3DGS: geometry that's accurate enough to mesh and use in physics or CAD, and feed-forward models that fail when scene scale differs from training data. Both affect whether 3DGS can serve as a general capture-to-asset backend for 3D modeling and XR.

## 4. P-MGS — "Perception Over Geometry" for Mesh-Based Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.07979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07979) · **Date:** submitted Nov 5, 2026

**What it is:** This is a mesh-based splatting method that allocates Gaussians according to estimated *appearance complexity* rather than geometric density.

**What's new:** Allocating by appearance cuts down floating artifacts when the underlying mesh is edited or deformed. Floaters are a known pain point when you bind Gaussians to meshes for animation.

**Why it matters:** Mesh-bound Gaussians connect splat realism with traditional 3D modeling and rigging. This work helps toward editable, animatable splat assets for games, avatars, and XR content.

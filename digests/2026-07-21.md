## 1. SIGGRAPH 2026 Best Paper — "Robust Planar Maps for 3D Vectorization"

**Source:** [ACM SIGGRAPH Blog — Technical Papers Awards](https://blog.siggraph.org/2026/05/siggraph-2026-technical-papers-awards-best-papers-honorable-mentions-and-test-of-time.html/) · [SIGGRAPH 2026 press coverage](https://s2026.siggraph.org/siggraph-2026-technical-papers-showcase-the-research-making-visual-computing-faster-more-reliable-and-accessible/)
**Authors:** Carnegie Mellon University research team
**Date:** Best Paper Award announced ahead of SIGGRAPH 2026, conference running Jul 19–23 in Los Angeles

**What it is:** A geometry-agnostic method for converting 3D scenes into clean, scalable 2D vector images with accurate occlusion handling. It replaces numerically fragile curve–curve intersection math with more tractable curve–line intersections, letting it robustly handle the messy, disconnected curve descriptions common in real-world CAD and scan data.

**What's new:** Prior 3D vectorization pipelines were notoriously unreliable on imperfect real-world geometry. This method runs orders of magnitude faster while being far more numerically stable, bringing production-grade 3D-to-vector conversion within reach for the first time.

**Why it matters to you:** Anyone converting reconstructed 3D scenes (from photogrammetry, Gaussian splats, or CAD) into clean line-art or technical drawings — for documentation, engineering handoff, or stylized rendering — gets a robust, fast building block that previously required manual cleanup.

## 2. Gaussian Point Splatting — Presented at SIGGRAPH 2026

**Source:** [Project page](https://momentsingraphics.de/Siggraph2026.html) · [GitHub](https://github.com/JorisAR/gaussian-point-splatting)
**Authors:** Joris Rijsdijk, Christoph Peters, Michael Weinmann, Ricardo Marroquim
**Date:** Presented at SIGGRAPH 2026 on July 20; published in ACM Transactions on Graphics

**What it is:** A stochastic rendering method that samples pixel-sized, opaque points from each Gaussian and splats them via 64-bit atomics and stochastic transparency, entirely eliminating sorting and tile-based rasterization. It renders scenes with hundreds of millions of Gaussians interactively on consumer GPUs.

**What's new:** Every mainstream real-time 3DGS renderer depends on sorted, tile-based rasterization — a memory and load-balancing bottleneck at scale. This atomic-accumulation approach removes that ceiling entirely, and its live SIGGRAPH presentation this week puts it directly in front of the production graphics community.

**Why it matters to you:** For city-scale captures, large outdoor scenes, or telepresence at photorealistic fidelity, this is the clearest current path past the Gaussian-count ceiling that limits most 3DGS pipelines today.

## 3–4. Standards Converge Around Gaussian Splatting: AMD + OpenUSD at SIGGRAPH 2026

**Source:** [AMD — "Open Standards, Real Solutions: AMD at SIGGRAPH 2026"](https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2026/open-standards-real-solutions-amd-at-siggraph-2026.html) · [Blockchain.News coverage](https://blockchain.news/news/amd-3d-gaussian-splatting-siggraph-2026)
**Date:** SIGGRAPH 2026 (this week)

**What it is:** AMD is using its SIGGRAPH 2026 presence to push 3DGS as a production-ready technique, publishing guides for training/rendering 3DGS with GSplat on ROCm and extending it to dynamic street-scene capture on Instinct MI300X GPUs. Separately, OpenUSD v26.03 (released earlier in 2026, now in active adoption) introduced a first-class schema for Gaussian splats plus a reference renderer (`hdParticleField`), meaning 3DGS assets can now live in the same USD production pipeline as conventional polygon geometry.

**What's new:** Until this year, Gaussian splats needed bespoke interchange pipelines outside standard DCC/VFX tooling. A native USD schema plus vendor-backed open training/rendering guides mean splats are moving from "research artifact" to "just another asset type" in professional 3D pipelines.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're integrating splats into existing VFX, game-engine, or digital-twin pipelines, this is the practical unlock — no more custom glue code between your 3DGS output and your production DCC tools.

## 5. Khronos KHR_gaussian_splatting glTF Extension — Standardization Update

**Source:** [Khronos press release](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/gltf-gaussian-splatting-press-release) · [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/02/3d-gaussian-splats-are-being-added-to-the-gltf-standard/)
**Date:** Release candidate since Feb 2026; ratification targeted for Q2 2026

**What it is:** A glTF 2.0 extension defining how Gaussian splat attributes (position, orientation, scale, spherical-harmonic color, opacity) are stored within glTF mesh primitives, backed by Google, NVIDIA, Apple, and Bentley Systems.

**What's new:** This is the interchange-format counterpart to the USD schema above — together they close the loop from splat capture through production editing to cross-platform delivery in any glTF-compatible viewer or engine.

**Why it matters to you:** Once ratified, this removes the biggest practical barrier to shipping Gaussian splats in consumer-facing apps and web viewers: a lack of a portable, engine-agnostic file format.

## 6. Thinking Machines Lab Releases Inkling — Open-Weight 975B Multimodal MoE

**Source:** [Thinking Machines Lab announcement](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/introducing-inkling/) · [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/15/thinking-machines-lab-releases-inkling-a-975b-parameter-open-weights-multimodal-moe-with-41b-active-parameters-and-controllable-thinking-effort/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/thinking-machines-amps-up-its-bet-against-one-size-fits-all-ai-with-its-first-open-model-inkling/)
**Date:** July 15–16, 2026

**What it is:** Inkling is a 975B-parameter mixture-of-experts transformer (41B active per token, 6-of-256 experts + 2 shared experts) trained from scratch on 45T tokens of text, image, audio, and video, with a 1M-token context window and an encoder-free multimodal design (dMel spectrograms for audio, 40×40-pixel patches for images). A lighter "Inkling-Small" preview (12B active) ships alongside it, and both are open-weight.

**What's new:** Rather than bolting vision/audio encoders onto a text model, Inkling trains all modalities jointly from scratch and forgoes RoPE for a relative-position scheme with local-attention windows on most of its 66 decoder layers — an unusual architecture choice among 2026's frontier releases.

**Why it matters to you:** An open-weight, natively multimodal MoE at this scale is directly usable as a controllable backbone for research pipelines that need vision+language reasoning without API dependency — relevant to any CV/3D project wanting to fine-tune or distill from a strong open multimodal base.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **Standardization is the story of mid-2026, not new algorithms.** The USD schema, the pending glTF extension, and AMD's ROCm/production tooling all point to Gaussian splatting's frontier shifting from "how do we render this faster" to "how do we make this a first-class citizen of existing pipelines."

2. **SIGGRAPH 2026 (this week) is worth watching closely** — the Best Paper and Gaussian Point Splatting talk are only two data points from a conference still running through July 23; more relevant announcements are likely to surface from the full papers/talks program before it closes.

3. **XR hardware is bifurcating toward enterprise + physical-AI use cases** rather than consumer entertainment — both the Niantic Quest 3 update and Xvisio's teleop/data-collection hardware target training, simulation, and robotics rather than media consumption.

4. **Open-weight multimodal foundation models (Inkling) are a growing option for CV/3D research pipelines** that want a strong vision+language backbone without depending on closed APIs, alongside the frontier closed releases from OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI this same window.

## 1. Gemini 3.5 Pro Reportedly Rebuilt From Scratch, Delayed to July 17

**Source:** [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320308/20260713/) · [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/6f0c6bb2-795f-4c57-9d09-6db691d7638a)
**Date:** July 13, 2026

**What it is:** Reports indicate Google DeepMind scrapped its original Gemini 3.5 Pro architecture (built on the 2.5 Pro base) after discovering structural failures in recursive tool-calling and SVG/image generation, rebuilding the model to target a 2M-token context window and improved image/SVG output quality.

**What's new:** A full architectural rebuild this late in a model's release cycle is unusual and suggests the failure modes were deep rather than superficial fine-tuning gaps — recursive tool-calling in particular is a capability increasingly load-bearing for agentic and multimodal pipelines.

**Why it matters:** Frontier multimodal models are the backbone many CV/3D research pipelines lean on for captioning, semantic scene understanding, and agentic tool use. A context-window jump to 2M tokens and better SVG/image generation would be directly useful for tasks like structured scene description, vector-graphics-to-3D workflows, and long-horizon multimodal reasoning over video or 3D captures.

## 2. X-Lens — Real-Time Metric Depth Estimation with Heterogeneous Cameras

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.12993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12993)
**Authors:** Heng Zhou, Shuhong Liu, Wei Sui, Yonghao He, et al.
**Date:** July 14, 2026

**What it is:** A real-time metric-depth estimation framework that works across cameras with differing intrinsics and lens types within a single model, rather than requiring per-camera calibration or retraining.

**What's new:** Most metric-depth models assume a fixed or known camera model; X-Lens targets heterogeneous multi-camera rigs directly, which is the norm for real-world capture setups (phones, action cams, drones, multi-rig scanning arrays).

**Why it matters:** Camera-agnostic, metric-scale depth is a persistent bottleneck for feeding real-world capture into 3D Gaussian Splatting or NeRF pipelines that need consistent absolute scale across heterogeneous sensors — directly relevant to capture-to-splat workflows and multi-device XR scene scanning.

## 3. DermDepth — Monocular Metric-Scale 3D Reconstruction for Dermatology

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.13010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13010)
**Authors:** Héctor Carrión, Narges Norouzi — accepted at MICCAI 2026
**Date:** July 14, 2026

**What it is:** A monocular, metric-scale 3D reconstruction model specialized for dermatological imaging — recovering absolute-scale skin-surface geometry (e.g., lesion size and shape) from a single RGB photo, rather than relative depth.

**What's new:** It extends the metric-depth-foundation-model paradigm (in the lineage of Metric3D, Depth Anything, and similar) into a domain-specific clinical application, showing how general 3D reconstruction advances are diffusing into medical imaging.

**Why it matters:** A useful data point for anyone adapting general-purpose monocular 3D reconstruction backbones to specialized, high-precision domains — the metric-scale calibration tricks here could transfer back to other close-range, small-object 3D capture problems.

## 4. FlowWAM — Optical Flow as a Unified Action Representation for World Action Models

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.13017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13017)
**Date:** ~July 13–14, 2026

**What it is:** Proposes optical flow as an embodiment-agnostic action representation for world/action models, joining a growing line of flow-based world-model work (FlowDreamer, DSWAM) that unifies motion representation across different robot embodiments and camera viewpoints.

**What's new:** Using flow as the shared action interface sidesteps the need for embodiment-specific action spaces, letting a single world model generalize across robots and video sources.

**Why it matters:** This sits at the CV↔robotics crossover — relevant to anyone using video/motion representation learning as a substrate for embodied prediction, and conceptually adjacent to feed-forward video-to-geometry work used in dynamic 3D/4D reconstruction.

## 5. The Seriality Gap in Video Diffusion Models

**Source:** [arXiv 2607.13031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13031)
**Authors:** Jorge Diaz Chao, Konpat Preechakul, Yuxi Liu, Yutong Bai
**Date:** ~July 13–14, 2026

**What it is:** An analysis paper identifying a fundamental limitation in how video diffusion models generate sequential frames — the "seriality gap" — with implications for temporal consistency in generated video.

**What's new:** As video generation increasingly doubles as a general-purpose vision pretraining signal (cf. "Video Generation Models are General-Purpose Vision Learners," arXiv 2607.09024, from earlier this month), understanding its failure modes is timely for anyone building on video diffusion backbones.

**Why it matters:** If you're using video diffusion models as a backbone for downstream 3D or dynamic-scene tasks, this paper's limitations analysis is directly relevant to diagnosing artifacts in generated sequences before they propagate into reconstruction or splatting pipelines.

## 6. Splat-Transform v3.0.0 (PlayCanvas) & aiMotive's Relightable Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** Reported via [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/)
**Date:** July 10, 2026 (just outside the strict 24–48h window, included for relevance)

**What it is:** PlayCanvas rewrote its open-source Gaussian Splatting CLI tool (Splat-Transform) to stream and convert large splat scenes in chunks rather than requiring full in-memory loads. Separately, autonomous-driving simulation vendor aiMotive announced PBR-based relightable Gaussian Splatting integrated into its aiSim 6 simulator.

**What's new:** Chunked streaming conversion addresses a real scaling pain point for large 3DGS scenes; relightable, physically-based GS in an AV simulator shows Gaussian Splatting moving beyond static novel-view rendering into editable, physically-consistent simulation pipelines.

**Why it matters:** Both point to the same trend — 3DGS tooling maturing from research-lab scripts into production infrastructure that has to handle scale (streaming) and editability (relighting), which is exactly what's needed for XR content pipelines and simulation/synthetic-data generation.

OTOY's Octane 2027.1 renders Gaussian splats faster and imports them via glTF. OTOY says relightable splat rendering is planned for Octane 2028. If you're shipping captures to artists or render pipelines, glTF is becoming the format to target.

## 3. UPP-Gauss: ultrasound-physics Gaussian splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.33920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33920)
**Date:** Nov 25 2026

**What it is:** A Gaussian splatting method for novel-view synthesis of B-mode ultrasound. It puts ultrasound propagation physics into the rendering model in place of standard optical alpha-compositing.

**What's new / why it matters:** This is another example of 3DGS being adapted to non-optical sensors by swapping the image-formation model while keeping the explicit primitives and fast rasterization. The same pattern of keeping the primitives and replacing the renderer could apply to other sensing setups, such as lidar, radar or thermal, in XR and robotics.

## 4. VersionGS: versioned Gaussians for dynamic scenes

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.33374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33374)
**Date:** Nov 24 2026

**What it is:** A dynamic Gaussian splatting representation that stores per-frame Gaussian states in a shared, versioned dictionary, so frames reuse states instead of deforming one canonical set.

**What's new / why it matters:** Deformation-field methods break down on fast or large motion. Storing explicit versions handles that motion while staying compact. That matters for streaming volumetric video to headsets, where you need both storage efficiency and robustness to motion.

## 5. WildAnySplat: feed-forward 3DGS in the wild

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.31023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31023)
**Date:** Nov 23 2026 · Accepted to SIGGRAPH Asia 2026

**What it is:** A feed-forward model that predicts 3D Gaussians directly from unconstrained internet photo collections, which have varying lighting, transient objects and unknown cameras. There's no per-scene optimization.

**What's new / why it matters:** Feed-forward splatting such as AnySplat has mostly worked on clean, consistent captures. Handling appearance variation and occluders in a single forward pass makes instant reconstruction from casual or crowdsourced photos realistic. It's a strong baseline to track if you build fast capture-to-XR pipelines.

## 1. TRELLIS.2 — Native and Compact Structured Latents for 3D Generation

**Source:** [CVPR 2026 Open Access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Xiang_Native_and_Compact_Structured_Latents_for_3D_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html) · [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/native-and-compact-structured-latents-for-3d-generation/) · [GitHub (TRELLIS.2)](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2) · [HuggingFace Model](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/TRELLIS.2-4B)  
**Authors:** Xiang et al. — Tsinghua University, Microsoft Research, USTC, Microsoft AI  
**Date:** CVPR 2026 Best Paper #2 (awarded June 5–6, 2026); model code released December 2025 (MIT licence)

**What it is:** TRELLIS.2 introduces O-Voxel (*omni-voxel*), a sparse voxel structure that encodes both geometry and PBR material appearance in a single "field-free" latent without relying on iso-surface fields. A 4B-parameter flow-matching transformer trained on diverse public 3D assets generates assets at up to 1536³ resolution in under 60 seconds. A Sparse Compression VAE provides 16× spatial downsampling into a compact latent. The open-source model (`TRELLIS.2-4B`) is available on Hugging Face under MIT licence.

**What's new:** Prior 3D generative models (e.g. TRELLIS 1, TripoSR) relied on iso-surface fields (SDF/occupancy) that break on open surfaces, non-manifold geometry, and objects with thin structures. O-Voxel robustly handles arbitrary topology and outputs full PBR materials including transparency/translucency — a combination not previously achievable at this quality. The CVPR Best Paper award (alongside D4RT, covered June 12) confirms it as one of the two landmark contributions of the conference.

**Why it matters to you:** TRELLIS.2 is the strongest open-source image-to-3D model available. The USD/OBJ export with PBR materials slots directly into spatial computing pipelines (RealityKit, Omniverse). For XR scene population, rapid high-quality 3D asset generation from reference photos addresses a core production bottleneck. The MIT licence removes IP friction for commercial XR development.

## 2. Velox — Learning Representations of 4D Geometry and Appearance

**Source:** [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/velox) · [Project Page](https://apple.github.io/ml-velox/) · [arXiv 2605.04527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04527)  
**Authors:** Anagh Malik, Dorian Chan, Xiaoming Zhao, David B. Lindell, Oncel Tuzel, Jen-Hao Rick Chang — Apple  
**Date:** Presented at CVPR 2026, June 6, 2026

**What it is:** Velox is a framework for learning compact latent representations of dynamic 4D objects from unstructured spatiotemporal colour point clouds. An encoder compresses the point cloud into a set of *dynamic shape tokens*, supervised jointly by a 4D surface decoder (capturing time-varying geometry) and a Gaussian decoder (mapping tokens to 3D Gaussians for appearance). The unified representation is evaluated on video-to-4D generation, 3D tracking, and cloth simulation via image-to-4D generation, achieving strong results across all three downstream tasks.

**What's new:** Most 4D representations either decouple geometry from appearance (separate NeRF + flow networks) or require per-scene optimisation. Velox learns a single shared latent that is simultaneously descriptive, compressive, and accessible from raw point cloud input. The Gaussian decoder output is particularly notable: it links the feed-forward token representation directly to the 3DGS rendering pipeline, enabling real-time rendering of reconstructed 4D objects without further optimisation.

**Why it matters to you:** Velox is a natural complement to D4RT (June 12 digest). D4RT reconstructs geometry and motion from video; Velox provides the compressed token representation needed to store, transmit, and render that result efficiently. For XR telepresence and dynamic avatar streaming, a compact 4D token representation that decodes to Gaussians is exactly the architecture needed for low-bandwidth, real-time dynamic scene delivery.

## 3. Neural Gabor Splatting — High-Frequency Surface Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2604.15941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15941) · [Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.15941)  
**Authors:** (Team unspecified in retrieved sources)  
**Date:** April 17, 2026 (arXiv)

**What it is:** 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents colour with a single isotropic value per primitive, which forces the optimiser to spawn very large numbers of tiny Gaussians to approximate high-frequency surface detail (detailed brick patterns, fine fabric, etched metal). Neural Gabor Splatting addresses this by augmenting each Gaussian primitive with a lightweight MLP that models colour as a Gabor-basis function, enabling a single primitive to represent a wide band of frequency content. A companion *frequency-aware densification* strategy selects over-mismatch primitives for cloning/pruning based on frequency energy rather than position gradient alone. Results show improved PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across standard benchmarks with substantially fewer primitives than vanilla 3DGS or neural-MLP-augmented variants.

**What's new:** Prior neural 3DGS approaches (e.g. Scaffold-GS, Spec-Gaussian) appended general MLPs to Gaussians to model view-dependent effects, not texture frequency. The Gabor basis is a principled signal-processing choice for frequency-band control, and the frequency-aware densification is a novel gradient signal that operates in frequency space rather than positional space.

**Why it matters to you:** Scenes with rich surface texture (architectural interiors, product surfaces, human skin) have historically required 2–5× more Gaussians in 3DGS than geometry-simple outdoor scenes. Neural Gabor Splatting directly reduces this overhead, lowering VRAM and rendering cost for exactly the high-fidelity XR use cases (virtual try-on, detailed object inspection in spatial computing) where texture accuracy matters most.

## 4. "Recent Advances and Trends in Learning-based 3D Representations" — Survey

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04871)  
**Authors:** (Team unspecified in retrieved sources)  
**Date:** June 3, 2026 (arXiv)

**What it is:** A comprehensive survey and taxonomy of learning-based 3D representations, spanning discrete explicit formats (point clouds, voxel grids, meshes) through continuous implicit fields (NeRF, occupancy networks, SDFs) to primitive-based splatting approaches (3DGS and variants). The paper unifies these families under a single design-space framework and analyses each along five axes: accuracy, efficiency, differentiability, editability, and downstream compatibility. Application domains covered include games, AR/VR, autonomous driving, robot navigation, and medical imaging.

**What's new:** While earlier surveys covered NeRF or 3DGS in isolation, this is the first post-CVPR 2026 survey that treats 3DGS and its derivatives (e.g. 4D-GS, 2D-GS, compact GS) as a mature third family alongside implicit fields and explicit representations. The timing — immediately after CVPR 2026 — means it incorporates D4RT, FastGS, and TRELLIS.2 as case studies in its taxonomy.

**Why it matters to you:** This survey is a useful orientation document for researchers and engineers entering the 3D representation space, and a good citation anchor for papers that need to justify representation choices. If you are evaluating which representation to use for a specific XR or 3D pipeline component (storage, rendering, editing, downstream ML), the five-axis analysis provides a structured comparison without requiring you to read the primary literature for each method.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **4D tokens + Gaussian rendering is converging** — Velox (Apple) and D4RT (Google DeepMind/UCL, June 12) together sketch a complete pipeline: D4RT reconstructs dynamic 4D geometry from video in one pass; Velox compresses it into tokens that decode to Gaussians for real-time rendering. The two systems are complementary and suggest feed-forward 4D capture → real-time rendering may be solved within 6–12 months.

2. **Frequency-aware 3DGS is an emerging sub-field** — Neural Gabor Splatting and 3D Gabor Splatting (arXiv 2504.11003) both appeared in 2026 Q2, signalling that frequency-domain methods are being systematically applied to fix 3DGS's known weakness on high-frequency textures. Expect this to be a theme at SIGGRAPH 2026 and ICCV 2026.

3. **Consumer AR glasses convergence** — Snap Specs (AWE June 16), XREAL × Google Project Aura, and Apple Vision Pro 2 (rumoured) are all on overlapping 2026 launch windows. This is the first year where multiple consumer-grade true-AR headsets ship from major vendors — a fundamental change in the XR deployment landscape.

4. **Open-source 3D generative models catching up to closed** — TRELLIS.2 (MIT licence, 4B params) now provides open-source image-to-3D quality that approaches closed APIs (Meshy, Tripo, Luma). Combined with Wonder 3D in Autodesk Flow Studio (June 12 digest), the toolchain for AI-assisted 3D asset production is becoming broadly accessible.

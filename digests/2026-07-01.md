## 1. Google Gemini Omni Flash (Preview) & Nano Banana 2 Lite

**Source:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-omni-flash-nano-banana-2-lite/) · [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/30/googles-gemini-omni-flash-nano-banana-2-lite-support-slick-media-content-creation-lower-costs/)
**Date:** June 30, 2026

**What it is:** Google DeepMind released Gemini Omni Flash Preview, the first model in a new "Omni" family that natively generates and conversationally edits video from mixed inputs (text, image, audio, video), plus Nano Banana 2 Lite, a lighter-weight image generation/editing model. Omni Flash is priced at $0.10/second of video output, comparable to Veo 3.1 Fast, and ships via the Gemini API, AI Studio, and Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform.

**Why it matters:** Mostly an industry signal rather than a direct research tool — native multimodal video generation with in-context "conversational" (iterate, don't regenerate) editing points toward video/vision generation stacks converging with agentic workflows. Tangentially useful for synthetic data generation and video-conditioned world-model pipelines, but not a CV/3D/XR-specific advance.

## 2. Lift4D — Single-View 3D Estimation Adapted for In-the-Wild 4D Reconstruction

**Source:** [arXiv 2606.23688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23688) (Carnegie Mellon University — Litman, Ma, Shah, Ugrinovic, Kitani, De la Torre, Tulsiani)
**Date:** arXiv ID places it in late June 2026 (~June 29–30); exact day-level timestamp unconfirmed since arxiv.org blocked direct fetch during research.

**What it is:** A test-time optimization framework that adapts a single-view 3D reconstruction model to produce temporally consistent 4D (dynamic) reconstructions from monocular video, combining causal latent conditioning with occlusion-aware "sculpting" driven by a view-conditioned diffusion prior to fill in unobserved regions.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to dynamic/4D reconstruction work — it targets a persistent weak point (large deformations and occlusions in real-world, not lab-captured, video) without requiring scarce 4D training data. Worth a closer read if you're doing monocular video-to-4D pipelines for AR/VR or content creation; conceptually adjacent to the D4RT/Flex4DHuman/World Tracing line covered in the June 12 digest.

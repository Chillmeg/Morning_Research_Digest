## 1. COSA-GS — Towards Practical Compression of 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.30245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30245)
**Authors:** Pengpeng Yu et al.
**Date:** September 24, 2026

**What it is:** COSA-GS is a new context model for compressing 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes. Instead of the spatial-aggregation context modeling used by prior compressors (which is slow to train/decode and prone to floating-point inconsistencies across platforms), it uses anchor-wise causal factorization: geometry context from each anchor's coordinates is fused with a compact learnable anchor latent to model attribute coding, using only linear transforms and activations.

**What's new:** The paper explicitly targets *practical* deployment problems that prior compression work ignored — non-deterministic floating-point context inference causes entropy-decoding failures when a splat is compressed on one machine and decoded on another. COSA-GS adds quantization-aware training and integer inference for bit-exact, cross-platform-consistent decoding while still hitting state-of-the-art compression ratios.

**Why it matters to you:** Storage/bandwidth has been the main blocker for shipping 3DGS scenes to consumer devices (web viewers, mobile AR, cloud-streamed XR). A compressor that guarantees bit-exact decode across CPU/GPU/OS combinations is a prerequisite for any product pipeline (e.g., Apple Maps-style splats, or streaming splats to a headset) — this is the kind of unglamorous engineering work that turns a research prototype into something shippable.

## 2. OceanXL — Large-Scale Underwater 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.29985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29985) · [Project page](https://wanghaoran16.github.io/OceanXL-Large-scale-Underwater-3D-Gaussian-Splatting/)
**Date:** ~September 25, 2026

**What it is:** A scalable underwater 3DGS framework that partitions large marine scenes into spatial blocks, reconstructs each independently, and merges them into one coherent representation. It introduces an underwater-aware density-control strategy and an adaptive pruning scheme tuned to underwater degradation (scattering, color attenuation, low texture), plus a new large-scale underwater dataset.

**What's new:** Most underwater-GS work (WaterSplatting, OceanSplat, SeaThru-NeRF-scale methods) targets small scenes. OceanXL is explicitly built for scale, with a divide-and-conquer pipeline and compact per-block pruning, showing better quality-vs-size trade-offs than large-scene baselines built for terrestrial capture.

**Why it matters to you:** Underwater and other degraded-visibility domains (fog, smoke — see the NTIRE 2026 RealX3D challenge) are a recurring stress test for how well GS/3D-reconstruction generalizes outside clean, well-lit benchmark scenes. If you work on real-world capture pipelines, OceanXL's block-partition + domain-aware pruning recipe is a reusable pattern for any large, non-ideal-illumination scene.

## 3. SplatLabel — Pseudo-Labelling Through 4D Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.29836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29836)
**Date:** ~September 25, 2026

**What it is:** An automated pipeline that uses 4D Gaussian Splatting to generate 3D semantic pseudo-labels and volumetric occupancy grids — extracting LiDAR segmentation with predictive confidence at arbitrary voxel resolutions. It models object trajectories and lifespans through an explicit temporal manifold rather than treating motion as post-processing or an implicit global deformation field, so it needs no pre-annotated 3D bounding boxes.

**What's new:** Prior 4DGS dynamic-scene work has mostly targeted rendering quality; SplatLabel repurposes the same explicit, per-primitive dynamics as a *labelling engine* — turning a trained 4DGS scene directly into training data (segmentation + occupancy) for downstream perception models.

**Why it matters to you:** This is a nice example of Gaussian Splatting being used as infrastructure rather than an end product — if you need cheap 3D/4D semantic labels for autonomous driving, robotics, or scene-understanding datasets, this sidesteps expensive manual annotation of dynamic scenes.

## 4. "Is Gaussian Splatting Becoming Neural Again?" — A Taxonomy of Learned Parameterization

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.12395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12395)
**Date:** September 19, 2026

**What it is:** A systematic study of the growing trend of injecting neural networks back into 3DGS pipelines (anchor decoders, spatial fields, deformation networks, feed-forward predictors). The authors organize 19 representative methods along five axes — attribute decoding, spatial sharing, view-conditioned decoding, topology generation, and amortized inference — and run controlled ablations to isolate which choices actually help.

**What's new:** The clean empirical finding: sharing *appearance and opacity* via learned functions consistently improves reconstruction quality, but decoding *geometric structure* through a network offers no further gain over explicit per-Gaussian geometry. Conclusion: 3DGS isn't reverting to NeRF-style implicit fields, it's selectively adopting neural parameter sharing only where it captures genuinely reusable correlations.

**Why it matters to you:** This is a useful map for anyone building a new 3DGS variant — it tells you where to spend a "neural component" budget (appearance/opacity sharing) and where not to bother (geometry decoding), instead of re-discovering this by trial and error.

## 5. Carveout — Post-hoc Object Segmentation for Trained 3DGS Scenes

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/)
**Date:** September 22, 2026

**What it is:** A tool that segments and labels objects *inside an already-trained* 3D Gaussian Splatting scene using SAM 3, exporting each identified object as its own standalone `.ply` — with no retraining of the original splat required.

**What's new:** Most 3DGS segmentation work (Feature 3DGS, GAGS, OpenInsGaussian, etc.) bakes semantic features in during training. Carveout instead operates purely post-hoc on a finished splat, which is a much lower-friction workflow for anyone who already has a large library of trained scenes and just wants to pull individual assets out of them.

**Why it matters to you:** Directly useful for asset-reuse workflows — extracting a single object (a prop, a piece of furniture, a person) from a captured scene for reuse elsewhere, without needing the original capture pipeline or a from-scratch re-train.

## 6. Spirula Studio v2026.9.24 & XGRIDS LCC Unreal SDK v3.4.0 — Tooling Roundup

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/)
**Date:** September 23–24, 2026

**What it is:** Two notable tooling releases this week: **Spirula Studio v2026.9.24** adds editing for Gaussian splats, sparse reconstructions, and meshes in one app, plus image/video render export and a new MCP server (for programmatic/agent-driven control of the editor). **XGRIDS LCC Unreal SDK v3.4.0** adds orthographic projection support for LCC/LCC2/SOG/SPZ/PLY splat formats, a "Pro Load Volume" region loader, and tiled rasterization for large scenes in Unreal Engine.

**What's new:** The Spirula MCP server is notable — it's an early example of a 3D-editing tool exposing itself to LLM-agent control, following the same pattern as Blender/CAD MCP integrations. The XGRIDS update pushes large-scale splat scenes further into standard game-engine (Unreal) production pipelines via tiled rasterization and format-agnostic loading.

**Why it matters to you:** If you're building agent-driven 3D content pipelines, the MCP-server trend in splat editors is worth tracking — it's a path toward "describe the edit, let an agent apply it" workflows for 3D assets. The Unreal SDK update is relevant if you need to bring city/campus-scale splats into a real-time game engine rather than a bespoke viewer.

## 8. World Labs Atlas — Omni World Model (Still Making Waves)

**Source:** [World Labs blog](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/atlas) · [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/01/fei-fei-lis-world-labs-debuts-atlas-a-world-model-showcase-for-advanced-spatial-intelligence/)
**Date:** September 1, 2026 (early access; still the dominant reference point in this week's discussion)

**What it is:** Fei-Fei Li's World Labs introduced Atlas, a multimodal autoregressive-diffusion-transformer model pretrained from scratch to natively operate across text, image, video, camera pose, and 3D depth. It generates 3D-consistent video up to 1440p/60s that stays geometrically coherent from any viewing angle, and can output 3D assets directly — including point clouds and 3D Gaussian splats.

**What's new:** Unlike Marble (World Labs' shipped consumer product) or prior video-diffusion world models, Atlas treats generation, reconstruction, and simulation as one unified task rather than three separate systems, and is explicitly aimed at producing simulation-grade environments for robotics training rather than just visualization.

**Why it matters to you:** Atlas is a template for where feed-forward 3D reconstruction and generative world models are converging — a single model that can hallucinate *and* reconstruct a 3D-consistent scene, directly emitting Gaussian splats as one of its output modalities. Currently early-access/partner-only (no public API yet), but worth tracking as a signal for where general-purpose "world models" and dedicated 3DGS pipelines are headed.

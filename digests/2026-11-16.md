## 1. World Labs Atlas: general availability + developer API

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/), with background from [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/world-labs-debuts-atlas-an-omni-world-model-in-early-access) and the [World Labs export docs](https://docs.worldlabs.ai/api/reference/worlds/export)
**Date:** Nov 13, 2026 (single source for the GA itself)

**What it is:** World Labs has opened its "omni world model" Atlas to everyone. Atlas was in partner-only early access from September 1. The launch adds the Viewfinder, Chisel, Splats-to-Mesh and Relight tools, plus a developer API that outputs worlds as SPZ-compressed Gaussian splats. Mesh export (GLB) is also available.

**What's new:** Before this, you could generate worlds in Marble, but you couldn't edit or relight the result, and getting a mesh out took extra work. Now generation, splat→mesh conversion and relighting all run in one hosted pipeline that you can call from code.

**Why it matters to you:** You can produce generated 3DGS scenes at scale through the API, for example as synthetic training or evaluation data, or as XR environments. Splats-to-Mesh and Relight also give you a baseline to compare against your own splat→mesh or relightable-GS work. Check the pricing and licensing before you use it to build a dataset.

## 2. Splattic Public Beta 4.0.0: 4D Gaussian splat editing in the browser

**Source:** [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/) (single source)
**Date:** Nov 14, 2026

**What it is:** A browser-based splat editor that can now play back and edit 4D (time-varying) Gaussian splats. It supports time-aware selection and retiming, and it exports to HTML and web AR.

**What's new:** Most splat editors (SuperSplat and similar) only handle static scenes. This is one of the first general-purpose tools that edits dynamic splats directly.

**Why it matters to you:** You can use it to inspect, clean up and demo 4DGS reconstructions. You can also publish dynamic captures to web AR without building your own viewer.

## 5. Topology-Aware 4D Gaussian Splatting for Egocentric Videos (Topo-4DGS + TopoEgo)

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.16012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16012)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 11, 2026

**What it is:** The paper introduces TopoEgo, which it describes as the first benchmark of topology changes (cutting, opening and similar) in egocentric video. It also proposes Topo-4DGS, a 4DGS method designed to reconstruct those changes.

**What's new:** Standard 4DGS uses deformation fields, which assume the scene's topology stays the same. They break down when an object is split or opened, which happens all the time in hand-object interaction.

**Why it matters to you:** It's directly relevant to reconstructing dynamic scenes from AR glasses (cooking, assembly, manipulation). The benchmark also gives you a new way to stress-test any 4DGS method.

## 6. MatrixSplat: matrix-parallel, feature-scalable 3DGS rendering

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.15153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15153)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 11, 2026 (accepted to NeurIPS 2026)

**What it is:** A GPU rendering framework that rewrites Gaussian splat rasterization so it runs on Tensor Cores. It is aimed at splats that carry high-dimensional features rather than just RGB.

**What's new:** Rendering cost in standard rasterizers grows quickly as the per-Gaussian feature dimension increases. Using matrix-parallel hardware makes feature fields (semantic, language or DINO features) much cheaper to render.

**Why it matters to you:** It speeds up feature-distillation and semantic 3DGS pipelines, and it could make real-time language-queryable splats practical in XR.

## 7. Camera3R: feed-forward 3D reconstruction across heterogeneous camera models

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.13453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13453)
**Date:** Submitted Nov 10, 2026

**What it is:** A LoRA-style adaptation (about 0.5% extra parameters) that extends feed-forward reconstructors pretrained on perspective images (the DUSt3R/VGGT family) to fisheye, omnidirectional and other non-pinhole cameras. Performance on perspective inputs is preserved.

**What's new:** Today you have to undistort images first or retrain from scratch. Camera3R handles mixed camera rigs in a single model.

**Why it matters to you:** Headsets, robots and 360° rigs mostly use wide-FOV lenses, so this removes a common source of preprocessing pain when you feed real XR capture data into feed-forward reconstruction.

*Also worth noting:* [SM4RT (arXiv 2611.08436)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08436), from Nov 5, is a cascaded feed-forward 4D reconstruction model (geometry first, then motion). [P-MGS (arXiv 2611.07979)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07979) allocates Gaussians in mesh-based splatting according to how much appearance detail each part of the surface needs. [SplatAnything (arXiv 2611.08264)](https://awesomepapers.io/machine-learning/papers/hf2611.08264) is a feed-forward 3DGS model, and its weights and code were released around Nov 8 ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/splatanything-releases-inference-code-with-gaussian-spacing-fix)).

## 8. Blender 5.3: native Gaussian splat support

**Date:** Nov 10, 2026

**What it is:** Blender 5.3 makes Vulkan the default backend, adds NVIDIA DLSS, and supports Gaussian splats natively.

**What's new:** Before this release, splats in Blender needed third-party add-ons.

**Why it matters to you:** You can now combine splat captures with meshes, cameras and animation in a mainstream DCC tool.

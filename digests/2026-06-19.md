## 3. EDoF-NeRF — Extended Depth-of-Field Neural Radiance Fields

**Source:** [arXiv:2606.18826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18826)  
**Date:** Submitted June 17, 2026

**What it is:** EDoF-NeRF introduces a coded aperture camera model into the NeRF framework to reconstruct scenes with an extended depth-of-field from defocused images. A coded aperture mask placed at the camera pupil preserves high-frequency spatial components under defocus that a standard aperture would blur out. The method develops a differentiable camera model that accepts coded aperture images directly and produces novel views with genuinely extended DOF — no post-processing deconvolution required.

**What's new:** NeRF pipelines to date assume sharp input images or model defocus as a nuisance to be corrected. EDoF-NeRF instead treats the optical coding as a feature, jointly learning the scene representation and the deblurring operation from coded inputs. This is the first NeRF method to exploit coded apertures for DOF extension rather than treating lens effects as degradation.

**Why it matters:** For macro or close-range 3D capture (medical imaging, product scanning, botanical documentation), DOF limitations are a fundamental constraint. EDoF-NeRF opens a route to high-fidelity NeRF reconstruction in precisely these regimes. It also raises the question of whether 3DGS can benefit from similar coded aperture priors — a near-term research direction.

## 4. 3DGS Applications Survey — Accepted in TPAMI 2026

**Sources:** [arXiv:2508.09977 v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09977) · [GitHub: Awesome-3DGS-Applications](https://github.com/heshuting555/Awesome-3DGS-Applications) · [Hugging Face Paper Page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.09977)  
**Date:** Updated June 15, 2026 (v4); accepted in IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (TPAMI) 2026

**What it is:** "A Survey on 3D Gaussian Splatting Applications: Segmentation, Editing, and Generation" is the first dedicated survey to systematically review downstream applications of 3DGS beyond classical novel view synthesis. The survey categorises work into three foundational task families — **segmentation** (semantic and instance understanding of splats), **editing** (scene modification, style transfer, inpainting), and **generation** (text/image-to-3DGS, compositional scene generation) — plus a broader set of functional applications. It surveys 2D foundation models (SAM, CLIP, DINOv2) that enable semantic control in 3DGS, and traces the lineage from NeRF-based methods to current 3DGS counterparts.

**What's new:** TPAMI acceptance confers peer-reviewed, archival status on what was an arXiv preprint. The v4 update on June 15 reflects community feedback since August 2025. The survey is now the canonical reference for 3DGS downstream tasks.

**Why it matters:** This is the reference to cite and consult when scoping 3DGS editing, scene composition, or segmentation work. The classification framework is immediately useful for identifying where new methods fit in the landscape and which baselines to run against.

## 5. KAIST VOTP — ICML 2026 Oral: Human Preference Learning from Video

**Sources:** [ICML 2026 Oral](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/oral/71090) · [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/06/10/kaist-develops-physical-ai-breakthrough-that-learns-human) · [Mirage News](https://www.miragenews.com/kaist-breakthrough-robots-now-mimic-human-1689467/)  
**Authors:** Lou Minh Tung (first author, KAIST EE PhD) et al.  
**Date:** Announced June 10, 2026; ICML 2026 Oral (top 0.7% of 23,918 submissions)

**What it is:** VOTP (Video-based Optimal TransPort Preference) is a semi-supervised reward learning framework for preference-based reinforcement learning. It uses Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) to embed visual trajectories into a rich representation space, then leverages optimal transport to align those trajectories and generate high-fidelity pseudo-labels for large quantities of unlabelled robot action data. The result: AI agents can be aligned with human intent using only a handful of labelled preference videos, eliminating the need for tens of thousands of human evaluations.

**What's new:** Prior preference-based RL methods required massive human annotation budgets. VOTP shifts the bottleneck from annotation volume to video model quality — the ViFM embedding does the heavy lifting so optimal transport can identify meaningful trajectory similarity with very few labels. Selection as an ICML Oral places it among the top ~168 papers globally in ML this year.

**Why it matters:** For any embodied AI or robotics pipeline that needs to learn from demonstrations — including XR interaction design, 3D scene manipulation agents, or robot-assisted 3D capture — VOTP dramatically reduces the cost of aligning model behaviour with human preferences. The technique is transferable to any task where video demonstrations are easier to collect than scalar reward labels.

## 6. Cesium June 2026 — Mesh-Free Gaussian Splat Reconstruction in ion

**Sources:** [Cesium Blog: June 2026 Releases](https://cesium.com/blog/2026/06/01/cesium-releases-in-june-2026/) · [Radiance Fields: Cesium Hierarchical LOD](https://radiancefields.com/cesium-adds-hierarchical-lod-for-gaussian-splats-to-3d-tiles-cesiumjs-and-cesium-for-unreal) · [TheFuture3D: State of GS in 2026](https://www.thefuture3d.com/blog/state-of-gaussian-splatting-2026/)  
**Date:** June 2026

**What it is:** The June 2026 release of Cesium ion removes the requirement that reconstruction jobs include mesh generation. Users can now run point cloud-only or Gaussian splat-only reconstruction pipelines through the ion REST API and web UI. CesiumJS 1.142 was also released alongside, adding vector tiles rendering support for 3D Tiles (points, lines, polygons with property-based styling and picking). Cesium's integration with Bentley's iTwin Capture backend means captured photo sets can now exit as mesh, point cloud, or 3DGS directly from one UI.

**What's new:** Previously, Gaussian splat jobs in Cesium ion required a mesh step as a prerequisite — adding latency and compute cost. The decoupled pipeline makes GS a first-class reconstruction output rather than a derivative of mesh reconstruction, and is the first major geospatial platform to offer this.

**Why it matters:** For large-scale outdoor scene capture (cities, infrastructure, terrain), Cesium ion is the dominant production pipeline. Removing the mesh requirement means Gaussian splat outputs are now accessible to infrastructure and mapping professionals without a photogrammetry workflow detour — a significant acceleration for the adoption of GS in geospatial contexts. Combined with Apple Maps' GS shift and Gaussian Point Splatting's scale, this represents city-scale GS production becoming operationally real.

## 7. KHR_gaussian_splatting — glTF Standard Ratification Imminent

**Sources:** [Khronos Press Release](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/gltf-gaussian-splatting-press-release) · [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/02/3d-gaussian-splats-are-being-added-to-the-gltf-standard/) · [Radiance Fields: Khronos GS glTF RC](https://radiancefields.com/the-khronos-group-introduces-a-gltf-baseline-for-gaussian-splatting) · [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/reality-capture-modelling/khronos-announces-gltf-gaussian-splatting-extension)  
**Date:** Release candidate announced February 4, 2026; final ratification expected Q2 2026 (ending June 30)

**What it is:** KHR_gaussian_splatting is a Khronos extension to glTF 2.0 that defines how 3D Gaussian Splat attributes (means, covariances, opacities, SH coefficients) are stored within glTF mesh primitives. Contributors include Autodesk, Bentley Systems, Huawei, Niantic Spatial, and NVIDIA. Once ratified, any glTF-compatible viewer, engine, or pipeline will be able to ingest and render Gaussian splat scenes using the same format.

**What's new:** The community feedback window on the release candidate is closing, and Q2 2026 ends June 30 — ratification is days away. A parallel standardisation effort by the Alliance for OpenUSD (AOUSD) is developing a Particle Fields schema for native USD-based GS representation, creating a two-standard landscape (glTF for delivery, USD for authoring) analogous to how FBX/OBJ vs. USD splits the current mesh world.

**Why it matters:** A ratified glTF GS standard means any 3DGS scene can be embedded in the same container as meshes, textures, and animations — eliminating bespoke `.splat`/`.ply` file handling in every application. For XR workflows (RealityKit, WebXR, Unreal, Unity), this is the interoperability foundation. Once Khronos ratifies, expect rapid adoption in web viewers, game engines, and DAM systems through H2 2026.

## Emerging Trends (This Week)

1. **AWE 2026 confirmed: spatial AI is the primary XR UX paradigm, not an add-on.** Both major hardware announcements (Snap Specs, VITURE Helix) ship with multimodal AI vision as a first-class feature. The era of AR-as-display is giving way to AR-as-AI-agent.

2. **Gaussian splatting standardisation is crossing the finish line.** KHR_gaussian_splatting ratification (before June 30), Cesium's mesh-free GS pipelines, and TPAMI acceptance of the GS applications survey collectively signal that GS is exiting the research phase and entering the toolchain mainstream.

3. **Coded-optics + NeRF/3DGS is an underexplored frontier.** EDoF-NeRF (Jun 17) is the first paper to fuse coded aperture optics with radiance field reconstruction. The approach is likely to generalise to light field cameras, event cameras, and other non-standard imagers — watch this space.

4. **Few-label preference learning (VOTP) could reshape embodied 3D AI.** If reward functions for robot manipulation or scene interaction can be learned from a handful of preference videos, it dramatically lowers the cost of training agents that work in physical 3D environments — a key bottleneck for XR-embodied AI.

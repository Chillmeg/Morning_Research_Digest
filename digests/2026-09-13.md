## 1. ECCV 2026 Wraps Up in Malmö — World Models Take Center Stage

**Source:** [ECCV 2026 official site](https://eccv.ecva.net/) · [ECCV 2026 Keynotes & Panels](https://eccv.ecva.net/virtual/2026/eventlistwithbios/keynotes-panels) · [TechTimes preview](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326764/20260905/eccv-2026-preview-computer-vision-converges-world-models-embodied-ai.htm)
**Date:** September 8–12, 2026

**What it is:** The 19th European Conference on Computer Vision closed yesterday in Malmö, Sweden, with 2,883 accepted papers out of 10,473 submissions and a record 86 workshops. The marquee event was Yann LeCun's closing keynote, "World Models: Enabling the Next AI Revolution" (Sep 11), building on his long-running argument that predictive world models — not pattern-matching LLMs — are the path to grounded machine intelligence. Kristen Grauman (egocentric AI) and Jamie Shotton (autonomous driving as embodied AI's proving ground) also keynoted.

**What's new:** This is the first major CV conference where "world models" and embodied/3D-grounded reasoning displaced pure 2D recognition as the dominant framing, with a dedicated workshop, *3D in the Era of World Models*, run alongside the main track.

**Why it matters to you:** The conference program is the best single signal of where CV research is headed over the next 12 months, and this year the through-line is explicit: feed-forward 3D/4D reconstruction, Gaussian-based scene representations, and video-generative priors are being treated as core infrastructure for world models rather than standalone niches — directly upstream of Gaussian splatting, 3D reconstruction, and XR work.

## 2. Heat Kernel Textures (HKTex) — ECCV 2026 Best Paper

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.07557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07557) · [ECCV Oral listing](https://eccv.ecva.net/virtual/2026/oral/6124) · [GitHub](https://github.com/circle-group/hktex)
**Authors:** Simone Foti, Caner Korkmaz, Stefanos Zafeiriou, Tolga Birdal
**Date:** Presented Sep 11, 2026 (arXiv Sep 9, 2026)

**What it is:** HKTex replaces UV-mapped textures with anisotropic "heat kernels" — geodesic analogues of Gaussians — placed directly and intrinsically on a triangle mesh surface, using discrete Riemannian geometry. It borrows 3D Gaussian Splatting's idea of representing appearance as a mixture of soft, blendable primitives, but grounds them on the mesh manifold instead of splatting them in screen space.

**What's new:** UV unwrapping has been a structural bottleneck in mesh texturing for decades — it introduces seams, distortion, wasted texture space, and resolution inconsistency. HKTex sidesteps unwrapping entirely by defining appearance intrinsically on the surface, and the ECCV award panel specifically praised the elegance and broad applicability of the idea beyond this one paper.

**Why it matters to you:** This is a genuinely new primitive at the intersection of Gaussian splatting and classical 3D modeling — relevant to anyone building 3D asset pipelines (game/film assets, generative 3D, digital twins) who currently pays the UV-unwrapping tax, and a candidate building block for future splat-to-mesh or hybrid mesh/Gaussian representations.

## 3. LSRM — ECCV 2026 Best Paper Honorable Mention (Meta Reality Labs)

**Source:** [arXiv 2604.05182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05182) · [GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/Large-Sparse-Reconstruction-Model) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/facebook/Large-Sparse-Reconstruction-Model)
**Authors:** Zhengqin Li, Cheng Zhang, Jakob Engel, Zhao Dong — Meta Reality Labs Research
**Date:** ECCV 2026 (Sep 2026)

**What it is:** LSRM (Large Sparse Reconstruction Model) is a feed-forward, object-centric 3D reconstruction and inverse-rendering transformer that produces high-fidelity, relightable 3D assets from sparse posed multi-view images. It adapts native sparse attention to 3D reconstruction via a coarse-to-fine pipeline, 3D-aware spatial routing based on explicit geometric distance, and a custom sequence-parallel training strategy.

**What's new:** LSRM scales feed-forward reconstruction to 20× more object tokens and 2×+ more image tokens than prior SOTA, yielding >2.4 dB higher PSNR and >40% lower LPIPS, and extends cleanly into inverse rendering (relighting) rather than stopping at geometry+color.

**Why it matters to you:** Coming out of Meta Reality Labs (with AR/VR SLAM veteran Jakob Engel as a co-author), this is squarely aimed at turning sparse phone/headset captures into relightable, production-grade 3D digital twins — a direct input to XR asset pipelines and a strong candidate as an initialization/prior for downstream Gaussian splatting reconstructions.

## 4. FixAnything — CMU's Universal 3D-Rendering Artifact Fixer

**Source:** [arXiv 2608.23549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23549) · [GitHub](https://github.com/kvuong2711/fix-anything)
**Authors:** Khiem Vuong, Deva Ramanan, Srinivasa Narasimhan — Carnegie Mellon University
**Date:** ECCV 2026

**What it is:** FixAnything repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model, with light finetuning, into a single generalist artifact-repair model that cleans up rendering artifacts from *any* 3D representation — 3D Gaussian Splatting, NeRF, meshes, or sparse point clouds — that shows up under sparse input views or extrapolated target viewpoints.

**What's new:** Prior artifact-fixing work has been representation-specific (splat-only or NeRF-only). FixAnything is representation-agnostic, treating the artifact-repair problem as a video-consistency problem and letting a single video generative prior handle all of them.

**Why it matters to you:** Sparse-view artifacts (floaters, ghosting, blur at extrapolated angles) are one of the biggest practical blockers to deploying casual-capture 3DGS/NeRF scenes in production XR and mapping applications; a drop-in, representation-agnostic cleanup pass is directly usable as a post-processing stage regardless of which reconstruction pipeline you use upstream.

## 5. ReSplat — Recurrent Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting (ECCV 2026 Spotlight)

**Source:** [arXiv 2510.08575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08575) · [Project page](https://haofeixu.github.io/resplat/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/cvg/resplat)
**Authors:** Haofei Xu, Daniel Barath, Andreas Geiger, Marc Pollefeys — ETH Zürich / University of Tübingen
**Date:** ECCV 2026 Spotlight

**What it is:** ReSplat is a feed-forward Gaussian splatting model that predicts a compact initial set of Gaussians (16× fewer than prior per-pixel methods) in a subsampled space, then iteratively refines them with a weight-shared recurrent module that uses the rendering error itself — rather than backpropagated gradients — as the refinement signal.

**What's new:** Using render-and-compare error as a direct feedback signal (instead of gradient descent through the renderer) lets the model generalize at test time to view counts, resolutions, and datasets it wasn't trained on, while using far fewer Gaussians than dense per-pixel prediction approaches.

**Why it matters to you:** Fewer Gaussians per scene at equal quality directly reduces memory and bandwidth costs for streaming splats to XR devices, and the recurrent test-time adaptation is attractive for any pipeline that needs to handle variable-length, variable-resolution capture sessions (e.g., casual phone scanning) without retraining.

## 6. Compact Neural Appearance Models for Efficient Gaussian Splatting

**Source:** [arXiv 2609.05255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05255)
**Authors:** Florian Hahlbohm, Jorge Condor, Linus Franke, Martin Eisemann, Marcus Magnor — TU Braunschweig
**Date:** September 4, 2026

**What it is:** A systematic comparison of spherical-harmonics vs. newer spherical appearance models for 3DGS view-dependent color, plus a new implicit alternative that decodes compact per-primitive latent codes through a tiny shared MLP instead of storing per-Gaussian SH coefficients.

**What's new:** Spherical harmonics dominate per-primitive storage and memory bandwidth in standard 3DGS while still band-limiting angular detail. This paper's implicit latent-code + shared-MLP approach gives the best quality/memory/speed trade-off the authors tested, from the same group behind the Faster-GS optimization work covered in a previous digest.

**Why it matters to you:** Appearance storage is one of the largest remaining cost centers in 3DGS scene files; a drop-in replacement for spherical harmonics that cuts memory while improving angular detail is directly relevant to anyone shipping splats over the network or onto memory-constrained XR hardware.

## Emerging Trends to Watch

1. **World models as the new organizing framework for CV.** ECCV 2026's programming — LeCun's keynote, the dedicated "3D in the Era of World Models" workshop, and the density of feed-forward 3D/4D reconstruction papers — signals that 3D reconstruction and Gaussian splatting are increasingly framed as *infrastructure for world models*, not standalone research areas.

2. **Feed-forward reconstruction keeps eating optimization-based pipelines.** LSRM, ReSplat, and FixAnything all replace some part of a traditionally per-scene-optimized pipeline (sparse-view reconstruction, Gaussian fitting, artifact cleanup) with a single forward pass through a pretrained network — continuing the trend flagged in the June 2026 digest with D4RT, Flex4DHuman, and World Tracing.

3. **The appearance/storage frontier is opening up.** With training and rendering speed largely solved by 2026 (FastGS, Faster-GS), research attention (HKTex, Compact Neural Appearance Models) is shifting to *how* appearance is represented and stored — UV-free texturing and compact learned appearance codes — which matters most for streaming splats and meshes to bandwidth- and memory-constrained XR devices.

4. **Representation-agnostic tooling is emerging.** FixAnything treats 3DGS, NeRF, meshes, and point clouds uniformly via a shared video-generative prior — a sign that tooling built around one specific 3D representation may increasingly be supplanted by representation-agnostic, video/diffusion-prior-based post-processing.

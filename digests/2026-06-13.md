## 1. Mobile-GS — Real-Time Gaussian Splatting at 116 FPS on Mobile

**Source:** [arXiv 2603.11531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11531) · [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=vRegY0pgvQ) · [Project page](https://xiaobiaodu.github.io/mobile-gs-project/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/xiaobiaodu/mobile-gs)  
**Authors:** Researchers from University of Technology Sydney, Adelaide University, and Li Auto Inc.  
**Venue:** ICLR 2026

**What it is:** Mobile-GS is the first method to achieve real-time 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering at full resolution on mobile GPU hardware. It renders at **116 FPS at 1600×1063** on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, and achieves **1,125 FPS** at standard benchmark resolution with a **4.6 MB** model footprint — compared to vanilla 3DGS's 174 FPS at 839.9 MB on a desktop GPU.

**What's new:** The core insight is that depth-sorting — required in all prior 3DGS renderers for correct alpha compositing — is the primary mobile bottleneck, not rasterisation itself. Mobile-GS eliminates sorting entirely with a depth-aware order-independent rendering (OIR) scheme, then compensates for compositing artifacts using a lightweight neural view-dependent enhancement network. The compression pipeline (structured pruning + entropy coding) gets scene size below 5 MB.

**Why it matters:** This is the enabling technology for deploying Gaussian Splatting in smartphone AR apps, XR headsets without discrete GPUs (Quest, Android XR glasses), and automotive/robotics displays. The 4.6 MB model size also makes streaming 3DGS scenes over cellular networks feasible. Code is public on GitHub.

## 2. GETA-3DGS — Automatic Joint Structured Pruning and Quantization for 3DGS

**Source:** [arXiv 2605.02086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02086) · [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02086)  
**Authors:** Not yet fully listed in public index  
**Date:** May 3, 2026 (arXiv preprint)

**What it is:** GETA-3DGS is described as the first end-to-end *automatic* joint structured pruning and quantization framework specifically designed for 3D Gaussian Splatting. Prior compression methods treat pruning, quantization, and entropy coding as separate hand-tuned stages that cannot generalise across scenes. GETA-3DGS introduces a **3DGS-aware quantization-aware dependency graph** that treats each Gaussian as a group with five attribute sub-nodes, a **render-aware saliency** metric fusing transmittance-weighted contribution and pixel coverage, and **heterogeneous per-attribute mixed-precision quantization** that assigns bit-width per attribute type rather than globally.

**What's new:** Existing approaches (PUP 3D-GS, LightGaussian, Compact3D) require separate tuning for each scene and bitrate target. GETA-3DGS takes a user-specified rate–quality target and automatically optimises the pruning/quantization schedule end-to-end via a differentiable rate–distortion objective. This removes the human-in-the-loop compression tuning step.

**Why it matters:** High-fidelity 3DGS scenes currently occupy hundreds of MB to several GB — far outside the budget of mobile, XR streaming, and volumetric video platforms. GETA-3DGS directly addresses the production deployment gap and could enable streaming 3DGS scenes at quality levels previously only possible offline.

## 3. Beyond Heuristics — Learnable Density Control for 3DGS (ICML 2026)

**Source:** [Awesome3DGS Accepted list](https://github.com/Awesome3DGS/3D-Gaussian-Splatting-Papers/blob/main/2026/Accepted.md)  
**Authors:** Zhenhua Ning, Xin Li, Jun Yu et al.  
**Venue:** ICML 2026

**What it is:** This paper replaces the heuristic Adaptive Density Control (ADC) mechanism in the original 3DGS paper with a fully learned density controller. ADC uses hand-crafted gradient thresholds and clone/split/prune rules that generalize poorly to indoor vs. outdoor scenes and different capture densities. The learned controller jointly optimises density management and scene quality end-to-end.

**What's new:** ADC has been one of the last remaining hand-engineered components in the 3DGS pipeline; virtually all acceleration and compression papers since 2023 have worked around it rather than replacing it. This is the first ICML-accepted paper to tackle ADC replacement directly with a learned approach, which suggests it generalises well enough to pass rigorous peer review.

**Why it matters:** Eliminating heuristic density control is a prerequisite for fully differentiable 3DGS pipelines that can be fine-tuned end-to-end (e.g., for novel-view synthesis with adversarial or perceptual losses). It is particularly relevant for feed-forward 3DGS methods where ADC is completely inapplicable.

## 4. SplAttN — Gaussian Soft Splatting + Attention for Point Cloud Completion (ICML 2026 Spotlight)

**Source:** [Awesome3DGS Accepted list](https://github.com/Awesome3DGS/3D-Gaussian-Splatting-Papers/blob/main/2026/Accepted.md)  
**Authors:** Zhaoyang Li, Zhichao You, Tianrui Li — Southwest Jiaotong University  
**Venue:** ICML 2026 Spotlight

**What it is:** SplAttN bridges 2D and 3D representations for point cloud completion by using Gaussian *soft splatting* to project partial 3D point clouds into multi-view feature maps, then applying attention mechanisms across views to infer missing geometry. The Gaussian soft splatting creates differentiable, smooth 2D projections that preserve spatial structure better than hard rasterisation for this task.

**What's new:** Point cloud completion has historically operated either purely in 3D (with costly volumetric or graph-network methods) or used hard projection to 2D images (losing depth information). SplAttN's Gaussian soft splatting creates a soft, spatially-continuous 2D projection that retains implicit depth encoding — a new bridge between the two paradigms. The ICML Spotlight designation (top ~5% of accepted papers) confirms strong empirical results.

**Why it matters:** For 3D reconstruction and XR, point cloud completion is critical whenever sensor data is sparse or occluded (LiDAR, depth cameras, sparse-view MVS). SplAttN's use of Gaussian representations for this task also suggests a unifying role for 3DGS-style primitives beyond scene rendering.

## 5. TelePIX & SatUnreal — Satellite Stereo 3D Reconstruction, CVPR 2026 Runner-Up Best Paper

**Source:** [Seoul Economic Daily (English)](https://en.sedaily.com/news/2026/06/10/telepix-wins-runner-up-best-paper-award-at-cvpr-2026-for) · [CVPR Highlights](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/events/Highlights2026)  
**Authors:** Han-Gyeol Kim, JaeWan Park, Junmin Park, Darongsae Kwon — TelePIX (Seoul, Korea)  
**Date:** Awarded June 5–7, 2026 at CVPR; paper date June 10, 2026 (news confirmed)

**What it is:** TelePIX, a Korean space-AI startup, won the CVPR 2026 Runner-Up Best Paper Award for research on 3D reconstruction from satellite stereo imagery in extreme observation geometries (oblique angles, harsh lighting). Their CVPR submission also includes **SatUnreal** — a high-precision synthetic dataset for satellite stereo matching built with Unreal Engine, offering photorealistic rendered satellite imagery with ground-truth depth and disparity.

**What's new:** Standard AI-based 3D reconstruction degrades significantly under the low incidence angles, atmospheric distortions, and sparse texture conditions common in satellite imagery. TelePIX's method specifically addresses these failure modes and the SatUnreal dataset provides an evaluation benchmark that was previously unavailable — synthetic satellite images with true metric depth, useful for pre-training and transfer to real sensor data.

**Why it matters:** Satellite 3D reconstruction is critical for geographic mapping, disaster response, and infrastructure monitoring. SatUnreal also sets a precedent for synthetic-to-real transfer benchmarks that could be replicated for other challenging sensor domains (underwater, low-light, medical).

## 6. RealityKit Gaussian Splat API — visionOS 27 Developer Docs Now Live (UPDATED)

**Source:** [Apple Developer Docs: Gaussian splats on visionOS](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/visionos/gaussian-splats-on-visionos) · [WWDC26: Explore advances in RealityKit](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/279/) · [Frame Sixty](https://framesixty.com/whats-new-in-visionos-27/)  
**Date:** Developer beta June 8, 2026; docs live as of this week

**Update on yesterday's visionOS 27 coverage:** The Apple Developer documentation for Gaussian Splats on visionOS is now live and accessible. Developers can:

- Import `.splat` scene files directly into Reality Composer Pro 3
- Place splat entities in a `RealityView` using the new `GaussianSplatComponent`
- Combine splats with RealityKit physics, cloth simulation, and the new reverb mesh for mixed real/virtual scenes
- Use Projective Textures and Physical Space Lighting alongside splat content for coherent illumination

**What's new today:** The specific API surface (`GaussianSplatComponent`, `SplatEntity`) and the Reality Composer Pro 3 import workflow are now documented — yesterday's digest noted the announcement; today the developer toolchain is confirmed and callable. The cloth simulation + splat combination is particularly novel: it allows a scanned real object (splat) to interact with simulated fabric in the same RealityKit scene.

**Why it matters:** This is the first first-party XR platform SDK to ship a native Gaussian Splat rendering component. Third-party developers can now ship apps with photorealistic 3DGS content on Apple Vision Pro without writing a custom renderer — a major adoption accelerant.

## 9. QuadBox — Geometry-Aware Acceleration for 3D Gaussian Splatting (ICIP 2026)

**Source:** [Awesome3DGS Accepted list](https://github.com/Awesome3DGS/3D-Gaussian-Splatting-Papers/blob/main/2026/Accepted.md)  
**Authors:** Xinze Li, Bowen Yang, Pengxu Chen et al. — Beijing Normal-HK Baptist University, Hong Kong Baptist University, Jilin University, Xiaomi  
**Venue:** ICIP 2026

**What it is:** QuadBox replaces the axis-aligned bounding boxes used in standard 3DGS rasterisation with geometry-informed spatial structures derived from the local scene geometry. This reduces per-tile overdraw and improves GPU cache locality during tile-based rendering.

**What's new:** Standard 3DGS acceleration work (FastGS, Faster-GS, 3DGS-Accel) focuses on reducing the *number* of Gaussians or improving training throughput. QuadBox improves *rendering throughput* through better spatial data structures without reducing Gaussian count or quality. The Xiaomi co-authorship suggests industrial relevance for mobile GPU rendering (complementing Mobile-GS's approach).

**Why it matters:** Rendering throughput rather than training speed is the bottleneck in interactive applications (AR overlays, real-time XR). QuadBox's geometry-aware bounding structures are also likely to compose well with Mobile-GS's order-independent rendering and GETA-3DGS's compression.

## 10. PropSplat — RF Field Reconstruction via 3DGS Propagation (DySPAN 2026)

**Source:** [Awesome3DGS Accepted list](https://github.com/Awesome3DGS/3D-Gaussian-Splatting-Papers/blob/main/2026/Accepted.md)  
**Authors:** William Bjorndahl, Maninder Pal Singh, Farhad Nouri, Joseph Camp — SMU, University of Houston  
**Venue:** DySPAN 2026

**What it is:** PropSplat applies the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation to **radio frequency (RF) field reconstruction** — representing electromagnetic signal strength and propagation patterns in 3D space without requiring a pre-built map of the environment. The Gaussian primitives model signal attenuation, reflection, and diffraction through the scene.

**What's new:** Gaussian Splatting has been applied to light fields, thermal imaging, and acoustic fields, but this appears to be one of the first map-free RF field reconstruction methods using 3DGS. The map-free aspect (propagation splatting, not pre-registered geometry) is the key novelty.

**Why it matters:** For XR and spatial computing, RF reconstruction has direct applications in indoor localisation, AR signal coverage mapping, and network-aware rendering (knowing where streaming bandwidth is available). It also demonstrates that 3DGS is generalising as a representation beyond optical radiance — a conceptually important expansion of the paradigm.

## Emerging Trends — June 13 Update

**1. 3DGS compression is converging toward end-to-end learnable pipelines.** GETA-3DGS (joint pruning + quantization), Mobile-GS (sorted-free + neural enhancement + compression), and Beyond Heuristics (learnable density control) collectively mark a shift from hand-tuned heuristics to differentiable, jointly optimised pipelines. Expect production-ready scene sizes under 10 MB within 6–12 months.

**2. Gaussian Splatting is generalising beyond optical radiance.** PropSplat (RF fields), SplAttN (point cloud completion), and QuadBox (GPU data structures) all apply Gaussian primitives to problems well outside the original radiance field framing. The representation's differentiability and spatial expressiveness are driving adoption as a general 3D primitive.

**3. The XR hardware landscape is bifurcating.** Apple Vision Pro (high-fidelity, tethered compute via Foveated Streaming) vs. Android XR glasses (lightweight, AI-driven, phone-tethered) represent fundamentally different compute/display tradeoffs. Building XR 3D content pipelines that target both requires hardware-agnostic streaming and LOD strategies.

**4. AWE 2026 (June 15–18) will be the bellwether for H2 2026 XR.** All major platform announcements (WWDC, Google I/O, CVPR) have now landed; AWE is where the industry synthesises them into product roadmaps. Watch for Meta Ray-Ban Display platform details and Android XR developer APIs to crystallise.

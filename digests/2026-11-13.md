## 2. Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting for concrete defect localization

**Source:** [Journal of Civil Structural Health Monitoring (Springer), DOI 10.1007/s13349-026-01171-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13349-026-01171-5)
**Date:** Published **Nov 13, 2026**

**What it is:** A 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) pipeline that attaches semantic labels to the surfels. It reconstructs concrete structures and localizes defects such as cracks and spalling in 3D, consistently across many views.

**What's new:** Earlier inspection pipelines detected defects in individual 2D images and then projected them onto a mesh or point cloud. Here the semantic field is optimized together with 2DGS geometry, so detections agree across views and sit on accurate surfaces.

**Relevance:** A clear applied example of *semantic* 2DGS, where you need surface accuracy as well as good-looking renders. The same approach (a semantic channel on surfels, with multi-view consistency) carries over to digital twins, AR overlays for inspection, and any task that needs labels placed on reconstructed surfaces.

## 3. Plant digital twins from ordinary cameras (University of Tartu)

**Source:** [Pensoft blog](https://blog.pensoft.net/2026/11/12/digital-twins-for-plants-how-ordinary-cameras-are-helping-preserve-living-collections/) · [Bioengineer.org](https://bioengineer.org/consumer-cameras-and-gaussian-splatting-create-explorable-plant-digital-twins/)
**Date:** **Nov 12, 2026**

**What it is:** Researchers combined photogrammetry and Gaussian splatting, using consumer cameras, to build explorable 3D digital twins of living plants in botanical collections.

**What's new:** Plants are hard to reconstruct: thin structures, self-occlusion, and leaves that move. This work shows that a low-cost capture rig is good enough for archival-quality splats, and it sets out a practical workflow for heritage collections and biodiversity records.

**Relevance:** Good evidence on how far casual capture can go with thin, high-frequency geometry, which also stresses densification and anti-aliasing. It is also useful as a capture-protocol reference if you build datasets or XR experiences of natural scenes.

## 4. Camera3R — feed-forward reconstruction across camera models

**Source:** [arXiv 2611.13453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13453)
**Date:** Submitted **Nov 10, 2026** (just outside the 24-hour window, included because it matters to this research area)

**What it is:** A way to extend existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (the DUSt3R/VGGT family) to non-perspective cameras such as fisheye and omnidirectional lenses. The authors report it adds only about **0.5% more parameters**.

**What's new:** Most feed-forward reconstruction models assume pinhole cameras, so images from wide-angle, fisheye, or 360° cameras have to be undistorted first, which loses field of view. Camera3R handles mixed camera models directly with a lightweight adapter instead of retraining the model.

**Relevance:** Very relevant if you capture with XR headset cameras (which are usually fisheye), action cameras, or 360° rigs. It could also give faster initialization for Gaussian splatting from mixed camera rigs.

*Also on arXiv this week:* georeferenced cross-view reconstruction (NeurIPS 2026, recovers geographic coordinates for ground-image reconstructions) and **VINO** ([arXiv 2611.07176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07176)), on 3D representation learning with volume transformers. See the [cs.CV new listing](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.CV/new).

## 5. Dark-2DGS — relightable 3D scenes from flash photos in the dark

**Source:** [Frontiers of Computer Science, DOI 10.1007/s11704-026-51767-9](https://journal.hep.com.cn/fcs/EN/10.1007/s11704-026-51767-9) · [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1151096) · [Bioengineer.org](https://bioengineer.org/researchers-reconstruct-relightable-3d-scenes-from-dark-flash-images/)
**Authors:** Xuening Zhu, Renjiao Yi, Chenyang Zhu, et al.
**Date:** Press coverage this week (approx.)

**What it is:** A method that reconstructs relightable scenes from multi-view photos taken with only a camera flash, in otherwise completely dark scenes. It starts from 2DGS geometry, groups the images into photometric-stereo subproblems, and uses the resulting normal and material estimates as priors when optimizing.

**What's new:** Earlier low-light splatting work deals with *dim* scenes. Here the moving flash is the *only* light, and the method uses that as a signal, much like photometric stereo. The authors report better decomposition, geometry, and relighting than prior methods.

**Relevance:** Relevant to inverse rendering and material capture with Gaussian primitives, and to capturing with phones or robots in dark spaces. A related line of work for comparison: DarkGS, SpotlessGS, and SunFaded (CVPR 2026).

## 6. Spaghetti Splatting — from points to curves

**Source:** [Radiance Fields paper entry](https://radiancefields.com/papers/spaghetti-splatting-differentiating-curvilinear-dirac-masses-via-convolution) · [Radiance Fields article](https://radiancefields.com/spaghetti-splatting-swaps-gaussian-points-for-curves)
**Date:** Recent (approx., covered this week)

**What it is:** A generalization of splatting from point primitives to curves. Curves are approximated as polylines, and each segment is volume-rendered so that segments that meet at a joint are not counted twice. As segments get shorter, the result converges to the continuous curve.

**What's new:** The paper's framing is that EWA splatting, Poisson reconstruction, and 3DGS all convolve point Dirac masses with a kernel. Swapping points for curves gives a **strict superset of 3DGS**, since a point is a zero-length curve. It reportedly matches 3DGS on general scenes.

**Relevance:** A natural fit for hair, fur, wires, vegetation, and thin structures, where 3DGS needs huge numbers of elongated Gaussians. It is also a promising primitive for vector-like 3D modeling and CAD-style edge reconstruction. Compare it with CurveGaussian ([arXiv 2506.21401](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21401v1)).

## 7. Gaussian splats keep moving into production tools

**Sources:** [UEFN 43.00 release notes (Epic)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/43-00-fortnite-ecosystem-updates-and-release-notes) · [Radiance Fields on UEFN](https://radiancefields.com/uefn-43-00-adds-experimental-gaussian-splat-import) · [Radiance Fields news hub](https://radiancefields.com/) · [Splatmania](https://radiancefields.com/design4real-releases-splatmania-a-free-splat-editor)
**Date:** Early–mid November 2026

- **UEFN 43.00 (Epic):** experimental PLY/SPZ Gaussian splat import into Unreal Editor for Fortnite. Splats render on every supported platform, *including mobile*, and can be driven by Niagara effects. Unreal Engine 5.9 lists experimental splat import on its roadmap.
- **Evergine (Nov release):** its Gaussian splatting add-on now runs on GPU compute shaders and supports more file formats.
- **ArcGIS Maps SDK for JS 5.2:** Viewshed and Shadow Cast analysis work on Gaussian splat layers. Esri warns that splat artifacts can affect the results, which is a sign of how geospatial users are starting to treat splats as data for analysis.
- **Splatmania (design4real):** a free browser-based splat editor built on PlayCanvas's SuperSplat. **Frankensplat** trains splats locally in the browser with WebGPU.

**Why it matters:** SPZ is becoming the common exchange format (World Labs, UEFN, Blender 5.3 since September). Splats can now be published to a mass-market, mobile-capable runtime. If you build splat pipelines, exporting to SPZ is now worth doing.
